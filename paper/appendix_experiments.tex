\subsection{Controlled label-ambiguity sensitivity analyses}
\label{app:noisy-alignment-sensitivity}

The analytic ambiguity floor is
\begin{equation}
\delta_{\rm noise}(r_{\rm n})=\frac{a^2}{2\tau^2}
\left[1-(1-r_{\rm n})^2-\frac{r_{\rm n}^2}{K-1}\right],
\label{eq:synthetic-noise-floor}
\end{equation}
which follows from Proposition~\ref{prop:pgib-ambiguity-decomposition} with the
explicit comparator of Section~\ref{sec:controlled-validation}: its posterior mean
$h_0(x)=a\sum_k\eta_k(x)q_k$ annihilates the bias term, its covariance $\tau^2I$
annihilates $V_0$, and $\eta_k(x)$ equals $1-r_{\rm n}$ on the true class and $r_{\rm n}/(K-1)$
otherwise, so $1-\|\eta\|_2^2=1-(1-r_{\rm n})^2-r_{\rm n}^2/(K-1)$. It grows from $0$ to $11.2$
nats as $r_{\rm n}$ runs over the noise grid.

Training evaluates the population objective by weighting all $K^2$
clean/observed-label pairs by the noise model, with a descending-$\beta$ warm start
to remove avoidable optimization error at the small-$\beta$ end; the comparator risk
$C_{\rm comp}(r_{\rm n})$ is estimated by Monte Carlo. The population-weighted experiment of
Section~\ref{sec:controlled-validation} uses the standard trainable
posterior variance. Figure~\ref{fig:noisy-alignment-sensitivity} changes these
choices one at a time. Fixing the posterior variance to
$\sigma^2\equiv\tau^2$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:noisy-alignment-sensitivity}a) leaves the
measured mismatch on the analytic floors, confirming that the result is not an
artifact of posterior-covariance flexibility.

The second panel (Fig.~\ref{fig:noisy-alignment-sensitivity}b) replaces the exact
population weights by sampled noisy labels. It
shows the expected finite-sample displacement from the population floor at weak
compression and convergence toward the empirical floor as compression strengthens.
Five sampled-label configurations at large $\beta$ fail the explicit comparator-objective
gate; they are marked as optimization failures rather than interpreted as violations
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pgib-comparator-bound}. The population-weighted main grid and the
structural control satisfy the gate at every point.

\subsection{Alignment and separation: definitions and diagnostics}
\label{app:direct-mismatch}

This section supplements Section~\ref{sec:direct-mismatch}, which checks how
much of the declared anchor geometry the learned posterior inherits and whether
the separation guarantee survives under the measured mismatch. It adds the
decoupling scatter, the slack reading, and the regression diagnostics. On the
ImageNet-100 (MLP) test split we compute, per anchor scale $a\in\{1,6,12\}$ and
across the $\beta$ grid, the slack $S-\mathrm{LB}$ between the realized
class-center separation and the sample-level lower bound of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:empirical-class-separation-bound} ($\tau^2=1$).

Panels (a) and (b) of Fig.~\ref{fig:direct-mismatch-deferred} read these
quantities. In the decoupling scatter (a), the points form a flat band at
$82$--$86\%$ accuracy while spreading across the full log-$\widehat D$ axis,
except that the $a=1$ points under strong compression fall far below the band:
only that regime loses substantial task information. The slack between
the bound and the realized separation (both defined in
Section~\ref{sec:direct-mismatch}) shrinks with compression for all three
scales and stays non-negative at every reported point (b): the bound is
consistent but conservative, and tightens exactly where compression is
strongest.

\paragraph{Regression.} We partition the normalized test labels into $20$
equal-width bins and merge any bin containing fewer than $30$ examples. Let
$\bar y_b$ be the mean label of bin $B_b$, $\widehat{\bar\mu}_b$ its posterior center,
$\widehat D_b$ its average sample-to-own-anchor mismatch, and
$h_b:=\operatorname{diam}(B_b)$. The finite-bin lower bound is
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathrm{LB}}_{bc}
=\rho|\bar y_b-\bar y_c|
-\sqrt{2\tau^2\widehat D_b}
-\sqrt{2\tau^2\widehat D_c}
-\rho(h_b+h_c),
\label{eq:empirical-regression-separation-bound}
\end{equation}
where the last term is the quantization correction of
Remark~\ref{rem:pgib-binning}. Panel (c) of Fig.~\ref{fig:direct-mismatch-deferred}
shows the Housing mismatch--$\beta$ curves by isometric scale. Only the $\rho=1$
curve descends visibly with $\beta$, converging onto the zero line from a large
weak-compression value, while $\rho=6,12$ stay close to zero throughout with only
a small weak-compression deviation: the smallest scale cannot hold the posterior
on its axis without compression. Panel (d) of
Fig.~\ref{fig:direct-mismatch-deferred}
plots the actual bin-center distances against the declared isometric distances at
$\rho=6$: the cloud hugs a line below the identity line at
all four $\beta$ values, so the posterior follows the declared metric structure at
a fixed fraction of its scale, and the scale-transfer ratio is essentially
$\beta$-invariant. After the quantization correction, a positive bound survives
for a substantial share of bin pairs.

\subsection{Adversarial robustness details}
\label{app:robustness}
This appendix supplements Section~\ref{sec:robustness}, which measures targeted
white-box PGD success on MNIST, with the transfer check that rules out gradient
masking. Targeted PGD uses 20 steps, EOT-10 gradients, 10-sample MC predictions,
and budgets $\varepsilon_\infty=0.3$ and $\varepsilon_2=6$. For the transfer
check, adversaries generated on one model are evaluated on another (CEB to OPB
and back); their transferred success (Fig.~\ref{fig:adv-transfer}) stays far
below the white-box level of Fig.~\ref{fig:robustness-main} at every budget, so
the white-box gains are not an artifact of masked gradients, though the check
excludes masking rather than establishing a causal geometry effect.

\subsection{Factor-wise ablations}
\label{app:ablation}
The factor-wise ablation ladder switches geometry, anchor scale, readout, and the IB
term separately; each variant is evaluated at its best validation-selected
configuration (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}). The complete means are reported in
Table~\ref{tab:ablation}. The paired bootstrap intervals are in the main text
(Table~\ref{tab:ablation-ci}) for the classification contrasts and in
Table~\ref{tab:ablation-ci-epb} for the regression mirrors; all paired differences
use the shared seeds at each variant's best validation-selected configuration,
and the intervals are $95\%$ bootstrap percentile CIs with $B=10{,}000$ and a fixed
seed. The factor ranking and
the sign-flip and negative-result patterns are read in the main text
(Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).

\emph{NCM-learn} removes the bottleneck entirely: the encoder output is used
deterministically ($z=\mu_\phi(x)$, no posterior and no KL) and classified by an
energy readout on trainable prototypes. \emph{NCM-ortho} is the same, except that
the prototypes are fixed to the orthogonal frame $a\,e_k$ (fixed geometry, still no
IB). \emph{CEB-energy} and \emph{CEB-tied} keep CEB's trainable free reference and
KL but replace its free head by the energy readout (resp.\ the tied projection).
\emph{OPB-FS} and \emph{EPB-FS} keep the orthogonal frame and the energy (resp.\
tied) readout but make the anchor radius learnable, so only the fixed scale is
switched off. \emph{GPB-L} is GPB's free-head counterpart on the classification
settings (identical orthogonal-frame prior, free linear head), and \emph{EPB-L}
and \emph{EPB} are the regression counterparts of GPB-L and GPB on Housing,
keeping the isometric-axis prior with a free head (resp.\ the tied projection).
\emph{OPB-RV} freezes all trainable prior parameters on the classification
settings, and \emph{EPB-RV} freezes all trainable prior parameters on the
regression settings.

\subsection{Information-plane estimates}
\label{app:info-plane}
\textbf{Estimator protocol.}\ $I(X;Z)$ is estimated by the InfoNCE lower bound
\citep{oord2018representation} ($\log N_{\rm neg}-\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{NCE}}$, $N_{\rm neg}=4096$; one
critic per run, trained on the training split, the bound read off the test split with
$z\sim q(z|x)$). $I(Y;Z)$ is read on the same sampled posterior: $H(Y)-\mathrm{CE}$
with the cross-entropy averaged over $M=20$ draws $z\sim q(z|x)$ per test point
(still a lower bound, as $\mathrm{CE}\ge H(Y|Z)$). The residual $I(X;Z|Y)$ is
read on the known Gaussian posteriors: the class-conditional mixture
$q(z|y)=\frac{1}{N_y}\sum_{j:y_j=y}q(z|x_j)$ over the test samples is evaluated
exactly by logsumexp, and
$I(X;Z|Y)=\mathbb{E}_{x,y}\,\mathrm{KL}(q(z|x)\,\|\,q(z|y))$ is read by Monte
Carlo, exact up to $z$-sampling noise and the finite per-class test sample. The
certificate-plane axis is drawn linear because the collapsed CEB trajectory lands
on zero.

\textbf{Matched-compression performance.}
Table~\ref{tab:info-plane-matched} pairs each CEB point with the OPB configuration
of nearest estimated $I(X;Z)$: at equal compression OPB retains no less accuracy
and its residual is comparable, so OPB's task plateau is not bought by extra
residual compression. These are paired empirical comparisons at two estimated
compression levels, not a causal attribution to geometry.

\textbf{Regression companion.}\ Figure~\ref{fig:info-plane-extra} verifies on the
regression setting what the classification panels of Section~\ref{sec:info-plane}
establish, namely what each method compresses and discards as compression
deepens; here
$I(Y;Z)$ is the Gaussian approximation
$\tfrac12 \ln\bigl(\mathrm{Var}(y)/\mathrm{Var}(y-\hat y_{\mathrm{MC}})\bigr)$
read on the sampled posterior, and the residual $I(X;Z|Y)$ uses a
Gaussian-kernel-weighted mixture of the test posteriors
($\hat q(z|y)=\sum_j w_j(y)\,q(z|x_j)$, with the bandwidth fitted on the
training split), evaluated exactly by logsumexp; every term is a KL between
proper densities, so the estimate is non-negative by construction. The
two panels of Fig.~\ref{fig:info-plane-extra} show the same phenomenon:
CEB's residual and task information fall toward zero together, whereas EPB ends
strong compression on a small positive residual plateau whose task-information
level grows with the scale; every EPB curve keeps its $R^2$ flat out to the
strongest compression, so compression again stops where the task information
sits.
