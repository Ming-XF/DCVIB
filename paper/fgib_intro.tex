% ---------------- 1. Introduction ----------------

The information bottleneck (IB) principle \cite{tishby2000information} casts
representation learning as a constrained optimization: find a representation $T$ of the
input $X$ that retains the information needed to predict $Y$ while discarding the rest,
$\min I(X;T)$ subject to $I(Y;T) \ge r$. Its Lagrangian relaxation
$L^{\mathrm{IB}}_\beta = I(X;T) - \beta I(Y;T)$, and the deep variational instantiation
(VIB) \cite{alemi2017deep} trains a stochastic encoder $q(z|x)$ with a KL penalty against
a fixed marginal prior, promising a single hyper-parameter $\beta$ that sweeps the
compression--accuracy frontier; compressed representations are in turn expected to
generalize and to be robust to distribution shift.

Most classification data, however, lives in a regime where this picture breaks down: the
label is (nearly) a deterministic function of the input, $Y = f(X)$. Kolchinsky \& Tracey
\cite{kolchinsky2019caveats} showed that in this deterministic scenario the IB curve
degenerates to the piecewise-linear shape $F(r) = \min\{r, H(Y)\}$, and the Lagrangian
cannot explore it: for every $\beta \in (0,1)$ the maximizer is pinned at the single copy
corner $\langle H(Y), H(Y)\rangle$, at $\beta = 1$ the entire diagonal is simultaneously
optimal, and trivial constant solutions take over for $\beta > 1$. Their fix --- squaring
the compression term --- and the convex IB Lagrangian
\cite{rodriguez2020convex}, which provides an exact mapping between the multiplier and the
compression level, repair the sweep at the level of the exact functional.

Fischer's Conditional Entropy Bottleneck (CEB) \cite{fischer2020conditional} re-derives
the objective from the Minimum Necessary Information (MNI) criterion and replaces the
compression term with the class-conditional residual $I(X;Z|Y)$, which vanishes exactly at
the MNI point; CEB reports better accuracy, adversarial robustness, and out-of-distribution
detection than VIB, and the conditional residual is precisely the quantity that provably
controls generalization \cite{kawaguchi2023how}. Geiger \& Fischer
\cite{geiger2020comparison} established the algebraic identity
$L_{\mathrm{CEB}}(\gamma) = I(X;Z) - (1+\gamma)I(Y;Z) = L^{\mathrm{IB}}_{1+\gamma}$ and
showed that CEB's empirical gains cannot be attributed to tighter variational bounds.
What has gone unnoticed is the consequence of this identity for the deterministic
scenario: since CEB corresponds to IB with $\beta_{\mathrm{IB}} = 1 + \gamma > 1$, the
\emph{entire} parameter range of CEB lies inside the degenerate half-plane of the analysis
of \cite{kolchinsky2019caveats}. We show (Prop.~\ref{prop:dead}) that for every
$\gamma > 0$ the CEB Lagrangian has the same minimizer --- the copy corner --- so $\gamma$
is a dead knob; and we identify three further failure channels specific to the
variational implementation.

The first channel is \emph{flatness} (Prop.~\ref{prop:flat}): on the noisy-label-copy
manifold $T_\alpha = B_\alpha \cdot Y$, the variational CEB objective equals
$\gamma\,(H(Y) - I(Y;T_\alpha))$. A code that drops $\delta$ nats of label information
pays exactly $\gamma\delta = \delta/\beta$ (in the loss convention
$\mathrm{CE} + \beta\,\mathrm{KL}$ with $\beta = 1/\gamma$); in the heavy-regularization
regime $\beta \to \infty$, solutions retaining all, part, or none of the label information
become indistinguishable to the objective, and the residual certificate $\mathrm{ReX}$ is
identically zero on the entire manifold --- it is blind to label retention. The second
channel is \emph{collusion} (Prop.~\ref{prop:variance}): the backward encoder $b(z|y)$ is
trainable and can absorb the certificate gap by moment matching alone --- tightness of the
bound certifies nothing about compression --- and the variance race between the encoder
and the backward encoder (the classifier presses $\sigma \to 0$ by Jensen, while the KL is
indifferent along $\sigma^2 = \tau^2$) collapses the code toward a deterministic copy of
the label targets; at the standard logvar clamp the objective degenerates into a
mean-matching force of weight $e^{10}$ between two trainable maps, and the certificate
reports $\approx 0$ while the true residual diverges in the noise-free limit. Third, the
certificate bounds the wrong object (Prop.~\ref{prop:object}(a)): the classifier is
trained on the sampled code and evaluated on its mean, so the bound's object
$I(X;Z|Y)$ lies strictly below the residual $I(X;\mu(X)|Y)$ of the representation used at
inference, by an unobservable, model-driven deficit. The collusion channel is the
conditional-bottleneck counterpart of posterior collapse in (conditional) variational
autoencoders \cite{dai2020usual,dang2024beyond}.

We propose the \emph{Fixed-Geometry Information Bottleneck} (FGIB), which closes all four
channels with a single structural change: replace the learnable label-conditioned prior
with a \emph{fixed} orthonormal anchor geometry, and decouple prediction from the
bottleneck noise. FGIB keeps a deterministic main path --- the classifier consumes the
encoder output $h$ at both training and evaluation, so no stochasticity enters the
inference path --- and adds a side branch $z \sim q(z|x)$ whose KL is measured against a
fixed anchor prior $r(z|y) = \mathcal{N}(a\,v_y, I)$: the $v_y$ are orthonormal directions
frozen as non-trainable buffers, and the anchor scale $a$ sets the geometry. The loss is
$\mathrm{CE} + \beta\,\mathrm{KL}$. Three properties follow from the fixed geometry.
First, the certificate cannot be gamed: the gap decomposition (Lemma~\ref{lem:gap}) shows
that the bound's slack can shrink only if the encoder moves toward the anchors, so
tightness is monotonically equivalent to compression (Prop.~\ref{prop:ordering}).
Second, the deterministic main path decouples the posterior variance from prediction, so
$\sigma^{2*} = 1$ for every $\beta$, and no collapse channel exists. Third, the anchor
geometry excludes the trivial corner --- the collapsed code $\mu \equiv 0$ pays a fixed
price $\tfrac12 a^2$ for every class --- and on the class-aligned equilibrium family the
Lagrangian is strictly convex with a unique interior minimizer per $\beta$
(Props.~\ref{prop:sweep} and \ref{prop:curve}): $\beta$ sweeps the trade-off curve
one-to-one, exactly the property the IB and CEB Lagrangians lack. Moreover, FGIB is not a
new information-theoretic objective: its side path is pointwise identical to a CEB
bottleneck applied to the inference representation (Prop.~\ref{prop:fgib-ceb}), so FGIB
retains CEB's object --- the residual of the representation actually used at inference, up
to known side-path noise (Prop.~\ref{prop:inverse}) --- while replacing its gameable prior
with a fixed geometry. For regression tasks, fixed random-Fourier anchors provide the same
mechanism for continuous labels.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Theory of failure.} We prove that CEB inherits the deterministic degeneration
of \cite{kolchinsky2019caveats} over its entire parameter range (the $\gamma$-knob is
dead), and we identify three additional variational failure channels: the flatness of the
label deficit (dropping label information costs $\delta/\beta$), the gameable certificate
with its variance-collapse endpoint, and the certificate-object mismatch
(Props.~\ref{prop:dead}, \ref{prop:flat}, \ref{prop:variance}, \ref{prop:object}).
\item \emph{Method.} FGIB: a fixed orthonormal anchor prior plus a deterministic main
path, for classification and regression (fixed random-Fourier anchors), requiring no
sampling and no trainable prior.
\item \emph{Theory of the method.} A sweep theorem (unique interior minimizer per
$\beta$; strictly concave curve), a certificate-honesty theorem (non-gameable gap;
the bound's object is the inference representation), and an equivalence theorem (the side
path is a CEB bottleneck on the main path)
(Props.~\ref{prop:sweep}, \ref{prop:curve}, \ref{prop:ordering}, \ref{prop:inverse},
\ref{prop:object}, \ref{prop:fgib-ceb}).
\item \emph{Experiments.} Nine tasks across MLP/CNN/GCN/RNN backbones: $\beta$-sweeps
confirm the predicted smooth frontier for FGIB and the pinned/degenerate behavior of
VIB and CEB; in the heavy-regularization regime CEB loses label information while
reporting $\mathrm{ReX} \approx 0$, and FGIB retains it; regression tasks extend the
mechanism to continuous anchors.
\end{itemize}
