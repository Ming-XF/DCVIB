% ---------------- 2. Preliminaries ----------------

\textbf{Notation and variational objects.}
$X$ is the input and $Y$ the label with $K$ classes; the encoder induces the Markov chain
$Y - X - Z$. The IB objective is
$\mathrm{IB} \equiv \min_Z I(X;Z) - \beta_{\mathrm{IB}} I(Y;Z)$
\cite{tishby2000information}; its variational form (VIB) \cite{alemi2017deep} bounds
$I(X;Z)$ by the rate $R \equiv \mathbb{E}[\operatorname{KL}(q(z|x)\,\|\,m(z))] \ge I(X;Z)$
with a marginal prior $m$. The CEB objective \cite{fischer2020conditional} replaces the
compression term by the class-conditional residual
$I(X;Z|Y) = I(X;Z) - I(Y;Z)$ (its Eq.~4),
$\mathrm{CEB} \equiv \min_Z I(X;Z|Y) - \gamma I(Y;Z)$ (Eq.~5), variationally bounded by
the residual certificate
$\mathrm{ReX} \equiv \mathbb{E}[\operatorname{KL}(q(z|x)\,\|\,b(z|y))] \ge I(X;Z|Y)$
(Eq.~20), where $b(z|y)$ is a \emph{trainable} backward encoder. By the chain rule the
two objectives are algebraically identical for $\beta_{\mathrm{IB}} = 1 + \gamma$
\cite{geiger2020comparison}. Throughout we write losses as
$L = \mathrm{CE} + \beta\,\mathrm{KL}$, so $\beta = 1/\gamma$ and
$\beta_{\mathrm{IB}} = 1 + 1/\beta$.

\subsection{Theoretical guidance}

\textbf{The failure channels.} In the deterministic scenario $Y = f(X)$ ($H(Y|X) = 0$),
the conditional bottleneck degenerates along four channels (proofs in the Appendix).
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Dead knob} (Prop.~\ref{prop:dead}): for every $\gamma > 0$,
$\min_Z I(X;Z|Y) - \gamma I(Y;Z) = -\gamma H(Y)$, attained exactly on the copy corner
$\{Z : I(Y;Z) = H(Y),\, I(X;Z|Y) = 0\}$ --- $\gamma$ sweeps nothing, because CEB is IB
with $\beta_{\mathrm{IB}} = 1 + \gamma > 1$, i.e.\ the degenerate half-plane of
\cite{kolchinsky2019caveats}.
\item \emph{Flatness} (Prop.~\ref{prop:flat}): on the manifold
$T_\alpha = B_\alpha \cdot Y$ the variational value is
$\gamma(H(Y) - I(Y;T_\alpha))$: dropping $\delta$ nats of label information costs
$\delta/\beta$, and $\mathrm{ReX} \equiv 0$ on the manifold --- the certificate is blind
to label retention.
\item \emph{Collusion} (Prop.~\ref{prop:variance}): with diagonal Gaussian families
$q(z|x) = \mathcal{N}(m(x), \sigma^2 I)$, $b(z|y) = \mathcal{N}(g(y), \tau^2 I)$, the KL
is minimized over variances at $\sigma^2 = \tau^2$ (value
$\tfrac{1}{2\tau^2}\|m-g\|^2$), the classifier presses $\sigma \to 0$ (Jensen), and at
the standard logvar clamp $s = e^{-10}$ the objective degenerates into the mean-matching
term $\mathrm{ReX} = \tfrac{1}{2s}\mathbb{E}\|m(x) - g(y)\|^2$ with gradient weight
$1/s = e^{10} \approx 2.2\times10^{4}$: the encoder overfits $z$ to the label targets
$g(y)$ while the true residual grows without bound as the noise vanishes.
\item \emph{Object mismatch} (Prop.~\ref{prop:object}(a)): the classifier is trained on
the sampled code $Z$ but evaluated on the mean $\mu(X)$ (Fischer 2020, Appendix~A.1), and
the certificate bounds the \emph{training} code: by the DPI,
$I(X;Z|Y) \le I(X;\mu(X)|Y)$, strictly whenever the noise destroys within-class detail.
The deficit $I(X;\mu(X)|Y) - I(X;Z|Y)$ is positive, a trained quantity, and unobservable
during training --- controlling the certificate does not control the residual of the
representation used at inference.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Design principles.} Each structural choice of FGIB is forced by one of the
channels.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{The prior must be fixed} (channels 2 and 3). By the gap identity
$\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{KL}(q(z|x)\|r(z|y))] = I(X;Z|Y)
+ \mathbb{E}_y[\operatorname{KL}(q(z|y)\|r(z|y))]$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:gap}), a trainable
backward encoder eliminates the slack by moment matching \emph{alone} --- tightness then
certifies nothing (Prop.~\ref{prop:ordering}) --- while against a fixed prior the slack
can shrink only if the encoder moves toward it. FGIB therefore stores its
label-conditional prior as non-trainable buffers.
\item \emph{Prediction must not depend on the bottleneck noise} (channels 3 and 4). The
classifier's noise-aversion (Jensen) is what drives $\sigma \to 0$; a classifier that
consumes the sampled code trains the noise away and degenerates the certificate--object
pair. A deterministic main path decouples the variance from prediction
($\sigma^{2*} = 1$ for every $\beta$) and makes training and evaluation identical.
\item \emph{The prior geometry must exclude the trivial corner} (channels 1 and 2). The
collapsed code $\mu \equiv 0$ must not be a zero-cost solution: with per-class
orthonormal anchors $\mathcal{N}(a\,v_y, I)$ the collapse pays $\tfrac12 a^2$ for every
class, and the equilibrium codes $s^* v_y$ on the anchor frame are pairwise orthogonal ---
label information is retained by construction while the residual vanishes.
\item \emph{The compression term must be convex in the alignment} (channel 1). The
IB/CEB Lagrangians are monotone in their compression scalar on the natural solution
family, hence pinned at endpoints; a fixed reference turns the KL into a quadratic
displacement $\tfrac12\|\mu - a v_y\|^2 + {}$(variance terms), and on the class-aligned
equilibrium family $L_\beta(s) = \ln(1 + (K-1)e^{-cs}) + \tfrac{\beta}{2}(s-a)^2$ is
strictly convex with a unique interior minimizer $s^*(\beta) > a$ per $\beta$
(Props.~\ref{prop:sweep}, \ref{prop:curve}): $\beta$ sweeps the curve one-to-one --- the
exact property the IB and CEB Lagrangians lack.
\item \emph{The regularizer must bound the inference object} (channel 4). Through
the inverse linear transform $A^{-1}$, the side path is pointwise a CEB bottleneck on the
noisy inference representation $\tilde z = h + \tilde\varepsilon$ with
$\tilde\varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, A^{-1}\Sigma_q A^{-\top})$ and canonical anchors
$A^{-1}v_y$, orthonormal in the induced metric $A^{\top}A$ (Prop.~\ref{prop:fgib-ceb}):
the bound's object is the representation actually used at inference, up to \emph{known}
side-path noise (Props.~\ref{prop:inverse}, \ref{prop:object}) --- unlike CEB, whose
certificate bounds the training code, which lies strictly below the inference residual.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Model variants}

All variants share the backbone encoders, the classifier, and the loss template
$L = \mathrm{CE} + \beta \cdot \mathrm{KL}$; they differ only in the bottleneck mechanism.

\textbf{FGIB (classification).} The backbone encoder computes $h = f_{\mathrm{enc}}(x)$;
the classifier consumes $h$ directly, $\hat y = \mathrm{softmax}(W h + b)$, at both
training and evaluation (deterministic main path, principle 2). A linear side head
$\mu(x) = A\,h(x)$ ($A \in \mathbb{R}^{z \times z}$, unconstrained, $z = d$; the bias of
the implementation's linear layer is omitted) and a zero-initialized logvar head
parameterize the side posterior $q(z|x) = \mathcal{N}(\mu(x), \operatorname{diag}
\sigma^2(x))$. The label-conditional prior is the \emph{fixed} anchor table
$r(z|y) = \mathcal{N}(a\,v_y, I)$ (principles 1 and 3): $K$ orthonormal directions $v_y$
obtained by QR-decomposing a random matrix ($K \le z$), scaled by the anchor scale $a$
(default $4.0$), variance fixed at $1$, stored as non-trainable buffers. The training
loss is
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{FGIB}} = \mathbb{E}\big[\mathrm{CE}(\hat y, y)\big]
+ \beta\,\mathbb{E}\big[\operatorname{KL}\big(q(z|x)\,\|\,r(z|y)\big)\big],
\label{eq:fgib-loss}
\end{equation}
with the classification gradient entering only the shared encoder and the classifier (not
the side heads), and the KL gradient reaching the main path only through the shared
encoder. No sampling is ever performed: the KL is the closed-form diagonal Gaussian
divergence. The zero-initialized logvar head fixes $\sigma^2 = 1$ at initialization, so
training starts at $\operatorname{KL} \approx \tfrac12 a^2$ --- the anchor scale sets the
initial regularization pressure. The anchor scale $a = 0$ recovers the shared
$\mathcal{N}(0, I)$ prior and the VIB corner; the logvars are clamped to $[-10, 10]$ as
usual.

\textbf{FGIB (regression).} For continuous labels the per-class table is replaced by
fixed random-Fourier anchors
$\mu_p(y) = a\sqrt{2/z}\,\cos(2\pi\omega y + b)$ with frozen
$\omega \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 2^2)$, $b \sim U[0, 2\pi)$ (principle 3): the anchors lie
approximately on the sphere of radius $a$, so the scale keeps its geometric meaning, and
$a = 0$ again recovers $\mathcal{N}(0, I)$. The main path and the side machinery are
unchanged; the classifier outputs a scalar for regression targets.

\textbf{Baselines.} The same backbones and classifier, differing only in the bottleneck.
\emph{Deterministic} baselines train the classifier on $h$ with no regularizer.
\emph{VIB} \cite{alemi2017deep} samples $z$ and penalizes
$\operatorname{KL}(q(z|x)\|\mathcal{N}(0, I))$. \emph{SVIB} \cite{kolchinsky2019caveats}
uses the squared penalty $\beta\,\operatorname{KL}^2$. \emph{CEB}
\cite{fischer2020conditional} uses a trainable backward encoder $b(z|y)$ (linear on the
one-hot label, zero-initialized) and consumes the sampled $z$ at training / the mean at
evaluation. \emph{NIB} \cite{kolchinsky2019nonlinear} injects trainable-variance noise
and penalizes the non-parametric pairwise bound $\widehat I$. \emph{DVCCA}
\cite{abdelaleem2025dvmib} adds an input reconstruction decoder to VIB. The comparison
is controlled: every difference traces to the bottleneck mechanism.

\textbf{Backbones and shared implementation.} MLP: $784 \to 512 \to 256$ with ReLU and
dropout ($0.2$). CNN: stacked Conv3x3+ReLU+MaxPool layers with channels $(32, 64)$,
flattened into a $256$-dim hidden state (small feature maps skip down-sampling). GCN: two
graph-convolution layers with symmetric normalization and mean graph readout for
graph-level tasks. RNN: an embedding layer plus a multi-layer LSTM (embedding dim and
per-layer hidden dims given by the hidden-dims list), with last-state or masked max/mean
readout. In all variants $z = d = 256$ and the heads follow the zero-initialization
convention.
