The evaluation has three tracks: predictive comparison, controlled theory
illustration, and structural diagnostics. To keep the main paper readable, the main
figures show one classification compression sweep, targeted white-box attacks, and
the aggregate geometry/mismatch summaries. Regression compression curves, transfer
attacks, pair-level mismatch plots, and full uncertainty tables are reported in
Appendices~\ref{app:compression}, \ref{app:robustness},
\ref{app:direct-mismatch}, and \ref{app:main-std}--\ref{app:main-ci}; the remaining

\subsection{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}
We evaluate twelve dataset--backbone settings over nine public datasets: MNIST,
ImageNet-100, Cora, IMDb, AG News, California Housing, STS-B, ZINC-12k, and AgeDB,
covering MLP, CNN, GCN, and RNN backbones. All methods use the same splits, backbone,
stochastic Gaussian posterior, bottleneck dimension $d=256$, Adam training, early
stopping, and validation-based selection. The baselines are Base, VIB, SVIB, NIB,
CEB, supervised $\beta$-DVCCA, and the external NCBD/AdaCap comparisons; GPB is OPB
on classification rows and EPB on regression rows, with GPB-L denoting the identical
prior and a free head. Full data preprocessing, grids, seeds, and baseline definitions
are in Appendix~\ref{app:construction}.

\subsection{Controlled label ambiguity}
\label{sec:controlled-validation}
To isolate the comparator floor, we use $K=10$, $X=e_C$, and symmetric label noise
$r\in\{0,0.1,0.2,0.4\}$ with OPB $(a,\tau)=(6,1)$. The analytic ambiguity floor is
\begin{equation}
\delta_{\rm noise}(r)=\frac{a^2}{2\tau^2}
\left[1-(1-r)^2-\frac{r^2}{K-1}\right],
\label{eq:synthetic-noise-floor}
\end{equation}
which equals $0$, $3.4$, $6.4$, and $11.2$ nats. At $\beta=10$, the measured
$\widehat D$ is $0.0000$, $3.4018$, $6.4067$, and $11.2172$, respectively; over the
full sweep the excess remains below $0.032$ nats. Training evaluates the population
objective by weighting all $K^2$ clean/observed-label pairs by the noise model, with
a descending-$\beta$ warm start to remove avoidable optimization error at the
small-$\beta$ end. The explicit comparator takes posterior mean
$h_0(x)=a\sum_k\eta_k(x)q_k$ and covariance $\tau^2I$, where
$\eta_k(x)=P(Y=k\mid X=x)$; its KL equals $\delta_{\rm noise}(r)$ exactly, and its
anchor-energy risk $C_{\rm comp}(r)$ is estimated by Monte Carlo. Whenever the
fitted objective satisfies $\widehat L\le L_{\rm comp}=C_{\rm comp}+\beta\delta_{\rm noise}$,
non-negativity of the fitted risk gives the directly checkable comparison
\begin{equation}
\widehat D\;\le\;\widehat{\mathcal K}
\;\le\;\delta_{\rm noise}+\frac{C_{\rm comp}}{\beta}.
\label{eq:synthetic-comparator-check}
\end{equation}
Thus the known-noise experiment tracks the predicted floor, while the sampled-label
sensitivity and comparator-gate checks are deferred to
Appendix~\ref{app:noisy-alignment-sensitivity}.

\input{result5}

Table~\ref{tab:controlled-noisy-alignment} shows the transition from the zero to the
non-zero floor. The zero-noise row reproduces the exact-realizability limit to the
stored precision; for $r>0$ the measured mismatch tracks the analytic floor at both
ends of the $\beta$ grid, and the largest mean excess over the entire nine-point
sweep is $0.032$ nats. The excess is attained at the smallest $\beta$ and declines
monotonically with compression, without implying an exact $1/\beta$ law. Every fitted
population objective satisfies $\widehat L\le L_{\mathrm{comp}}$, so the explicit
comparator gate holds at every reported point; the fitted margin over the fixed
comparator grows with the noise level (from $-0.005$ to $-0.191$), as the comparator's
prediction rule is not free to trade KL against risk. Seed variation is at the
$10^{-4}$--$10^{-3}$ level, so the floor agreement is not an artifact of sampling
noise, and the corrupted-label and clean-class accuracies remain at their respective
ceilings ($1-r$ and $1$) across the sweep. Sampled-label, fixed-variance, and
trainable-frame sensitivity controls are deferred to
Appendix~\ref{app:noisy-alignment-sensitivity}.

\subsection{Direct mismatch analysis}
\label{sec:direct-mismatch}
For each checkpoint, the empirical variance bound $\widehat\tau^2_{\max}$ is the
largest conditional-reference variance realized over the class table
(classification) or test labels (regression); all statistics are first computed
within each seed and then aggregated across the five seeds, and class or bin pairs
are not treated as independent replicates. For test examples of class $k$, define
\begin{equation}
\widehat D_k:=\frac{1}{n_k}\sum_{n:y_n=k}
\frac{\|\mu_\phi(x_n)-a q_{\theta,k}\|^2}{2\widehat\tau^2_{\max}},
\qquad
\widehat m_k:=\frac{1}{n_k}\sum_{n:y_n=k}\mu_\phi(x_n).
\label{eq:empirical-class-mismatch}
\end{equation}
Jensen gives the per-class check
$\|\widehat m_k-aq_{\theta,k}\|
\le\sqrt{2\widehat\tau^2_{\max}\widehat D_k}$, and the triangle inequality gives the
empirical separation bound
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathrm{LB}}_{ij}=\sqrt{2}a
-\sqrt{2\widehat\tau^2_{\max}\widehat D_i}
-\sqrt{2\widehat\tau^2_{\max}\widehat D_j},
\label{eq:empirical-class-separation-bound}
\end{equation}
evaluated without clipping. Across all OPB scales, $\beta$ values, seeds, and
classes, no Jensen or separation-slack violation exceeds $10^{-6}$. CEB's mismatch is
shown descriptively, but its bound and slack entries are left empty because its
reference has no declared $\Delta$.

On ImageNet-100, OPB mismatch at
$a=6$ falls from $62.3$ to $2.54$ as $\beta$ increases from $5\cdot10^{-5}$ to $25$.
At $\beta=25$, the separation lower bound is positive for $96.9\%$, $62.3\%$, and
$0\%$ of class pairs at $a=12,6,1$. The raw bound rises toward the declared
$\sqrt2a$ as $\beta$ grows while the realized separation shrinks, so the slack
narrows exactly where the bound becomes informative. The fraction of certified pairs
climbs from near zero at weak compression to its plateau at $\beta=25$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:direct-mismatch}d); for $a=1$ the mismatch corrections exceed the
declared margin $\sqrt2a$ at every $\beta$, so no pair is ever certified. The median
slack grows with the anchor scale and narrows with compression
(Fig.~\ref{fig:direct-mismatch}e); its wide p5--p95 band shows the conservativeness
is non-uniform---a few pairs are near-tight while most are loose by a factor of
several. The mismatch ordering across scales reverses
along the sweep: at $\beta=5\cdot10^{-5}$ it is largest for the smallest frame
($349.8$ at $a=1$, against $82.1$ at $a=12$ and $62.3$ at $a=6$), while at $\beta=25$
the $a=12$ frame is tightest among the separated frames ($0.56$ against $2.54$ at
$a=6$; the $0.385$ at $a=1$ reflects the collapse of its centers). Since the
anchor-energy readout attains its prediction optimum exactly at the anchors, a larger
declared scale exerts a stronger pull on the posterior, making the tied readout
self-enforcing. MC-10 accuracy stays within $82$--$86\%$ while $\widehat D$ spans
three orders of magnitude: the mechanism curve is decoupled from the task metric, so
neither substitutes for the other. On Housing with $\rho=6$, mismatch falls from
$0.264$ to $0.026$, the axial slope remains $0.73$--$0.77$, and $43\%$ of finite-bin
pairs have a positive bound. The mismatch is almost entirely axial (about $0.2$
against $0.004$ off-axis), and $R^2$ changes only from $0.786$ to $0.767$ over the
sweep: a tenfold mismatch reduction need not imply a comparable task-metric gain.
For the finite-bin diagnostic we partition the standardized test labels into $20$
equal-width bins, merging any bin with fewer than $30$ examples. For bin $B_b$, let
$\bar y_b$ be its mean label, $\widehat m_b$ its posterior center, $\widehat D_b$
its average sample-to-own-anchor mismatch, and $h_b:=\operatorname{diam}(B_b)$. The
finite-bin lower bound is
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathrm{LB}}_{bc}
=\rho|\bar y_b-\bar y_c|
-\sqrt{2\widehat\tau^2_{\max}\widehat D_b}
-\sqrt{2\widehat\tau^2_{\max}\widehat D_c}
-\rho(h_b+h_c),
\label{eq:empirical-regression-separation-bound}
\end{equation}
where the last term is the quantization correction of
Remark~\ref{rem:pgib-binning}. Figure~\ref{fig:direct-mismatch}f plots the actual
bin-center distances against the declared isometric distances at $\rho=6$: the cloud
hugs a line of slope $0.73$--$0.77$ below the identity line at all four $\beta$
values, so the posterior follows the declared metric structure at about three
quarters of its scale, and the scale-transfer ratio is essentially
$\beta$-invariant. After the quantization correction, $43\%$ of bin pairs retain a
positive bound; all raw slacks remain non-negative to numerical tolerance---the
bound is consistent but conservative. The remaining Housing panels are in
Appendix~\ref{app:direct-mismatch}.

\subsection{Factor-wise attribution}
\label{sec:ablation}
The main comparison changes several factors simultaneously, so we also report a
paired intervention ladder in a common operating environment: every variant is
re-trained at its best shared configuration on the three settings of the mechanism
analysis (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}), and all comparisons are paired by seed. On
ImageNet-100, GPB reaches $84.2$ points, within $0.1$ of the free-scale OPB-FS
($84.3$) and clearly above the CEB ladder ($82.8$/CEB-energy $81.6$); on MNIST it
matches the best variants at $98.4$. On Housing, EPB ($0.788$) is within $0.003$
$R^2$ of EPB-L ($0.791$).

The ladder switches each factor independently. \emph{NCM-learn} removes the
bottleneck entirely: deterministic $z=\mu_\phi(x)$, energy readout on trainable
prototypes, no posterior and no KL. \emph{NCM-ortho} fixes the prototypes to the
orthogonal frame $a\,e_k$: fixed geometry, still no IB. \emph{CEB-energy} and
\emph{CEB-tied} mount the same energy readout (resp.\ tied projection) on CEB's
trainable free reference. \emph{OPB-FS} and \emph{EPB-FS} keep the orthogonal frame
and the energy (resp.\ tied) readout but make the anchor radius learnable, so only
the fixed scale is switched off. Together with Base, CEB, GPB-L and GPB this
isolates geometry (CEB-energy vs.\ OPB-FS), scale (OPB-FS vs.\ GPB), readout
(GPB-L vs.\ GPB; CEB vs.\ CEB-energy), and the IB term itself (NCM-ortho vs.\ GPB).

\input{ablation_result}

The factor-wise contrasts (accuracy points on ImageNet-100, $95\%$ paired CIs in
Appendix~\ref{app:ablation}) attribute the gain. The geometry intervention is the
largest ($+2.67$ $[+2.45,+2.92]$, OPB-FS over CEB-energy), followed by adding the IB
term ($+1.89$ $[+1.41,+2.36]$, GPB over NCM-ortho) and the tied readout ($+0.82$
$[+0.34,+1.17]$, GPB over GPB-L), while fixing the anchor scale costs essentially
nothing ($-0.04$ $[-0.07,-0.01]$, GPB over OPB-FS). The tied head thus retains most
of the free-head gain, and the fixed scale does not hurt: the ladder isolates the
contribution of the geometric prior, the bottleneck term, and the tied readout as
factor-wise contrasts, not a unique causal decomposition.

\label{sec:mechanism}

\paragraph{Limitations and future work.} The theorem is comparator-relative: on a
real benchmark its floor is determined by a prespecified comparator and its label,
mean-approximation, and covariance terms. The main table consequently reports
end-to-end utility, while the controlled and structural studies provide the
mechanism-level evidence for the theorem's ingredients; a post-hoc fit would not be
an informative floor estimate. Likewise, the separation lower bound is informative
when the realized mismatch is below the declared margin, so anchor scale remains an
operating choice rather than a universal constant. Extending the analysis to
data-driven comparator certificates and adaptive scale selection is a useful
direction for future work. The information-plane quantities are estimator-level
diagnostics, so tightening their connection to mutual-information estimands is
another useful extension. Finally, GPB combines known geometric primitives with the
conditional prior, KL, and tied readout rather than introducing a new ETF or prototype
primitive. Proofs and all supplementary evidence are collected in
Appendices~\ref{app:deferred}--\ref{sec:related}.

\paragraph{Evidence synthesis.} The experiments form a consistent progression from
construction to behavior. The complete baseline table establishes the end-to-end
utility of GPB across modalities and backbones; the factor interventions identify
which part of the construction accounts for the largest change. The controlled noise
study then varies label ambiguity while holding the representation problem fixed, so
the observed floor can be compared with the analytic decomposition. Finally, the
compression, information-plane, robustness, geometry, and mismatch diagnostics show
how the declared prior is reflected in the learned posterior at different operating
points. The detailed regression, transfer, uncertainty, and pair-level analyses in the
appendix use the same checkpoints and seeds, making the main figures summaries of a
single evaluation protocol rather than separate demonstrations.

Taken together, the results support a mechanism-level interpretation: constraining the
conditional prior changes the geometry available to the KL, and the tied readout makes
that geometry visible to prediction. The measured mismatch determines how much of the
declared geometry reaches the posterior, while the anchor scale determines where the
trade-off is useful. This separation between construction, transfer, and utility is
the organizing principle for reading the empirical results.
