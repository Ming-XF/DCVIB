A BSTRACT
Conditional information bottlenecks identify the information point of a representation — CEB’s minimum necessary information (MNI) — but not the geometry
at that point: we prove that any MNI solution can be bijectively recoded into
an equivalent solution whose inter-class distances, scale, and conditioning are arbitrary, so the optimizer, not the objective, settles the final label geometry; the
variational certificate moreover constrains the noisy training code, not the deterministic representation inference deploys. We take the zero-penalty set of the regularizer as the design object and propose the Fixed-Geometry Information Bottleneck (FGIB): the inference path stays deterministic, and the prior is replaced by a
frozen label-conditional geometry — an orthonormal anchor frame for classification, fixed random-Fourier anchors for regression — reached through a side head
inference never reads. Because the reference cannot move, we prove the variational KL is identically a fixed-prototype center loss throughout training: a deterministic main path freezes the zero-initialized variance head at the prior value, so
the compression term is exactly 2τ12 ∥µ − avy ∥2 at every step — the variational
form carries the conditional-information interpretation, while the empirical mechanism is pure geometry regularization. In the canonical configuration the side
head is a fixed orthogonal map, anchoring the deployed representation directly
with κ(A⊤ A) = 1, and at a fixed classifier scale every β selects a distinct interior
optimum whose fully compressed corner is the class-separated anchor configuration (cross-entropy ln(1 + (K−1)e−ca ), not the chance-level ln K of a marginal
prior). Across seven classification settings and four backbones under equal budget, FGIB’s β knob is the only one that is non-destructive over a grid spanning
five orders of magnitude — a property that survives a scale-free comparison of effective regularization strength — while its accuracy is essentially the backbone’s:
gains are small and statistically unstable (classification-only Wilcoxon p ≈ 0.22).
The contribution is the designed, well-conditioned, predictive geometry; a geometry ablation (orthonormal vs. simplex/ETF vs. random frames vs. learned centers,
on the same representation) shows fixedness matters more than the frame, and the
fixed head’s measured price is quantified rather than hidden. Regression is an exploratory transfer kept in the appendix; a full ledger separates what is proved from
what is open.

I NTRODUCTION

An information bottleneck asks a representation Z of an input X to be maximally informative about
a target Y and minimally informative about everything else (Tishby et al., 1999). The deep variational instantiation (Alemi et al., 2017) replaces the intractable I(X; Z) with E KL(q(z|x) ∥ m(z))
and minimizes CE + β KL; the Conditional Entropy Bottleneck (Fischer, 2020) sharpens the target
to the residual I(X; Z|Y ) with a learned backward encoder b(z|y). The programme has an appealing shape: a single knob β should trace a trade-off curve, and the value of the KL should serve as a
meter reporting how much unnecessary information the model still carries.
Two properties of that construction are usually left implicit. First, where the zero-penalty set lives:
the KL vanishes exactly on the prior, so the prior chooses the set of representations the regularizer

Conditional bottlenecks identify information, not geometry. The diagnosis starts from a property of the objective, stated and proved in Appendix A. In the deterministic scenario CEB’s objective
is, up to constants, the IB Lagrangian at βIB = 1 + γ > 1, and every γ > 0 shares the same exact minimizer — the copy corner — which is precisely CEB’s own declared target, the MNI point
(Proposition 1, Remark 1). Read correctly, this is not a failure of CEB: the objective does what it
is declared to do — identify the information point. What it does not identify is the geometry of the
representation at that point.
Geometric non-identifiability at the MNI solution. The gap is made precise by a single proposition, the paper’s theoretical pivot. (G) Geometric non-identifiability: any MNI solution can be bijectively recoded into an equivalent solution with the same information point and the same optimal
Bayes cross-entropy, while its Euclidean geometry — inter-class distances, scale, condition number
— is arbitrary within the class: for linear recodings, ∥B(my − my′ )∥2 = (my − my′ )⊤ B ⊤ B (my −
my′ ) with κ(B ⊤ B) arbitrary (Proposition 2). The objective identifies an equivalence class, never
a member of it; which member — which geometry — is reached is settled by the optimizer. Two
further observations are supporting rather than load-bearing. The residual certificate is blind to how
much of the label axis is retained on the collapse-to-copy manifold, and the objective trades one nat
of label information for 1/β nats of certified compression (Proposition 3) — a rate statement about
the certificate, not a claim of literal indifference, since the full objective also contains the prediction term. And no finite certificate bounds the residual of a deployed deterministic representation
(Proposition 5), a limitation of every variational bottleneck, ours included — one sentence here,
one paragraph in the appendix. A further channel — a variance race of the Gaussian parametrization, which exists only when the prior variance is trainable (CEB, not VIB; Remark 5) — is an
optimization diagnostic measured in the appendix, not part of the argument.
Designing the zero-penalty set. FGIB (Section 3) makes three changes that follow from the diagnosis: (i) the inference path is deterministic — the classifier consumes the encoder output h
at training and at evaluation; (ii) the stochastic bottleneck moves to a side path (µ(x) = Ah(x)
plus a log-variance head) that no inference path ever reads, keeping the variational bound finite
without putting noise in front of the classifier; (iii) the label-conditional prior is fixed by design,
r(z|y) = N (avy , τ 2 I) with an orthonormal anchor frame, so the zero-penalty set is the prescribed
class-separated configuration rather than collapse or an underdetermined family (regression: fixed
random-Fourier anchors on a sphere of radius a). The canonical configuration fixes the side head as
an orthogonal map (A⊤ A = I), so the anchor matching acts directly on the deployed representation
with κ = 1 by construction (Proposition 7); a trainable side head is an optional empirical variant
whose conditioning is measured, not assumed (Remark 9, Section 4.5).

X is the input, Y the label with K classes, and an encoder induces the Markov chain Y − X − Z;
throughout we use the loss convention CE + β KL (β = 1/γ, βIB = 1 + 1/β). The variational
objects R = E[KL(q(z|x)∥m(z))] ≥ I(X; Z) (VIB’s rate), ReX = E[KL(q(z|x)∥bθ (z|y))] ≥
I(X; Z|Y ) (CEB’s residual, trainable bθ ), and ReXF with a fixed anchor r are instances of the
gap identity E[KL(q(z|x)∥r(z|y))] = I(X; Z|Y ) + Ey [KL(q(z|y)∥r(z|y))] (Lemma 4): a bound
plus a slack, and everything below turns on who is allowed to move that slack to zero — CEB’s
backward encoder can do it with the encoder held fixed, so its bound can be tight while the representation is entirely uncompressed. Two observations follow (proved in Appendix A, stated in detail
in Appendix B).
The information point is unique. In the deterministic scenario every γ > 0 of CEB has the same
exact minimizer, the copy corner — which is the MNI point (Proposition 1, Remark 1). This is not
a failure of CEB: the objective does what it is declared to do, identify the information point. What
it does not identify is the geometry at that point, and γ cannot be reading off a position on the IB
curve.

Result 1 (Geometric non-identifiability at the MNI solution). If Z attains the MNI point, then every bijective recoding ψ(Z) attains the same information point, I(X; ψ(Z)|Y ) = I(X; Z|Y ) and
I(Y ; ψ(Z)) = I(Y ; Z), with the same optimal Bayes cross-entropy H(Y |ψ(Z)) = H(Y |Z). The
Euclidean geometry is unconstrained within the class: for linear recodings B ∈ GL(d) the classmean distances transform as ∥B(my − my′ )∥2 = (my − my′ )⊤ B ⊤ B (my − my′ ), so B = sI
rescales every inter-class distance by s and B = diag(c, 1, . . . , 1) sends κ(B ⊤ B) → ∞. Inter-class
distances, scale and conditioning of an MNI solution are optimizer artifacts, not objective-selected
quantities (Proposition 2).
The certificate is blind on the necessity axis. ReX is identically 0 on the collapse-to-copy manifold
Tα from total collapse to full copy, and the objective trades one nat of label information for 1/β nats
of certified compression (Proposition 3). Since the full objective also contains the prediction term,
this is a rate statement about the certificate — supporting, not load-bearing.
Scope. The certified object is the noisy training code, never the deployed representation:
I(X; Z|Y ) ≤ I(X; µ(X)|Y ) = +∞ for any continuous non-class-constant µ, CEB’s or FGIB’s
(Proposition 5) — a limitation, kept to one paragraph in the appendix. A variance race of the Gaussian parametrization exists only when the prior variance is trainable (CEB, not VIB, whose fixed
prior restores the posterior variance and whose high-β failure is the mean collapse q → N (0, I);
Remark 5); it is measured as a diagnostic in the appendix and carries no load in the argument.

Result 2 (The side path is a CEB bottleneck on the deployed representation). Let dim z =
dim h and let A be invertible. Pulling back by A−1 maps the posterior to q ′ (z̃|x) =
N (h(x), A−1 ΣA−⊤ ) — centered exactly at the representation inference uses — and the anchors
to N (aA−1 vy , τ 2 (A⊤ A)−1 ), which are orthonormal at scale a in the induced metric A⊤ A. Since
the Gaussian KL is invariant under a common invertible linear map (Lemma 1), the regularizer
of Eq. (3) is pointwise identical to a CEB bottleneck — with the backward encoder frozen at the
current side head — applied to z̃ = h + ε̃ (Proposition 6). At the variance optimum Σ = τ 2 I the
perturbation is τ (A⊤ A)−1/2 ε: known, side-path-only noise whose covariance is a model parameter (Proposition 12).
This licenses calling the construction a bottleneck at all: the regularizer carries a residualinformation bound read in coordinates centered on the deployed representation — an interpretation
a bare prototype loss lacks. The identity holds pointwise at each value of A; because a trainable A
moves, the pulled-back reference moves with it — the one channel the trainable-head configuration
leaves open, closed by the canonical configuration (Result 4).
Result 3 (A deterministic main path freezes the variance: the realized KL is a fixed-anchor center loss). With the classifier consuming h (never z), a zero-initialized log-variance head, a frozen
prior log-variance ℓp = ln τ 2 = 0, and a first-order optimizer without weight decay, the posterior log-variance is identically ℓq ≡ ℓp at every training step, and the regularizer is exactly

zero gradient at every subsequent step — we make no global-attractor claim for arbitrary initializations (Proposition 8). For trainable-prior bottlenecks the released variance is a real channel

the trainability of the anchor means — the ablation table’s number-coincidence is an identity —
and the one variant that escapes the family, DCEB’s trainable prior variance, is the one that collapses at β = 10. Its mechanism is a variance-inflation escape: the prior variance is driven up
(∂ℓp KL = − 12 (µ − µp )2 < 0 at the initialized point), which dilutes the anchor force e−ℓp (µ − µp )

positive price 2τ12 a2 per class.
Result 6 is the prediction the experiments test most directly: it says that pushing β up should not
destroy an FGIB model, and that the anchor scale a is the dial controlling how benign the fully
compressed corner is. Both are visible in Tables 2 and 3.

W HAT THE FROZEN REFERENCE DOES NOT BUY

We state the open items here rather than in a closing paragraph, because two of them bear on how
the experiments should be read. (a) A trainable side head is a second trainable party in the KL
— structurally CEB’s backward encoder — and it can absorb the anchor fit without moving h;
the canonical fixed-orthogonal head closes this channel by construction, and the measurement of
Section 4.5 shows the trainable variant’s κ(A⊤ A) degrades to 105.8 –106.8 while accuracy is unaffected, so the freedom buys nothing (Appendix C). (b) The sweep needs a bounded classifier scale
c; the implementation leaves it free, so Result 5 is conditional as implemented, and enforced-scale
variants are tested on MNIST (Section 4.5). (c) The certificate still does not bound the deployed
residual (P4 applies unchanged). (d) The compressed endpoint is designed, not certified: the anchor
corner is a label code, not semantic compression (Remark 15). (e) The sweep is over a surrogate:
the (KLanchor , CE) plane on the aligned family, not the information plane (Remark 13). The full
statement of each item is in Appendix C.

Result 6 predicts what happens at the other end of the sweep: a marginal-prior bottleneck has its
KL = 0 point at µ ≡ 0, so raising β far enough must drive it to the trivial predictor; FGIB’s KL = 0
point is class-separated. Table 2 reports the stress test: for each objective we take the worst test
score produced by any β in the grid, and report the deficit against the deterministic backbone of the
same row. We call it a stress test deliberately: the raw β grid is a shared protocol, not a shared axis
of effective regularization strength, since the objectives’ compression terms are not dimensionally
identical (Section 4); the scale-free re-reading below is the comparison.
The separation is stark. VIB, CEB and DVCCA are driven below the trivial predictor on all 7
classification settings (median worst-case deficits 71.9, 66.1, 79.1 points); SVIB is only marginally
better (64.8), as Remark 15 predicts: squaring the KL does not move the corner. FGIB’s median
worst-case deficit is 0.31 points at the validation-selected anchor and 0.35 at fixed a = 16; on

of the backbone on every one of the seven settings (per-setting values in Table 2) — against deficits
of 34–88 points for VIB, CEB and DVCCA on the same rows.

4.4

T HE ANCHOR SCALE IS THE DIAL THE THEORY SAYS IT IS

Result 6 says why FGIB is robust, and therefore when robustness should fail: the compressed corner
costs CE = ln(1 + (K−1)e−ca ), small only when ca is large relative to ln(K−1); at a → 0 the
anchors merge onto a single origin and FGIB degenerates to a VIB-style marginal prior (Remark 24).
Table 3 holds β at the largest grid value — maximum compression pressure — and varies a.
The prediction holds. At a = 1, FGIB behaves like the marginal-prior bottlenecks: 1.0% on
ImageNet-100/MLP (chance for 100 classes), 26.6% on Cora (below the majority class), R2 =
−0.001 on AgeDB/MLP (the mean predictor). Growing a recovers the backbone — monotonically
on ImageNet-100/MLP (1.0 → · · · → 83.0, 0.2 below its backbone), with local dips elsewhere
(Cora ends 87.2%, AgeDB/MLP R2 = 0.293, 7.5 points above its backbone). The trend, not the
monotonicity, is what the theory predicts, and it holds on 10 of the 12 rows. The two rows with no
upward trend are the two the theory says should not have one: IMDb is binary, so the corner value is
already negligible at a = 1 (the whole row sits above its backbone at every a); ZINC is a regression
row, where the random-Fourier anchors carry no corresponding guarantee (Remark 18) and it stays
4–6 points below its GCN throughout. Consistent with the (K−1) factor, the 100-class task needs
by far the largest anchor (MNIST/MLP, K=10, is within 0.3 points at a = 1; ImageNet-100/MLP
needs a ≥ 10).
The formula does not predict the transition threshold, and we should not pretend it does. Cora (K=7)
collapses at a ≤ 2 where MNIST (K=10) does not, so K is not the only factor — the classifier scale
c enters the corner value multiplicatively and is free in our implementation (Remark 14), and Cora
is a small transductive problem with correspondingly different margins. What Table 3 establishes is
the qualitative mechanism — recovery with a, chance-level collapse as a → the VIB limit, largest-K
needs largest a — not a quantitative fit, and not strict monotonicity.

C ONFOUND ABLATIONS AND DIAGNOSTICS ON MNIST

Section 3.3 identifies the channels FGIB leaves open; this section reports the supplementary experiments that test whether they bite, that separate FGIB’s two design changes from each other, and
that verify the two closure results of Section 3. All runs use the identical protocol as the main
sweep (100 epochs, patience 10, 5 seeds), except the long-horizon control noted below; the additional experiments total 360 runs across a 12-setting CentFGIB sweep at equal budget, a 4-setting
FGIB-H (A=I) sweep, a full-batch diagnostic rerun, a 400-epoch long-horizon control, a frozenETF baseline, and a subject-disjoint AgeDB sweep. Figure 1 plots the full β sweeps and Table 12
the numbers.
What the ablations show. FGIB differs from the comparison in two coupled ways: its classifier
consumes the deterministic h at training and evaluation (which also removes the trainable-prior
variance channel of Section 2), and its prior is frozen (the zero-penalty set is designed). Table 12

odd one out, in the corrected direction: its trainable prior variance is driven up (∂ℓp KL < 0 at
the initialized point), and the variance-inflation escape dilutes the anchor force e−ℓp (µ − µp ) —
the regularizer dies rather than amplifies; the diagnostic of Section 4.5 measures the trajectory. By

KL is the center loss exactly, and CentFGIB, TAFGIB and FGIB-H are the same objective up to the
trainability of the anchor means. The trainable anchors of TAFGIB do move (final column norms
shrink from 16 to 5.7–10), yet the accuracy curve is untouched — the load-bearing ingredient is the

W HAT WE HAVE NOT MEASURED

The experiments this paper’s own theory originally asked for are now in it (Section 4.5): the

path deterministic and confine the noise — and thus the finite bound — to a side path that inference
never reads.
Fixed geometric targets. Center loss pulls features toward learned per-class centers (Wen et al.,
2016); hyperspherical prototype networks place prototypes on a hypersphere a priori (Mettes et al.,
2019); the final classifier itself can be fixed with little accuracy loss (Hoffer et al., 2018), in particular to a simplex equiangular tight frame (Zhu et al., 2021; Pernici et al., 2019); and neural-collapse
analyses show such configurations arise spontaneously (Papyan et al., 2020). FGIB’s compressed
corner is a neural-collapse-like geometry — orthonormal class codes rather than the centered simplex/ETF configuration — but the construction differs in the role the geometry plays: it enters as the
reference of a variational bound, i.e. as the design of the bottleneck’s zero-penalty set, which makes
the compression term quadratic (Result 5) and gives the regularizer a residual-information reading
for the auxiliary code (Result 2) rather than serving as an auxiliary classification loss (Table 13,
Appendix D). The boundary of that claim is a theorem rather than an admission: in the released
implementation the KL is the fixed-anchor center loss (Result 3), and the canonical fixed-orthogonal
head moves the matching onto the deployed representation itself (Result 4) — the empirical robustness is shared with fixed-prototype regularization by construction, while the bound reading and the
sweep theory are specific to the variational form. The natural baselines from this literature are tested
directly: a frozen ETF classifier alone reproduces the backbone but buys no knob, and trainablecenter and fixed-center losses are the TAFGIB and CentFGIB rows of the ablation (Section 4.5).

C ONCLUSION

The high-regularization behavior of a variational bottleneck is decided by its zero-penalty set, and
the standard choices are poorly designed: VIB’s marginal prior places that set at collapse, CEB’s
learned backward encoder leaves it underdetermined. The deeper point of this paper is that the
conditional bottleneck’s objective identifies an information point — the MNI point — but never
the geometry at that point: any MNI solution can be bijectively recoded into an equivalent solution
with arbitrary inter-class distances, scale and conditioning (Result 1), and the optimizer alone settles which member of the equivalence class is trained. FGIB designs the zero-penalty set directly:
an orthonormal anchor frame fixed by construction makes the compression term quadratic, wellposes the sweep at fixed classifier scale, and turns the fully compressed corner into the prescribed
class-separated anchor configuration. Two properties of the construction are provable rather than
merely observed: the deterministic main path pins the variance at τ 2 for the entire run, so the realized regularizer is exactly the fixed-anchor center loss 2τ12 ∥µ − avy ∥2 — the variational form keeps
the conditional-information reading, while the empirical mechanism is pure geometry regularization; and the canonical fixed-orthogonal side head anchors the deployed representation directly with
κ = 1, closing the conditioning channel a trainable head leaves open, at a measured price of ≈ 0.5
points per setting that re-tuning the anchor scale largely recovers. Empirically, the knob is nondestructive over a six-point β grid spanning five orders of magnitude on every classification setting
— a property no other objective in the comparison has, and one that survives a scale-free comparison of effective regularization strength — while accuracy is essentially the backbone’s: gains are

FGIB notation. The deterministic main path computes h = fenc (X) and the classifier consumes
h directly; the side path parameterizes q(z|x) = N (µ(x), Σ(x)) with linear mean head µ(x) =

Propositions 6 and 12 are defined under this assumption), and a separate logvar head for Σ(x).
The side path is not part of inference: at evaluation the model returns the classifier output and
never evaluates the mean head (so A is a trainable module that exists only inside the regularizer

(default 1.0): the vy are orthonormal directions obtained by QR-decomposing a random matrix

the anchor variance, σ 2∗ = τ 2 ; we write σp2 for this common variance where the distinction does
not matter. The orthogonality of the method lies in this fixed frame and in the equilibrium it induces

an invertible linear side head T (h) = Ah, and per-class Gaussian priors with anchor variance τ 2 .
The code implements an affine head (nn.Linear with bias) — which the theory absorbs as an idealization, the bias being immaterial when A is learned jointly — and does not enforce orthogonality,
invertibility, or conditioning constraints on A. The guarantees below (orthonormal frames, invariance, conditioning) are properties of the idealized model; the implementation approximates them,
and the guarantees do not transfer automatically (Remarks 9, 11). Distributional standing assumption. Several results state that a deterministic continuous representation carries infinite conditional
information. Those statements assume the standard continuous setting: X | Y is non-atomic, and
the induced representation H(X) | Y is a non-atomic continuous variable. For the finite empirical
distribution of the training data no such conclusion is claimed; there the quantities are finite and only
their growth with sample size is governed by these results.

Remark 11 (Rank deficiency). If z < d or A is rank-deficient, only the projection of h onto the
row space of A (the image of A⊤ in h-space) receives gradient from the regularizer; the null-space
component is unconstrained. In the default configuration z = d and a randomly initialized A
is almost surely invertible, but invertibility carries no conditioning margin — near-singularity is
enough to produce the effects of Proposition 13(iii), and it is the direction the gap gradient points in
(Remark 9). If A ∈ Rz×d has full column rank (z ≥ d), a left inverse B with BA = I exists and the
data-processing inequality for KL gives, per (x, y), KL(q∥r) ≥ KL(B# q ∥ B# r) ≥ I(X; BZ | Y )

Remark 12 (Noise-free limit). The equivalence of Proposition 6 should be understood at the level
of the variational objective, not as a mutual-information limit: as the side-path noise vanishes,
I(X; Z|Y ) diverges for continuous deterministic H under the standing non-atomic assumption (the
pathology of deterministic networks discussed by Amjad & Geiger and Kolchinsky et al.), and the

noise into the inference path — is precisely what keeps the regularizer well-defined; this is the design
rationale of FGIB.

Remark 15 (The compressed endpoint is non-collapsed but still a label code — Caveat 2 remains).
VIB’s fixed marginal m(z) = N (0, I) places the KL = 0 point at µ = 0 for all classes: the fully
compressed solution is the trivial collapse with CE = ln K (chance level), and squaring (SVIB)
leaves this corner in place since KL2 is stationary at KL = 0. FGIB’s per-class orthonormal
anchors make the KL = 0 point a class-separated configuration: µy = avy with ⟨vy , vy′ ⟩ = δyy′ , so
at the corner CE(a) = ln(1 + (K − 1)e−ca ), which is bounded and tends to 0 as the anchor scale
a grows, and a = 0 recovers the VIB corner. But class-separation does not buy non-triviality in the
sense of the Caveats paper: their Caveat 2 identifies as uninteresting the variables on the manifold
Tα that mix a constant map with an exact copy of Y , compressing by “forgetting the input with
some probability” rather than by merging perceptually similar classes — and FGIB’s compressed
corner µy = avy is a deterministic function of Y alone, i.e. a label code of exactly that kind. The
orthonormal frame is in fact opposed to the kind of compression Caveat 2 holds up as interesting: it
places every pair of classes at the same distance and can never express “merge collie with retriever,
keep both far from teapot”. What the anchors do is choose one specific label-code geometry —

certified, and any claim that the method compresses input semantics at that endpoint is exactly the
claim Caveat 2 rules out.

Remark 20 (Two senses of tightness). In the pure variational value sense (same encoder, same
Gaussian family), CEB’s bound is tighter: ReX(θ∗ ) ≤ ReXF (Prop. 11), the excess being the
anchor gap — the price of the fixed anchor. The object sense does not reverse this ordering: both
bounds are valid upper bounds on their own noisy codes, and neither bounds the residual of a
deployed deterministic continuous representation, which is infinite under the standing non-atomic
assumption (Prop. 5). The structural difference is where the certified-versus- deployed gap lives —
in CEB it is driven by the prediction term inside the inference path; in FGIB it is a side-path quantity
whose size is governed by the conditioning of A (Prop. 13(iii)) — not which bound is “tighter” or
“valid”. The learnability of the side head A bears on that difference: it is a second trainable party
in the matching, unused at inference (Remark 9), so it can absorb part of the anchor gap without
moving the deployed representation. On the aligned family, where A is fixed by the ansatz, its
optimization drives the gap to the equilibrium value 12 (s∗ − a)2 at fixed classifier scale (Prop. 9);
off the ansatz this is an open question (Section A.4).

linear head: the KL invariance is exact, and the object claim needs only that the perturbation covariance τ 2 (A⊤ A)−1 be known (A is a model parameter) — but the object is informative about h only
insofar as that covariance is moderate, which the construction does not guarantee (Prop. 13(iii)).
The diagonal variational family is not closed under A−1 (Remark 10), so within the diagonal parameterization of the codebase the equivalence is exact at the distribution level and approximate as
a variational identity; a full-covariance side head would make it exact within the family. No orthogonality constraint on A is required — the orthogonality of the method lives in the fixed anchor frame
and in the equilibrium it induces (the aligned family).

The information point is unique in the deterministic scenario. By the chain rule, CEB’s objective is I(X; Z) − (1 + γ)I(Y ; Z), and the data-processing inequality gives Lγ (Z) ≥ −γI(Y ; Z) ≥
−γH(Y ) with equality iff I(X; Z) = I(Y ; Z) and I(Y ; Z) = H(Y ). Every γ > 0 therefore has
the same exact optimum — the corner, which is the MNI point — so γ 7→ (I(X; Z ∗ |Y ), I(Y ; Z ∗ ))
is constant and no interior point of the IB curve is ever a Lagrangian minimizer (Proposition 1).
Since βIB = 1 + γ > 1 always, CEB sits in the regime that Kolchinsky et al. (2019a) show to be
degenerate over its entire parameter range. Kolchinsky et al. (2019a) themselves note that if one’s
goal is the corner, this reads as β-robustness rather than failure — which is exactly our point: the
objective identifies the information point robustly, and nothing else. Proposition 2 states what it
does not identify: the corner is one point in the information plane but an entire equivalence class
of representations whose Euclidean geometry (inter-class distances, scale, conditioning) is arbitrary
within the class. Which member of the class is reached is settled by the optimizer, and FGIB’s
anchor frame is a designed choice of member.

The certificate is blind on the necessity axis (supporting). On the manifold Tα = Bα · Y of
Kolchinsky et al. (2019a), Tα is a function of Y alone, so I(X; Tα |Y ) = 0 and, at the optimal
backward encoder, ReX(Tα ) = 0 for every α — from total collapse (α = 0, no label information)
to full copy (α = 1). Reading ReX as a meter of distance to the MNI point is therefore valid
only along the I(X; Z|Y ) axis; on the necessity axis the meter reads 0 at chance level. With the
optimal classifier the objective is δ + β ReX where δ = H(Y ) − I(Y ; Tα ), so it is indifferent
between giving up δ nats of label information and saving δ/β nats of certified residual: in the heavyregularization regime the whole label axis is purchasable for H(Y )/β nats of certified compression
(Proposition 3). Because the full objective also contains the prediction term, this is a rate statement
about the certificate rather than a claim of literal indifference — supporting evidence, not a loadbearing premise.

The trainable-prior variance race (parametrization diagnostic). For the diagonal Gaussian
family with a trainable prior variance τ 2 , the KL is stationary in the posterior variance at σ 2 = τ 2 ,
with residual value ∥m − g∥2 /(2τ 2 ) — pure mean matching. The cross-entropy term, however,
is convex in z for a linear classifier (the log-sum-exp of an affine function of z), so by Jensen
Ez [CE(c(z), y)] ≥ CE(c(m), y) and it strictly prefers σ 2 → 0. Along the matched line the KL
resists shrinking τ 2 only while the means are unmatched (∂τ 2 KL = −∥m − g∥2 /(2τ 4 ) < 0); once
∥m − g∥ → 0 the race is unopposed and runs to the implementation clamp s = e−10 , at which the
certificate has become a mean-matching force of weight 1/s ≈ 2.2 × 104 between two trainable
maps (Proposition 4). With a fixed prior (VIB) this channel does not exist: the KL restores the posterior variance toward the prior value, and VIB’s high-β failure is the mean collapse q → N (0, I)
(Remark 5). Parts (ii)–(iii) of Proposition 4 are a direction-of-force argument, not a convergence
proof, and the whole channel is a parametrization-level diagnostic, not a premise of the paper’s argument. We ran the falsification test it invites, by logging the trained log-variances (Section 4.5):
at the early-stopping time scale of our protocol the race does not materialize — CEB’s variances
remain at their initialization σ 2 = τ 2 with zero clamp fraction on every β — so the convergence
prediction of part (iii) is empirically void under this protocol, exactly as Remark 6 allows.

small correction: whenever µ is continuous and not class-constant the right-hand side is +∞ (Amjad & Geiger, 2020; Kolchinsky et al., 2019b), so no finite quantity bounds the deployed residual.
This applies to FGIB as well (Proposition 5); what differs is structural, not a tightness ordering. In
CEB the perturbation is inside the inference path and is driven by the prediction term, so removing
it changes the model; in FGIB it lives on a side path that inference never reads and is controlled by
side-path parameters alone. That is a cleaner separation of regularizer from model, and we claim
nothing more for it. This limitation is orthogonal to the geometric non-identifiability argument and
carries no load in it.

(b) The sweep needs a bounded classifier scale. If c is free, then (s, c) = (a, ∞) sends KL → 0 and
CE → 0 simultaneously, so the infimum of Lβ is 0 for every β and Result 5 has no global counterpart
— the FGIB analogue of the dead knob, reached by growing the margin rather than by the linearity of
the IB curve. Our classifier is an unconstrained nn.Linear trained by Adam without weight decay,
so the proposition is conditional as implemented. A fixed-temperature cosine classifier satisfies
the assumption by construction; weight decay only mitigates the divergence (Remark 14). One
consequence matters for Section 4: since s∗ drifts toward a as c grows, part of any observed β-effect
under early stopping is plausibly an early-stopping effect, and we have not separated the two.
(c) The certificate still does not bound the deployed residual. P4 applies to FGIB unchanged (Proposition 5(b)). What FGIB buys is that the perturbation is side-path-only and its covariance is a known
model parameter — a cleaner separation of regularizer from model, not a tighter or more valid bound
(Remark 20).
(d) The compressed endpoint is designed, not certified. Caveat 2 of Kolchinsky et al. (2019a) identifies as uninteresting those variables that compress by mixing a constant map with a copy of Y ;
FGIB’s compressed corner µy = avy is a deterministic function of Y alone, i.e. a label code of
exactly that kind. The orthonormal frame is in fact opposed to the semantic compression Caveat 2
holds up as interesting: it places every pair of classes at the same distance and can never express
“merge these two classes, keep both far from that one”. What the anchors do is choose one specific
label-code geometry — a neural-collapse-like configuration (Papyan et al., 2020) — instead of leaving it to the optimizer, and refuse the chance-level corner. Any claim that FGIB compresses input
semantics at that endpoint is the claim Caveat 2 rules out (Remark 15).
(e) The sweep is over a surrogate. Result 5 concerns the (KLanchor , CE) plane on the aligned
family, not the information plane (I(X; T ), I(Y ; T )). On that family the posterior is class-constant,
so I(X; Z|Y ) = 0 for every s; what varies along the sweep is anchor mismatch and classifier
margin, not a proved compression of mutual information (Remark 13).

The variance race does not materialize. We ran the falsification test that Proposition 4 invites, logging the mean posterior log-variance, the clamp fraction (e−10 ), and (for CEB) the prior
log-variance at every epoch of every run, aggregated over the entire validation set, not a single batch. At the final epoch, averaged over the 5 seeds, CEB’s posterior log-variance sits at
+0.37, +0.27, +0.13, +0.08, −0.05, −0.01 (nats) for β = 10−4 , . . . , 10 — a mild monotone drift
with β, nowhere near the clamp — and the clamp fraction is 0.0 at every epoch of every run; CEB’s
prior tracks its posterior, and VIB’s posterior stays within 0.37 of zero (Figure 3(b)). To probe
whether the race is an early-stopping artifact, we retrained CEB at β ∈ {1, 10} for 400 epochs
with early stopping disabled. The answer is nuanced and we report it as measured: the variance
does drift, monotonically and in exactly the direction Proposition 4(i)–(ii) predicts — at β = 1 the
posterior log-variance ends at −2.27 nats (−1.20 at β = 10; the prior tracks the posterior), passing
−0.92 at epoch 100 — but the clamp fraction is 0.0 throughout, and −2.3 nats (σ 2 ≈ 0.10) is still
far from the e−10 clamp the two-stage account predicts as the terminal state (Figure 4). The race
is therefore real but slow: at the protocol horizon it is invisible, at four times the horizon its direction is unmistakable, and its convergence time-scale is beyond 400 epochs at these hyperparameters.

That is precisely the conditional status Remark 6 assigns to Proposition 4(iii): a direction-of-force
statement whose terminal state our longest run has not reached. For FGIB the question is settled
analytically: by Result 3 the posterior variance is pinned at τ 2 at every step — the flat zero line of
Figure 4 is the fixed point itself, held for 400 epochs — and Corollary 1 identifies the trainable prior
variance as the only re-entry point for the race.
The side-head conditioning channel. Section 3.3(a) identifies the trainable side head A as the
channel the optional variant leaves open, and predicts that the approach to a rank-K projector would
show up as diverging κ(A⊤ A). Figure 3(a) plots log10 κ(A⊤ A) over training, at a = 16, for every
β: from its initialization near 103.9 the condition number rises to 105.8 –106.8 at the final epoch,
with transient peaks to 108.3 — A⊤ A becomes severely ill-conditioned (≈ 1000× degradation, no
monotone dependence on β). Predictive accuracy nevertheless remains stable, so the conclusion is
narrow: the ill-conditioning does not hurt MNIST predictions in this experiment, but it means the
pulled-back reference of Result 2 is not close to a well-conditioned frame, and the KL’s correspondence with h is not guaranteed by anything we measured for the trainable variant. The canonical
configuration needs no such caveat: A = I has κ = 1 at every step and anchors h directly (Result 4). The A=I and frozen-A rows of Table 12 show what the degradation costs in accuracy (little
on MNIST), and Table 8 quantifies the fixed head’s price across five settings: ≈ 0.5 points per
setting at the inherited anchor scale, largely recovered by re-tuning the scale — a real but bounded
margin.
Fixed classifier scale. Proposition 9 requires a fixed classifier scale c, which the default
nn.Linear does not enforce. The cosine rows of Table 12 enforce it by construction: FGIB’s
sweep is unchanged (97.9–98.3 across all six β values), while VIB and CEB are not rescued (51.9%
at β = 1 and 41.6% at β = 0.1 respectively), exactly as Remark 14 predicts — fixed c does not
repair the trainable-prior corners; the anchor geometry, not the free classifier scale, keeps FGIB’s
corner non-degenerate (Appendix D, cosine rows).
Does the geometry alone generalize? Result 3 proves the KL machinery is never load-bearing
in this architecture, on any dataset. Table 7 asks the cross-dataset version: CentFGIB run on all
12 settings under the equal-budget protocol (a = 16, 6-point β grid, 5 seeds). The geometry alone
generalizes: at the validation-selected configuration, CentFGIB matches FGIB(a=16) to within 0.01
points on 10 of 12 settings and to within 0.43 on the remaining two, including all four regression
settings with RFF anchors; in a nine-entry ranking the FGIB family occupies the top three slots. The
empirical engine of the method is the fixed anchor geometry itself; the KL contributes the bound
reading and the sweep theory (Appendix D).

Does the side head need to exist at all? The canonical configuration FGIB-H (A=I) removes
the side head from the theory’s concerns entirely: the anchor matching acts directly on the deployed
representation (Result 4). Table 8 measures the price across five settings — MNIST/MLP and /CNN,
ImageNet-100 features, California Housing, Cora. At a = 16, the scale inherited from the z-space
sweep, the fixed head trails the trainable one by ≈ 0.5 points per setting (mean rank 4.00 of 5;
FGIB 1.20, CentFGIB 1.40) — the trainable head is doing real adaptive work, and part of that
work is rescaling the effective geometry. Re-tuning the anchor scale for h-space recovers most of
the gap: at a = 4, FGIB-H takes mean rank 2.25 and ties or beats the backbone on every row
(MNIST/CNN 99.29, best of the table; Cora 87.68, tied-best), so the price of the κ = 1 closure is
small and quantifiable — an accuracy-versus-certification choice whose two sides both ship in the
released code. We also test the other fixed geometry the class invites: a backbone with a frozen ETF
classifier (equiangular tight frame head, no regularizer) scores 98.32% on MNIST/MLP against
the backbone’s 98.33% — a fixed classifier alone buys neither margin nor a knob, so the anchor
regularization on the representation is the load-bearing ingredient (Table 12, ETF row).

W HAT WE HAVE NOT MEASURED : FULL STATEMENT

(i) Separating the β-effect from the early-stopping effect. Remark 14(b) predicts that the βdependence of the equilibrium decays with training length; our runs use patience 10, so training
well past saturation and comparing converged KL across β would test it. The cosine-classifier runs
of Section 4.5 enforce the fixed-c assumption of Proposition 9 but do not remove early stopping, and
the 400-epoch control of that section covers the variance trajectory, not the sweep itself. (ii) Equal
budget for the other methods. The a = 16 column of Table 4 gives FGIB the same 6-point budget as
the comparison; Table 6 shows the rank is stable across fixed anchor scales, not only at a = 16. The
converse — giving VIB/CEB/DVCCA an extra temperature or variance hyperparameter dimension
— is not run, so the comparison’s rank ordering is only shown to survive budget restriction for FGIB,
not budget expansion for the others. (iii) The remaining protocol asks. All results come from one
fixed 60/20/20 split per dataset (seed 42), so split-sensitivity is unmeasured — the subject-disjoint
AgeDB control measures leakage sensitivity for one dataset, not split noise in general. Downstream
robustness properties claimed for conditional bottlenecks elsewhere (adversarial robustness, OoD
detection, calibration) are not evaluated here and carry no weight in this paper’s argument; the labelfree anchor-distance score miny KL(q(z|x)∥N (avy , τ 2 I)) that FGIB’s geometry makes available
at inference (Remark 8) is a hypothesis, not a result.

MNIST (LeCun et al., 1998): 70,000 28 × 28 grayscale digits; the 60,000-image training partition
is split into 50,000 train / 10,000 validation, the official 10,000-image test set is untouched. Inputs
are normalized to [0, 1].
ImageNet-100 (Deng et al., 2009): frozen ResNet-50 (He et al., 2016) layer-3 features. Two
variants are used: global average pooling (1024-d, the MLP backbone) and 4 × 4 spatial pooling
(1024 × 4 × 4, the CNN backbone). Features are split 60/20/20 (stratified) and standardized with
a StandardScaler fitted on the training split.
Cora (Sen et al., 2008): the standard transductive node-classification graph (2708 nodes, 1433d features, 7 classes) with a fixed random split (seed 42) into train/validation/test node masks;
the adjacency matrix is symmetrized, self-loops are added, and the normalized Laplacian-style
D̃−1/2 (Ã)D̃−1/2 is used. Training is full-graph (transductive), following standard GCN practice
(Kipf & Welling, 2017); this is not the fixed Planetoid split, so absolute numbers are not directly
comparable with published Planetoid numbers — all comparisons in this paper are internal to the
same split.
IMDb (Maas et al., 2011): the official 50,000 labeled reviews (25k train + 25k test) are merged
and re-divided 60/20/20 (stratified) into 30k/10k/10k. HTML tags are stripped, tokens are alphabetic words from a training-split vocabulary (approx. 72k words at length cap 250), and sequences
are truncated or padded to 250 tokens. Official-split literature numbers are therefore not directly
comparable; again, all comparisons are internal.
AG News (Zhang et al., 2015): frozen bert-base-uncased (Devlin et al., 2019) token features. We
use the second-to-last hidden layer (layer 10) of each token, PCA-reduced to 50 dimensions (the
PCA fit on training-split tokens only), sequences capped at 64 tokens; 60/20/20 stratified split over
127,600 documents.
California Housing (Pace & Barry, 1997): 20,640 samples, 8 features, 60/20/20 split (regression, unstratified); features standardized on the training split, target min-max scaled.
STS-B (Cer et al., 2017; Wang et al., 2019): the official GLUE train and dev partitions (7,249
labeled sentence pairs, similarity 0–5) are merged and re-divided 60/20/20 into 4,349/1,450/1,450
(the GLUE test partition carries no labels and is unused). Each sentence is stripped of HTML,
tokenized alphabetically, and truncated to 124 tokens; the pair is joined by a [SEP] token into a
single sequence of length ≤ 250. The vocabulary is built from the training split (approx. 10k words).
Embeddings are GloVe-6B-100d (Pennington et al., 2014), initialized and frozen; the LSTM has a
single 256-d hidden layer and max-pooling readout.
