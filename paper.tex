\begin{abstract}
Conditional information bottlenecks identify the \emph{information point} of a
representation --- CEB's minimum necessary information (MNI) --- but not the
\emph{geometry} at that point: we prove that any MNI solution can be bijectively
recoded into an equivalent solution whose inter-class distances, scale, and
conditioning are arbitrary, so the optimizer, not the objective, settles the final
label geometry; the variational certificate moreover constrains the noisy training
code, not the deterministic representation inference deploys. We take the
zero-penalty set of the regularizer as the design object and propose the
Fixed-Geometry Information Bottleneck (FGIB): the inference path stays deterministic,
and the prior is replaced by a \emph{frozen} label-conditional geometry --- an
orthonormal anchor frame for classification, fixed random-Fourier anchors for
regression --- reached through a side head inference never reads. Because the
reference cannot move, we prove the variational KL is \emph{identically} a
fixed-prototype center loss throughout training: a deterministic main path freezes
the zero-initialized variance head at the prior value, so the compression term is
exactly $\tfrac{1}{2\tau^2}\|\mu-a v_y\|^2$ at every step --- the variational form
carries the conditional-information interpretation, while the empirical mechanism is
pure geometry regularization. In the canonical configuration the side head is a
\emph{fixed orthogonal} map, anchoring the deployed representation directly with
$\kappa(A^{\top}A)=1$, and at a fixed classifier scale every $\beta$ selects a
distinct interior optimum whose fully compressed corner is the class-separated anchor
configuration (cross-entropy $\ln(1+(K{-}1)e^{-ca})$, not the chance-level $\ln K$ of
a marginal prior). Across seven classification settings and four backbones under
equal budget, FGIB's $\beta$ knob is the only one that is non-destructive over a
grid spanning five orders of magnitude --- a property that survives a scale-free
comparison of effective regularization strength --- while its accuracy is essentially
the backbone's: gains are small and statistically unstable (classification-only
Wilcoxon $p\approx0.22$). The contribution is the designed, well-conditioned,
predictive geometry; a geometry ablation (orthonormal vs.\ simplex/ETF vs.\ random
frames vs.\ learned centers, on the same representation) shows fixedness matters more
than the frame, and the fixed head's measured price is quantified rather than hidden.
Regression is an exploratory transfer kept in the appendix; a full ledger separates
what is proved from what is open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

An information bottleneck asks a representation $Z$ of an input $X$ to be maximally
informative about a target $Y$ and minimally informative about everything else
\citep{tishby1999ib}. The deep variational instantiation
\citep{alemi2017vib} replaces the intractable $I(X;Z)$ with
$\mathbb{E}\,\KLd(q(z|x)\,\|\,m(z))$ and minimizes $\CE + \beta\,\KLd$; the Conditional
Entropy Bottleneck \citep{fischer2020ceb} sharpens the target to the \emph{residual}
$I(X;Z|Y)$ with a learned backward encoder $b(z|y)$. Two properties of that
construction are usually left implicit. First, \emph{where the zero-penalty set
lives}: the KL vanishes exactly on the prior, so the prior chooses the set of
representations the regularizer never penalizes --- a marginal prior places it at
collapse, a learned conditional prior leaves it an underdetermined family, and the
optimizer, not the objective, selects the final label geometry. Second, \emph{the
object being regularized is not the object being deployed}: the bound constrains a
noisy training code $z \sim q(z|x)$ while inference consumes the deterministic mean
$\mu(x)$. This paper takes the zero-penalty set as the design object, and asks what
changes if it is fixed by design rather than left to move or to vanish.

\paragraph{Conditional bottlenecks identify information, not geometry.}
In the deterministic scenario CEB's objective is, up to constants, the IB Lagrangian at
$\beta_{\mathrm{IB}} = 1 + \gamma > 1$, and every $\gamma > 0$ shares the same exact
minimizer --- the copy corner --- which is precisely CEB's own declared target, the MNI
point (Proposition~\ref{prop:dead}, Remark~\ref{rem:mni}). Read correctly, this is not
a failure of CEB: the objective does what it is declared to do --- identify the
information point. What it does \emph{not} identify is the geometry of the
representation at that point.

\paragraph{Geometric non-identifiability at the MNI solution.}
The gap is made precise by a single proposition, the paper's theoretical pivot.
\emph{(G) Geometric non-identifiability:} any MNI solution can be bijectively recoded
into an equivalent solution with the same information point and the same optimal Bayes
cross-entropy, while its Euclidean geometry --- inter-class distances, scale, condition
number --- is arbitrary within the class: for linear recodings,
$\|B(m_y - m_{y'})\|^2 = (m_y - m_{y'})^{\top}B^{\top}B\,(m_y - m_{y'})$ with
$\kappa(B^{\top}B)$ arbitrary (Proposition~\ref{prop:geomet}). The objective
identifies an \emph{equivalence class}, never a member of it; which member --- which
geometry --- is reached is settled by the optimizer. Two further observations are
supporting rather than load-bearing: the residual certificate is blind to label
retention on the collapse-to-copy manifold (Proposition~\ref{prop:flat}), and no
finite certificate bounds the residual of a deployed deterministic representation
(Proposition~\ref{prop:object}) --- a limitation of every variational bottleneck, ours
included. A variance race of the Gaussian parametrization, which exists only when the
prior variance is trainable (CEB, not VIB; Remark~\ref{rem:vib-prior}), is measured as
an optimization diagnostic in the appendix and carries no load in the argument.

\paragraph{Designing the zero-penalty set.}
FGIB (Section~\ref{sec:method}) makes three changes that follow from the diagnosis:
(i) the inference path is deterministic --- the classifier consumes the encoder output
$h$ at training \emph{and} at evaluation; (ii) the stochastic bottleneck moves to a
\emph{side path} ($\mu(x) = Ah(x)$ plus a log-variance head) that no inference path ever
reads, keeping the variational bound finite without putting noise in front of the
classifier; (iii) the label-conditional prior is \emph{fixed by design},
$r(z|y) = \Ncal(a v_y, \tau^2 I)$ with an orthonormal anchor frame, so the zero-penalty
set is the prescribed class-separated configuration rather than collapse or an
underdetermined family. The \emph{canonical} configuration fixes the side head as an
orthogonal map ($A^{\top}A = I$), anchoring the deployed representation directly with
$\kappa = 1$ by construction (Proposition~\ref{prop:orth}); a trainable side head is
an optional empirical variant whose conditioning is measured, not assumed
(Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).

Freezing the reference is what buys the theory. Against a fixed $\mu_p$, the KL's
displacement term $\tfrac{1}{2\tau^2}\|\mu - \mu_p\|^2$ is \emph{quadratic} in the
alignment scalar --- the convexification squared-IB
\citep{kolchinsky2019caveats} installs by hand is built in --- and at fixed classifier
scale every $\beta$ has a unique interior optimum on a strictly concave trade-off curve
(Propositions~\ref{prop:sweep} and~\ref{prop:curve}). Equally important, the KL now
vanishes at the class-separated configuration $\mu_y = a v_y$, with cross-entropy
$\ln(1 + (K{-}1)e^{-ca}) \to 0$ as $a$ grows, where a marginal $\Ncal(0,I)$ prior
places $\KLd = 0$ at $\mu \equiv 0$ with cross-entropy $\ln K$ --- chance
(Remark~\ref{rem:endpoint}): the degenerate corner is excluded by geometry rather than
by the optimizer's good behavior.

\paragraph{Fixed geometry as a variationally interpretable prototype regularizer.}
We evaluate the canonical configuration on 9 datasets over MLP, CNN, GCN and LSTM
backbones against the deterministic backbone and five objectives: VIB
\citep{alemi2017vib}, squared-IB \citep{kolchinsky2019caveats}, nonlinear IB
\citep{kolchinsky2019nib}, CEB \citep{fischer2020ceb} and supervised $\beta$-DVCCA
\citep{abdelaleem2025dvmib}; the sweep is $1{,}020$ configurations $\times$ 5 seeds
$=5{,}100$ runs plus equal-budget and ablation controls, with every hyperparameter
selected on validation. Three findings, in Section~\ref{sec:exp}:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{The regularizer is exactly a fixed-prototype loss, and the prototype
geometry is designed.} A deterministic main path freezes the zero-initialized variance
head at the prior value, so the variational KL is \emph{identically}
$\tfrac{1}{2\tau^2}\|\mu - a v_y\|^2$ at every training step
(Proposition~\ref{prop:varfreeze}): the variational form carries the
conditional-information interpretation, the empirical mechanism is pure geometry
regularization, and a geometry ablation shows the stability rides on
\emph{fixedness and class separation}, not on the particular frame
(Table~\ref{tab:geometry}).
\item \emph{The canonical fixed head costs little and closes the theory.} Across the
seven classification settings under equal budget, FGIB-H (at the re-tuned scale
$a{=}4$) stays within $0.23$ accuracy points of its backbone at its worst setting and
is best or tied-best on $2$ of $7$ settings, with mean rank $2.86$ of $5$ against the
backbone's $3.29$ and $\kappa(A^{\top}A)=1$ by construction (Table~\ref{tab:hanchor});
the optional trainable head reaches the same accuracies but its conditioning degrades
to $10^{5.8}$--$10^{6.8}$ without buying accuracy.
\item \emph{The knob is non-destructive, and this survives a scale-free comparison.}
Sweeping $\beta$ over a six-point grid spanning five orders of magnitude leaves FGIB
within $0.52$ accuracy points of its backbone on \emph{every} classification setting,
while VIB, CEB and DVCCA fall to the trivial predictor on all seven
(Table~\ref{tab:robust}); the same picture holds on the scale-free axis of effective
regularization strength (Table~\ref{tab:effective}, Figure~\ref{fig:effective}).
Accuracy gains are small and task-set-sensitive (classification-only Wilcoxon
$p\approx0.22$), so we claim a designed, well-conditioned, predictive geometry --- not
a better stochastic bottleneck.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What we do not claim.}
The one-to-one sweep requires a bounded classifier scale the implementation does not
enforce (Remark~\ref{rem:c-scale}); no finite certificate bounds the deployed
deterministic residual, ours included (Proposition~\ref{prop:object}); the sweep is a
KL--CE surrogate curve on a class-symmetric family, not a recovered IB curve
(Remark~\ref{rem:surrogate}); and the KL adds the residual-information reading and the
sweep theory, not a measured accuracy margin. Appendix~\ref{app:theory} keeps a
complete ledger of what is proved and what is open.

\section{Preliminaries: conditional bottlenecks identify information, not geometry}
\label{sec:prelim}

$X$ is the input, $Y$ the label with $K$ classes, and an encoder induces the Markov chain
$Y - X - Z$; we use the loss convention $\CE + \beta\,\KLd$ ($\beta = 1/\gamma$,
$\beta_{\mathrm{IB}} = 1 + 1/\beta$). The variational objects
$R = \mathbb{E}[\KLd(q(z|x)\|m(z))] \ge I(X;Z)$ (VIB's rate) and
$\ReX = \mathbb{E}[\KLd(q(z|x)\|b_\theta(z|y))] \ge I(X;Z|Y)$ (CEB's residual, trainable
$b_\theta$) --- and $\ReX_F$ with a \emph{fixed} anchor $r$ --- are instances of the gap
identity $\mathbb{E}[\KLd(q(z|x)\|r(z|y))] = I(X;Z|Y)
+ \mathbb{E}_y[\KLd(q(z|y)\|r(z|y))]$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:gap}): a bound plus a slack, and
everything below turns on \emph{who is allowed to move that slack to zero}. Two
observations follow (proved in Appendix~\ref{app:theory}, stated in detail in
Appendix~\ref{app:prelim-detail}).

\emph{The information point is unique.} In the deterministic scenario every $\gamma>0$
of CEB has the same exact minimizer, the copy corner --- which \emph{is} the MNI point
(Proposition~\ref{prop:dead}, Remark~\ref{rem:mni}). This is not a failure of CEB: the
objective does what it is declared to do, identify the information point. What it does
not identify is the geometry at that point, and $\gamma$ cannot be reading off a
position on the IB curve.

\begin{result}[Geometric non-identifiability at the MNI solution]\label{res:geomet}
If $Z$ attains the MNI point, then every bijective recoding $\psi(Z)$ attains the same
information point, $I(X;\psi(Z)|Y) = I(X;Z|Y)$ and $I(Y;\psi(Z)) = I(Y;Z)$, with the
same optimal Bayes cross-entropy $H(Y|\psi(Z)) = H(Y|Z)$. The Euclidean geometry is
unconstrained within the class: for linear recodings $B \in \mathrm{GL}(d)$ the
class-mean distances transform as
$\|B(m_y-m_{y'})\|^2 = (m_y-m_{y'})^{\top}B^{\top}B\,(m_y-m_{y'})$, so $B = sI$
rescales every inter-class distance by $s$ and $B = \mathrm{diag}(c,1,\dots,1)$ sends
$\kappa(B^{\top}B) \to \infty$. Inter-class distances, scale and conditioning of an MNI
solution are optimizer artifacts, not objective-selected quantities
(Proposition~\ref{prop:geomet}).
\end{result}

\emph{The certificate is blind on the necessity axis.} $\ReX$ is identically $0$ on the
collapse-to-copy manifold $T_\alpha$ from total collapse to full copy, and the objective
trades one nat of label information for $1/\beta$ nats of certified compression
(Proposition~\ref{prop:flat}). Since the full objective also contains the prediction
term, this is a rate statement about the certificate --- supporting, not load-bearing.

\emph{Scope.} The certified object is the noisy training code, never the deployed
representation: $I(X;Z|Y) \le I(X;\mu(X)|Y) = +\infty$ for any continuous
non-class-constant $\mu$, CEB's or FGIB's (Proposition~\ref{prop:object}). A variance
race of the Gaussian parametrization exists only when the prior variance is trainable
(CEB, not VIB, whose fixed prior restores the posterior variance and whose high-$\beta$
failure is the mean collapse $q \to \Ncal(0,I)$; Remark~\ref{rem:vib-prior}); it is
measured as a diagnostic in the appendix and carries no load in the argument.

\section{Method: the Fixed-Geometry Information Bottleneck}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Model}

A shared encoder computes the deterministic representation $h = f_{\mathrm{enc}}(X)$, and
the task head consumes $h$ directly at training and evaluation:
$\mathrm{logits} = c(h)$. In parallel, a \emph{side path} maps $h$ to a diagonal Gaussian
posterior
\begin{equation}
q(z|x) = \Ncal\big(\mu(x),\,\Sigma(x)\big),
\qquad \mu(x) = A\,h(x), \qquad
\Sigma(x) = \mathrm{diag}\,\exp\!\big(\ell(h(x))\big),
\label{eq:side}
\end{equation}
with a linear mean head $A$ and a log-variance head $\ell$, neither evaluated at
inference. The label-conditional prior is a set of frozen buffers,
\begin{equation}
r(z|y) = \Ncal\big(a\,v_y,\ \tau^2 I\big),
\qquad
V = [v_1,\dots,v_K] \ \text{orthonormal},\ \ V^{\top}V = I_K ,
\label{eq:anchor}
\end{equation}
where $V$ is the orthonormal factor of the QR decomposition of a random matrix, $a > 0$
the \emph{anchor scale} and $\tau^2$ the \emph{anchor variance} (default $1$). The
training objective is
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{FGIB}}
= \mathbb{E}\big[\CE\big(c(h), y\big)\big]
+ \beta\,\mathbb{E}\big[\KLd\big(q(z|x)\,\|\,r(z|y)\big)\big] .
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
Gradients from the second term reach the main path only through the shared encoder. The
log-variance head is zero-initialized, and Result~\ref{res:varfreeze} proves the
variance stays pinned at $\tau^2$ for the entire run, so the realized regularizer is
exactly $\tfrac{1}{2\tau^2}\|\mu - \mu_p\|^2$ at every step. The canonical
configuration fixes the side head to an \emph{orthogonal} map --- with
$\dim z = \dim h$ one may take $A = I$ without loss (Result~\ref{res:orth}) --- so the
anchor matching acts directly on the deployed representation; the trainable head of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:side} is then an optional ablation, and we call the canonical form
\emph{FGIB-H} ($A = I$). For regression the per-class table is replaced by fixed
random-Fourier anchors \citep{rahimi2008random} on a sphere of radius $a$; $a = 0$
recovers a shared $\Ncal(0,\tau^2 I)$ prior (Remark~\ref{rem:a0}). Three design
decisions distinguish this from CEB: the classifier consumes $h$ rather than $z$; the
two loss terms act on disjoint parameter sets; and the reference is a frozen buffer
rather than a network.

\paragraph{Theory--implementation layering.}
The analysis idealizes the side head as a linear invertible map; the implementation
uses an \texttt{nn.Linear} \emph{with bias} and no conditioning constraint --- and that
freedom is what lets the trainable head absorb the anchor fit without moving $h$
(Section~\ref{sec:limits-main}(a), measured in Section~\ref{sec:ablation}). The
canonical FGIB-H removes the gap rather than flagging it: $A = I$ is orthogonal by
construction, so the idealizations --- invertibility, conditioning, the pull-back
reference of Result~\ref{res:equiv} --- hold exactly in the implementation. A second
gap: the exact KL invariance of Result~\ref{res:equiv} holds for the underlying
Gaussians, but the diagonal family is not closed under $A^{-1}$, so within it the
identity is exact distributionally and approximate as a variational identity
(Remark~\ref{rem:closure}).

\subsection{What the frozen reference buys}

\begin{result}[The side path is a CEB bottleneck on the deployed representation]
\label{res:equiv}
Let $\dim z = \dim h$ and $A$ be invertible. Pulling back by $A^{-1}$ maps the
posterior to $q'(\tilde z|x) = \Ncal(h(x),\,A^{-1}\Sigma A^{-\top})$ --- centered at the
representation inference uses --- and the anchors to
$\Ncal(a A^{-1}v_y,\, \tau^2 (A^{\top}A)^{-1})$. The Gaussian KL is invariant under a
common invertible linear map (Lemma~\ref{lem:kl-invariance}), so the regularizer is
pointwise identical to a CEB bottleneck --- backward encoder frozen at the current side
head --- applied to $\tilde z = h + \tilde\varepsilon$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:fgib-ceb});
at the variance optimum the perturbation is $\tau\,(A^{\top}A)^{-1/2}\varepsilon$:
\emph{known, side-path-only} noise. This licenses calling the construction a bottleneck
at all: the regularizer carries a residual-information bound centered on the deployed
representation --- an interpretation a bare prototype loss lacks. The identity holds
at each fixed $A$; because a trainable $A$ moves, the pulled-back reference moves with
it. The \emph{canonical} configuration removes this channel: for a fixed orthogonal $A$,
$\|A h - a v_y\| = \|h - a\,A^{\top}v_y\|$, so the regularizer is a fixed-anchor center
loss on the deployed representation itself, with $A^{\top}A = I$ and $\kappa = 1$ at
every step; for Haar-distributed random orthogonal $A$ the rotated frame is
identically distributed to $\{v_y\}$ across seeds, so $A = I$ is a representative
canonical choice (Propositions~\ref{prop:orth},~\ref{prop:conditioning}). We call
this form \emph{FGIB-H} and measure its price across settings
(Section~\ref{sec:ablation}, Table~\ref{tab:hanchor}).
\label{res:orth}
\end{result}

\begin{result}[A deterministic main path freezes the variance: the realized KL is a fixed-anchor center loss]
\label{res:varfreeze}
With the classifier consuming $h$ (never $z$), a zero-initialized log-variance head, a
frozen prior log-variance $\ell_p = \ln\tau^2 = 0$, and a first-order optimizer without
weight decay, the posterior log-variance is \emph{identically} $\ell_q \equiv \ell_p$ at
every training step, and the regularizer is exactly
$\KLd(q\,\|\,r) = \tfrac{1}{2\tau^2}\|\mu - \mu_p\|^2$ --- the KL of
Result~\ref{res:sweep} evaluated permanently at the variance optimum, not merely in the
limit. The mechanism is exact invariance, not attraction: the per-coordinate partial
derivative $\partial_{\ell_q}\KLd = \tfrac12(e^{\ell_q-\ell_p}-1)$ vanishes at
$\ell_q = \ell_p$, so the zero-initialized head receives zero gradient at every step ---
we make no global-attractor claim for arbitrary initializations
(Proposition~\ref{prop:varfreeze}). For trainable-prior bottlenecks the released
variance is a real channel --- CEB's parametrization races toward the clamp
(Remark~\ref{rem:vib-prior}) --- and the deterministic main path eliminates it by
design. The same argument pins the posterior variance in every FGIB-family ablation:
FGIB, TAFGIB and CentFGIB are \emph{exactly} the same loss up to the trainability of
the anchor means. The one variant that escapes the family, DCEB's trainable prior
variance, is the only one that collapses at $\beta = 10$; its measured trajectory
confirms the corrected sign of Corollary~\ref{cor:family} (the prior log-variance
\emph{rises} at $\beta{=}1$, never falls), while the collapse itself occurs with the
variance near initialization --- an open, measured diagnostic
(Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).
\end{result}

\begin{result}[Every $\beta$ selects a distinct interior optimum]
\label{res:sweep}
Consider the class-symmetric aligned family --- the encoder maps class $y$ to
$h_y = s\,v_y$, the classifier is held at $w_j = c\,v_j$ with fixed scale $c$. Then
\begin{equation}
\KLd(s,\sigma^2) = \tfrac{\dim z}{2}\Big(\tfrac{\sigma^2}{\tau^2} - 1
- \ln\tfrac{\sigma^2}{\tau^2}\Big) + \tfrac{1}{2\tau^2}(s-a)^2,
\qquad
\CE(s) = \ln\!\big(1 + (K-1)e^{-cs}\big),
\label{eq:reduced}
\end{equation}
and the variance optimum is $\sigma^{2*} = \tau^2$ for \emph{every} $\beta$, because the
prediction term does not see $\sigma^2$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:lagrangian}). The Lagrangian
$L_\beta(s) = \CE(s) + \tfrac{\beta}{2\tau^2}(s-a)^2$ is then strictly convex with a
unique minimizer $s^*(\beta) > a$, strictly decreasing in $\beta$ with $s^* \to a$ as
$\beta \to \infty$, so $\beta \mapsto (\KLd(s^*), \CE(s^*))$ is one-to-one onto the
entire curve, which is strictly concave (Propositions~\ref{prop:sweep},~\ref{prop:curve}).
The mechanism is the point of freezing the reference: deterministic-IB has no usable
compression knob, repaired in \citet{kolchinsky2019caveats} by \emph{squaring} the
compression term by hand; FGIB needs no surgery, because $\KLd =
\tfrac{1}{2\tau^2}\|\mu-\mu_p\|^2 + \text{(variance terms)}$ is quadratic in the
displacement \emph{whenever $\mu_p$ does not move}: a fixed anchor turns
self-referential entropy compression into a squared distance, and the convexification
is inherited, not installed.
\end{result}

\begin{result}[A persistent force and a non-collapsed compressed endpoint]
\label{res:endpoint}
At a fixed encoder, CEB's bound is tighter in value: $\ReX(\theta^*) \le \ReX_F$, the
excess being the anchor gap $\mathbb{E}_y[\KLd(q(z|y)\|\Ncal(a v_y, \tau^2 I))] -
\mathbb{E}_y[\KLd(q(z|y)\|\Ncal(m_y,S_y))] \ge 0$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:ordering}). That excess is the price of the fixed anchor, paid
for a reason: CEB's gap can be driven to zero with the representation untouched,
whereas FGIB's can shrink only if the posterior moves toward the anchors --- the force
is persistent (Remark~\ref{rem:force}). Moreover, the $\KLd = 0$ point is
$\mu_y = a v_y$, a class-separated configuration with $\CE = \ln(1+(K{-}1)e^{-ca})$,
bounded and $\to 0$ as $a$ grows; a marginal $\Ncal(0,I)$ prior places $\KLd = 0$ at
$\mu \equiv 0$ with $\CE = \ln K$, i.e.\ chance (Remark~\ref{rem:endpoint}). This is
the prediction the experiments test most directly: pushing $\beta$ up should not
destroy an FGIB model, and $a$ is the dial controlling how benign the compressed
corner is (Tables~\ref{tab:robust} and~\ref{tab:anchor}).
\end{result}

\subsection{What the frozen reference does not buy}
\label{sec:limits-main}

We state the open items here because two of them bear on how the experiments should be
read. \emph{(a) A trainable side head is a second trainable party in the KL} and can
absorb the anchor fit without moving $h$; the canonical fixed head closes this by
construction, and the measurement shows the trainable variant's $\kappa(A^{\top}A)$
degrades to $10^{5.8}$--$10^{6.8}$ while accuracy is unaffected --- the freedom buys
nothing. \emph{(b) The sweep needs a bounded classifier scale}, which the implementation
leaves free, so Result~\ref{res:sweep} is conditional as implemented. \emph{(c) The
certificate still does not bound the deployed residual.} \emph{(d) The compressed
endpoint is designed, not certified:} the anchor corner is a label code, not semantic
compression (Remark~\ref{rem:endpoint}). \emph{(e) The sweep is over a surrogate,} the
$(\KLd_{\mathrm{anchor}},\CE)$ plane, not the information plane
(Remark~\ref{rem:surrogate}). Full statements in Appendix~\ref{app:limits}.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Setup}

\paragraph{Tasks and backbones.}
We use 9 datasets over 4 backbones, giving 12 task$\times$backbone settings: MNIST
\citep{lecun1998mnist} (MLP/CNN), ImageNet-100 \citep{deng2009imagenet} frozen
ResNet-50 \citep{he2016resnet} features (MLP/CNN), Cora \citep{sen2008collective}
(GCN \citep{kipf2017gcn}), IMDb \citep{maas2011imdb} and AG News
\citep{zhang2015character} (frozen BERT \citep{devlin2019bert} token features) with
LSTM \citep{hochreiter1997lstm} encoders; and the regressions California Housing
\citep{pace1997sparse} (MLP), STS-B \citep{cer2017semeval,wang2019glue} (GloVe
\citep{pennington2014glove} LSTM), ZINC-12k \citep{dwivedi2023benchmarking} (GCN), and
AgeDB \citep{moschoglou2017agedb} (MLP/CNN). Splits are $60/20/20$ (seed 42) except
ZINC, so every comparison is internal to the same split; a subject-disjoint AgeDB
control preserves the objective ordering (Table~\ref{tab:agedb-disjoint}).
Appendix~\ref{app:data} gives the preprocessing.

\paragraph{Objectives.}
Each backbone carries the same seven heads: the deterministic backbone, VIB
\citep{alemi2017vib}, squared-IB \citep[SVIB, penalty $\beta\KLd^2$]{kolchinsky2019caveats},
nonlinear IB \citep[NIB, non-parametric pairwise bound, learned noise
variance]{kolchinsky2019nib}, CEB \citep[diagonal-Gaussian backward
encoder]{fischer2020ceb}, supervised $\beta$-DVCCA
\citep[VIB plus an input-reconstruction decoder]{abdelaleem2025dvmib}, and FGIB. For
regression, CEB and FGIB condition their priors on the min-max-scaled continuous target.
Every variant shares the encoder, the bottleneck dimension ($\dim z = 256$) and the
optimizer. The objectives' compression terms are not dimensionally identical (NIB's
bound is in nats; DVCCA's $\beta$ also weights reconstruction), so $\beta$ is a
per-objective knob swept independently and selected on validation, never a shared axis
of equal compression.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
Adam \citep{kingma2015adam} at $10^{-3}$ ($10^{-2}$ for Cora), batch size 512 (128 for
ZINC), at most 100 epochs, early stopping with patience 10 on validation macro-AUC
(classification) or validation $R^2$ (regression); each configuration is run 5 times
with seeds $0..4$ and we report the mean over runs. The grid is
$\beta \in \{10^{-4},\dots,10\}$ for every bottleneck plus
$a \in \{1,\dots,16\}$ for FGIB: $1{,}020$ configurations, $5{,}100$ runs.
\textbf{Hyperparameters are selected on validation, never on test}: each cell reports
the test score of its best mean-validation configuration, with exact ties broken by a
second validation quantity and then fixed grid order, so no selection step consults the
test set. Per-run standard
deviations and selected hyperparameters are in Appendix~\ref{app:tables}; the ablations
of Section~\ref{sec:ablation} use the identical protocol on MNIST/MLP.

\subsection{Accuracy: FGIB ranks first, but no bottleneck wins by much}

Table~\ref{tab:main} reports the validation-selected configuration of each objective
on the seven classification settings (the five regression settings are
Table~\ref{tab:main-reg}, reported as exploratory). Its FGIB column is the
\emph{optional trainable-head} variant, selected over the full $6\times9$ grid; the
canonical fixed-head configuration (FGIB-H), selected over the same 6-point budget as
every other objective, is Table~\ref{tab:hanchor}, whose rank and win counts are
computed over the complete seven classification rows. With that said: the trainable
variant attains the best mean rank, $1.86$ of $7$, and is best or tied-best on $4$ of
$7$ settings; the runner-up is the deterministic backbone at $3.14$ and every prior
bottleneck sits above $3.5$; on the regression rows its rank is $1.60$
(Table~\ref{tab:main-reg}).

Three qualifications are essential. First, \emph{the margins are small}: excluding the
two settings where every bottleneck helps a weak backbone, no objective improves on
its backbone by more than about one point, and losses can be large even at the
validation-selected optimum (SVIB gives up $10.1$ points on Cora). Reading
Table~\ref{tab:main} as evidence that bottlenecks substantially improve
in-distribution accuracy is reading it wrong; it shows that FGIB is the only one that
is reliably not \emph{worse}. Second, \emph{FGIB's selection grid is $9\times$ larger}:
the equal-budget control (a single fixed $a=16$, post-hoc) has mean rank $2.14$ on
classification ($2.33$ over all 12), still ahead of the backbone and every prior
bottleneck --- part of the rank advantage is search budget; the ordering of methods is
not (Table~\ref{tab:anchor-ranks}: the fixed-anchor rank ranges from $2.00$ at $a=14$
to $3.17$ at $a=1$). Third, \emph{paired
statistics, with their sensitivity reported} (Table~\ref{tab:paired}): a Wilcoxon test
over the 12 setting-level paired means finds FGIB the only objective whose gain over
its backbone is significantly positive ($p=0.014$; $p=0.12$ at the equal-budget
control), and FGIB beats each prior bottleneck pairwise ($p \le 0.016$) --- but neither
subset is significant on its own (classification-only $p\approx0.22$, mean gain $+0.32$
points; regression-only $p\approx0.13$, mean $+3.05$), and excluding the two AgeDB rows
the mixed $p$ falls to $0.055$ with the mean gain falling from $+1.46$ to $+0.62$
points. The mixed $p=0.014$ pools heterogeneous metrics across 12 non-independent
settings and is exploratory only. The data therefore support \emph{non-destructiveness
on classification} and a small, unstable gain overall --- not a significant
in-distribution accuracy improvement; we take the \emph{rank and the
non-destructiveness of the knob} as the primary statistical claims.

\subsection{The knob: a fixed-protocol stress test, re-read on a scale-free axis}
\label{sec:knob}

Result~\ref{res:endpoint} predicts what happens at the \emph{other} end of the sweep: a
marginal-prior bottleneck has its $\KLd = 0$ point at $\mu \equiv 0$, so raising $\beta$
far enough must drive it to the trivial predictor; FGIB's $\KLd = 0$ point is
class-separated. Table~\ref{tab:robust} reports the stress test: for each objective we
take the \emph{worst} test score produced by any $\beta$ in the grid, and report the
deficit against the deterministic backbone of the same row --- a shared \emph{protocol},
deliberately not a shared axis of effective strength, since the compression terms are
not dimensionally identical (Section~\ref{sec:exp}). The separation is stark. VIB, CEB
and DVCCA are driven below the trivial predictor on all 7 classification settings
(median worst-case deficits $71.9$, $66.1$, $79.1$ points); SVIB is only marginally
better ($64.8$): squaring the KL does not move the corner
(Remark~\ref{rem:endpoint}). FGIB's median worst-case deficit is $0.31$ points at the
validation-selected anchor and $0.35$ at fixed $a=16$; on classification the worst case
over the six-point, five-order-of-magnitude $\beta$ grid is within $0.52$ points of the
backbone on \emph{every} one of the seven settings (per-setting values in
Table~\ref{tab:robust}), against deficits of $34$--$88$ points for VIB, CEB and DVCCA.



\paragraph{A scale-free axis of effective strength.}
Because the penalties are not dimensionally identical, we re-measure the stress test on
the axis of \emph{effective regularization strength}
$r = \beta\,\lVert\nabla_\theta R\rVert_2 / \lVert\nabla_\theta \mathrm{CE}\rVert_2$
at a shared one-epoch CE-only operating point on MNIST/MLP (Table~\ref{tab:effective};
Figure~\ref{fig:effective}). The axis exposes two confounds and one fact that survives.
\emph{(i) NIB's stability is inertia:} its bound's gradient is numerically zero across
the entire grid ($r \equiv 0$), the saturating-gradient phenomenon of
\citet[\S IV A]{kolchinsky2019nib} made quantitative. \emph{(ii) SVIB's raw-grid
collapse is a scale artifact:} its penalty $\beta\,\KLd^2$ carries different units, and
at raw $\beta = 1$ its effective strength is $r \approx 10^{6.2}$ --- two orders of
magnitude beyond any KL-nat objective's grid --- so its $38$-point deficit there does
not signal fragility relative to the others; squaring the KL rescales the grid, not the
geometry. \emph{(iii) The fact that survives:} among the KL-nat objectives, the others
collapse between $r \approx 10^{3.15}$ (CEB) and $10^{3.23}$ (VIB, DVCCA), while
FGIB($a{=}16$) reaches $r \approx 10^{3.75}$ --- $\approx 3\times$ beyond the largest
collapse threshold --- with zero deficit across the whole range: on the
effective-strength axis the raw grid pushes FGIB \emph{further} than any objective
that collapses, and it does not break.

NIB is the interesting control. It is as stable as FGIB (median deficit $0.53$), but for
a different reason: its non-parametric bound saturates with exponentially vanishing
gradient \citep[\S IV A]{kolchinsky2019nib}, so the knob barely acts --- on MNIST/MLP
its accuracy moves from $98.15$ to $98.27$ across the entire sweep. Two gain summaries
in Table~\ref{tab:robust} make the distinction: at the \emph{best} $\beta$
(test-selected, an exploratory upper bound) NIB scores $-0.27$ points, and VIB, SVIB,
CEB, DVCCA are likewise negative --- only FGIB is positive ($+0.39$; $+0.27$ at
$a=16$); at the \emph{validation-selected} configuration FGIB scores $+0.26$ against
$-0.21$ to $-0.37$ for the others. \emph{Among the six bottlenecks, FGIB is the only
one whose compression knob is both non-destructive and, at the median, worth turning.}

The exceptions are informative and we do not hide them: FGIB is destroyed (worst case
$>5$ points below the backbone) in 3 of 12 settings at $a=16$, and all three are
regression --- STS-B ($13.2$), AgeDB ($15.6$ CNN, $24.0$ MLP), with ZINC just below
threshold at $4.5$. We therefore report the regression results as \emph{exploratory}
and keep the core claims on classification; the theory anticipates this asymmetry ---
the random-Fourier anchor construction for continuous targets is a transfer argument,
not a proof (Remark~\ref{rem:caveats}).

\subsection{The anchor scale is the dial the theory says it is}

Result~\ref{res:endpoint} says when robustness should fail: the compressed corner costs
$\CE = \ln(1+(K{-}1)e^{-ca})$, small only when $ca$ is large relative to
$\ln(K{-}1)$; at $a \to 0$ the anchors merge onto a single origin and FGIB degenerates
to a VIB-style marginal prior (Remark~\ref{rem:a0}). Table~\ref{tab:anchor} holds
$\beta$ at the largest grid value and varies $a$. The prediction holds: at $a=1$ FGIB
behaves like the marginal-prior bottlenecks ($1.0\%$ on ImageNet-100/MLP, $26.6\%$ on
Cora), and growing $a$ recovers the backbone --- monotonically on ImageNet-100/MLP,
with local dips elsewhere. The trend, not the monotonicity, is what the theory predicts,
and it holds on 10 of 12 rows; the two exceptions are the two the theory says should
have none: IMDb is binary (the corner is already cheap at $a=1$), and ZINC is
regression, where the random-Fourier anchors carry no corresponding guarantee
(Remark~\ref{rem:caveats}). Consistent with the $(K{-}1)$ factor, the 100-class task
needs by far the largest anchor.

The formula does not predict the transition threshold, and we do not pretend it does:
Cora ($K{=}7$) collapses at $a \le 2$ where MNIST ($K{=}10$) does not, since the
classifier scale $c$ enters the corner value multiplicatively and is free in our
implementation (Remark~\ref{rem:c-scale}); Table~\ref{tab:anchor} establishes the
qualitative mechanism, not a quantitative fit.

\subsection{Confound ablations and diagnostics}
\label{sec:ablation}

This section separates FGIB's two design changes (deterministic main path; frozen
prior) and verifies the closure results. All runs use the main protocol, except the
400-epoch controls; the additional experiments total 710 runs (full per-variant details
in Appendix~\ref{app:ablation-detail}).

\paragraph{What the ablations show.}
VIB and CEB fall to chance at $\beta=1$ ($15.3\%$ and $26.8\%$); \emph{DCEB} --- the
deterministic path with CEB's trainable backward encoder as prior --- holds at
$\approx 98$ through $\beta=1$ and falls to $11.4\%$ only at $\beta=10$
(Table~\ref{tab:ablation}). Corollary~\ref{cor:family} locates what is measured: its
trainable prior \emph{variance} is driven \emph{up}, never down (the corrected sign,
Figure~\ref{fig:longtrain}), while its $\beta{=}10$ collapse occurs with the variance
near initialization --- the collapse mechanism remains open. Every variant that \emph{starts} from the
orthonormal anchor table stays at $\approx 98.3$ across the whole grid (TAFGIB,
CentFGIB, frozen $A$, FGIB-H) --- by Result~\ref{res:varfreeze} an identity, not a
coincidence: FGIB's KL \emph{is} the center loss exactly, and the load-bearing
ingredient is the anchor-table \emph{initialization} geometry at scale $a$, not the
frozenness of the means and not any Gaussian variance machinery, which is provably
inert. Enforcing the fixed classifier scale leaves FGIB's sweep unchanged while VIB
and CEB still collapse --- the anchor geometry, not the free classifier scale, keeps
the corner non-degenerate. A geometry ablation varies the frame itself on the same
representation: orthonormal, simplex/ETF and random-normalized anchors behave alike,
and learned centers too --- fixedness and class separation matter more than the frame
(Table~\ref{tab:geometry}).

\paragraph{The variance channel, measured.}
CEB's final posterior log-variance stays near initialization under the 100-epoch
protocol (clamp fraction $0.0$ everywhere); the 400-epoch control shows the race's
\emph{direction} --- posterior and prior drift down together, to $-2.27$/$-1.20$ nats
at $\beta{=}1/10$, still far from the $e^{-10}$ clamp: real but slow
(Figure~\ref{fig:longtrain}). The same control on VIB shows no race: the fixed prior
restores the variance, which holds at initialization while accuracy collapses through
the mean ($q \to \Ncal(0,I)$) (Remark~\ref{rem:vib-prior}). FGIB's variance is the
flat zero line --- the fixed point of Result~\ref{res:varfreeze}. DCEB confirms the
corrected sign of Corollary~\ref{cor:family}: at $\beta=1$ its prior log-variance
\emph{rises} (to $+0.12$ nats, posterior tracking, never down), while its $\beta=10$
collapse occurs with the variance near initialization --- the collapse mechanism is
therefore not the variance channel and remains an open, measured diagnostic.

\paragraph{The side head: measured conditioning, quantified closure.}
The trainable head's $\log_{10}\kappa(A^{\top}A)$ rises from $3.9$ at initialization to
$5.8$--$6.8$ (transient peaks $8.3$), while accuracy is unaffected on MNIST
(Figure~\ref{fig:diag}(a)). FGIB-H closes the channel by construction
(Result~\ref{res:orth}); Table~\ref{tab:hanchor} quantifies its price across the seven
classification settings at equal budget: at the inherited scale $a=16$ it trails the
trainable head by $\approx 0.5$ points per setting (mean rank $3.86$ of $5$), at the
re-tuned scale $a=4$ it recovers most of the gap (mean rank $2.86$, worst-case deficit
against the backbone $0.23$ points on the same seven rows). The cross-dataset reach of
the geometry itself is measured separately: CentFGIB matches FGIB($a{=}16$) at equal
budget on 10 of 12 settings (Table~\ref{tab:centfgib}); a frozen ETF classifier alone
buys no knob; and a subject-disjoint AgeDB split preserves the ordering
(Table~\ref{tab:agedb-disjoint}).

\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Variational bottlenecks.}
VIB \citep{alemi2017vib} bounds $I(X;Z)$ with a marginal prior; CEB
\citep{fischer2020ceb} bounds the residual $I(X;Z|Y)$ with a learned backward encoder;
DVCCA \citep{abdelaleem2025dvmib} adds a reconstruction term to the same family. FGIB
inherits the residual-level object and replaces the learned backward encoder with a
frozen geometric one; the resulting bound is looser by the anchor gap, and that excess
is what makes its force persistent (Proposition~\ref{prop:ordering},
Remark~\ref{rem:force}).

\paragraph{Degeneracies of the IB Lagrangian.}
\citet{kolchinsky2019caveats} prove the deterministic IB curve is piecewise linear and
repair it by squaring the compression term. Proposition~\ref{prop:dead} shows CEB
inherits the degeneracy over its entire range, and Result~\ref{res:sweep} shows a fixed
reference supplies the same strict convexity \emph{without} squaring --- while squaring
alone (SVIB) does not fix the collapse; \citet{kolchinsky2019nib}'s non-parametric
pairwise bound instead saturates (Section~\ref{sec:knob}). $I(X;Z)$ is infinite or
ill-defined for deterministic continuous encoders
\citep{amjad2020learning,kolchinsky2019nib,saxe2018information}; FGIB's response is
architectural: confine the noise --- and thus the finite bound --- to a side path
inference never reads.

\paragraph{Fixed geometric targets.}
Center loss pulls features toward learned per-class centers \citep{wen2016center};
hyperspherical prototypes are placed a priori \citep{mettes2019hpn}; the classifier
itself can be fixed with little loss \citep{hoffer2018fix}, in particular to a simplex
equiangular tight frame \citep{zhu2021geometric,pernici2019regular}; neural-collapse
analyses show such configurations arise spontaneously \citep{papyan2020prevalence}.
FGIB's corner is a neural-collapse-like geometry, but the geometry enters as the
\emph{reference of a variational bound} --- the designed zero-penalty set --- which
makes the compression term quadratic and gives it a residual-information reading rather
than serving as an auxiliary classification loss (Table~\ref{tab:related},
Appendix~\ref{app:tables}). In the released implementation the KL \emph{is} the
fixed-anchor center loss (Result~\ref{res:varfreeze}) and the canonical fixed head
moves the matching onto the deployed representation (Result~\ref{res:orth}): the
\emph{empirical} robustness is shared with fixed-prototype regularization by
construction, while the bound reading and the sweep theory are specific to the
variational form. The natural baselines are tested directly (ETF classifier, TAFGIB,
CentFGIB, geometry ablation of Table~\ref{tab:geometry}, Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).

\section{Conclusion}

The high-regularization behavior of a variational bottleneck is decided by its
zero-penalty set: VIB's marginal prior places it at collapse, CEB's learned backward
encoder leaves it underdetermined. The deeper point: the objective identifies an
information point --- the MNI point --- but never the geometry at that point
(Result~\ref{res:geomet}), so the optimizer alone settles which member of the
equivalence class is trained. FGIB designs the zero-penalty set directly: a fixed
orthonormal anchor frame makes the compression term quadratic and turns the fully
compressed corner into the prescribed class-separated configuration. Two properties
are provable: the deterministic main path pins the variance at $\tau^2$, so the
regularizer is \emph{exactly} the fixed-anchor center loss; and the canonical
fixed-orthogonal head anchors the deployed representation with $\kappa=1$ at a small
measured price. Empirically, the knob is non-destructive over a five-order-of-magnitude
$\beta$ grid on every classification setting, while accuracy is essentially the
backbone's (classification-only Wilcoxon $p\approx0.22$). The claim we stand behind is
the designed, predictive, well-conditioned representative of the MNI equivalence
class, not a better stochastic bottleneck: robustness rides on the endpoint geometry
(Table~\ref{tab:geometry}).

\section{The diagnosis in detail}
\label{app:prelim-detail}

This section states the observations of Section~\ref{sec:prelim} with the precision
the main text omits; the propositions themselves are proved in
Appendix~\ref{app:theory} (Part~I).

\paragraph{The information point is unique in the deterministic scenario.}
By the chain rule, CEB's objective is $I(X;Z) - (1+\gamma)I(Y;Z)$, and the
data-processing inequality gives
$L_\gamma(Z) \ge -\gamma I(Y;Z) \ge -\gamma H(Y)$ with equality iff
$I(X;Z) = I(Y;Z)$ and $I(Y;Z) = H(Y)$. Every $\gamma > 0$ therefore has the same exact
optimum --- the corner, which is the MNI point --- so
$\gamma \mapsto (I(X;Z^*|Y), I(Y;Z^*))$ is constant and no interior point of the IB curve
is ever a Lagrangian minimizer (Proposition~\ref{prop:dead}). Since
$\beta_{\mathrm{IB}} = 1+\gamma > 1$ always, CEB sits in the regime that
\citet{kolchinsky2019caveats} show to be degenerate over its \emph{entire} parameter
range. \citet{kolchinsky2019caveats} themselves note that if one's goal \emph{is} the
corner, this reads as $\beta$-robustness rather than failure --- which is exactly our
point: the objective identifies the information point robustly, and nothing else.
Proposition~\ref{prop:geomet} states what it does not identify: the corner is one point
in the information plane but an entire equivalence class of representations whose
Euclidean geometry (inter-class distances, scale, conditioning) is arbitrary within
the class. Which member of the class is reached is settled by the optimizer, and FGIB's
anchor frame is a designed choice of member.

\paragraph{The certificate is blind on the necessity axis (supporting).}
On the manifold $T_\alpha = B_\alpha \cdot Y$ of \citet{kolchinsky2019caveats}, $T_\alpha$
is a function of $Y$ alone, so $I(X;T_\alpha|Y) = 0$ and, at the optimal backward encoder,
$\ReX(T_\alpha) = 0$ for every $\alpha$ --- from total collapse ($\alpha = 0$, no label
information) to full copy ($\alpha = 1$). Reading $\ReX$ as a meter of distance to the MNI
point is therefore valid only along the $I(X;Z|Y)$ axis; on the necessity axis the meter
reads $0$ at chance level. With the optimal classifier the objective is
$\delta + \beta\,\ReX$ where $\delta = H(Y) - I(Y;T_\alpha)$, so it is indifferent between
giving up $\delta$ nats of label information and saving $\delta/\beta$ nats of certified
residual: in the heavy-regularization regime the whole label axis is purchasable for
$H(Y)/\beta$ nats of certified compression (Proposition~\ref{prop:flat}). Because the
full objective also contains the prediction term, this is a rate statement about the
certificate rather than a claim of literal indifference --- supporting evidence, not a
load-bearing premise.

\paragraph{The trainable-prior variance race (parametrization diagnostic).}
For the diagonal Gaussian family with a \emph{trainable} prior variance $\tau^2$, the KL
is stationary in the posterior variance at
$\sigma^2 = \tau^2$, with residual value $\|m-g\|^2/(2\tau^2)$ --- pure mean matching. The
cross-entropy term, however, is convex in $z$ for a linear classifier (the log-sum-exp of
an affine function of $z$), so by Jensen
$\mathbb{E}_z[\CE(c(z),y)] \ge \CE(c(m),y)$ and it strictly prefers $\sigma^2 \to 0$.
Along the matched line the KL \emph{resists} shrinking $\tau^2$ only while the means are
unmatched ($\partial_{\tau^2}\KLd = -\|m-g\|^2/(2\tau^4) < 0$); once $\|m-g\| \to 0$ the
race is unopposed and runs to the implementation clamp $s = e^{-10}$, at which the
certificate has become a mean-matching force of weight
$1/s \approx 2.2\times 10^{4}$ between two trainable maps
(Proposition~\ref{prop:variance}). With a \emph{fixed} prior (VIB) this channel does not
exist: the KL restores the posterior variance toward the prior value, and VIB's
high-$\beta$ failure is the mean collapse $q \to \Ncal(0,I)$
(Remark~\ref{rem:vib-prior}). Parts (ii)--(iii) of Proposition~\ref{prop:variance} are a
direction-of-force argument, not a convergence proof, and the whole channel is a
parametrization-level diagnostic, not a premise of the paper's argument. We ran the
falsification test it invites, by logging the trained log-variances
(Section~\ref{sec:ablation}): at the
early-stopping time scale of our protocol the race does not materialize --- CEB's
variances remain at their initialization $\sigma^2 = \tau^2$ with zero clamp fraction on
every $\beta$ --- so the convergence prediction of part~(iii) is empirically void under
this protocol, exactly as Remark~\ref{rem:p3-status} allows.

\paragraph{The certified object is not the deployed object (limitation).}
At the variance optimum the posterior standard deviation is class-constant, so
$Z = \mu(X) + \sqrt{S(Y)}\,\varepsilon$ is a noisy function of $\mu(X)$ given $Y$ and the
data-processing inequality gives $I(X;Z|Y) \le I(X;\mu(X)|Y)$. The gap is not a small
correction: whenever $\mu$ is continuous and not class-constant the right-hand side is
$+\infty$ \citep{amjad2020learning,kolchinsky2019nib}, so no finite quantity bounds the
deployed residual. This applies to FGIB as well (Proposition~\ref{prop:object}); what
differs is structural, not a tightness ordering. In CEB the perturbation is \emph{inside}
the inference path and is driven by the prediction term, so removing it changes the
model; in FGIB it lives on a side path that inference never reads and is controlled by
side-path parameters alone. That is a cleaner separation of regularizer from model, and
we claim nothing more for it. This limitation is orthogonal to the geometric
non-identifiability argument and carries no load in it.

\section{What the frozen reference does not buy: full statement}
\label{app:limits}

\emph{(a) The side head is a second trainable party --- unless it is fixed.} The
equivalence of
Result~\ref{res:equiv} holds \emph{at each fixed} $A$, and a \emph{trainable} $A$, unused
at
inference --- structurally the same position CEB's backward encoder occupies on the other
side of the KL. Since $h$ is linearly separable by the jointly trained classifier, much of
the anchor fit can be absorbed by $A$ alone, and nothing in the objective resists $A$
drifting toward a projector onto the $K$-dimensional between-class subspace. Full collapse
is blocked (with $A$ invertible, $\KLd = 0$ forces $h$ itself to be class-constant), but
the approach to the rank-$K$ regime is not, and that is exactly where the conditioning of
$A^{\top}A$ --- which governs the size of the side-path perturbation in
Result~\ref{res:equiv} --- degrades without bound (Remark~\ref{rem:A-channel},
Proposition~\ref{prop:conditioning}(iii)). This channel is closed by the canonical
configuration: a fixed orthogonal head has $\kappa(A^{\top}A) = 1$ by construction and
anchors $h$ directly (Result~\ref{res:orth}). The
measurement of Section~\ref{sec:ablation} quantifies how badly the \emph{optional
trainable} variant degrades on MNIST:
$\kappa(A^{\top}A)$ rises to $10^{5.8}$--$10^{6.8}$, i.e.\ severely ill-conditioned,
with predictive accuracy unaffected --- so the freedom buys nothing on MNIST; across
the classification settings the fixed head pays $\approx 0.5$ points per setting for the
closure at the inherited scale, largely recovered by re-tuning the anchor scale
(Table~\ref{tab:hanchor}) --- the accuracy-versus-certification trade-off made
explicit. FGIB-H with the fixed head is therefore \emph{the} method this paper stands
behind: it is the configuration whose results carry the closure claims, and the
trainable-head version of the main sweep (Table~\ref{tab:main}) appears as the optional
empirical variant whose conditioning is measured, not assumed.

\emph{(b) The sweep needs a bounded classifier scale.} If $c$ is free, then
$(s,c) = (a,\infty)$ sends $\KLd \to 0$ and $\CE \to 0$ simultaneously, so the infimum of
$L_\beta$ is $0$ for every $\beta$ and Result~\ref{res:sweep} has no global counterpart
--- the FGIB analogue of the dead knob, reached by growing the margin rather than by the
linearity of the IB curve. Our classifier is an unconstrained \texttt{nn.Linear} trained
by Adam without weight decay, so the proposition is conditional as implemented. A
fixed-temperature cosine classifier satisfies the assumption by construction; weight decay
only mitigates the divergence (Remark~\ref{rem:c-scale}). One consequence matters for
Section~\ref{sec:exp}: since $s^*$ drifts toward $a$ as $c$ grows, part of any observed
$\beta$-effect under early stopping is plausibly an early-stopping effect, and we have not
separated the two.

\emph{(c) The certificate still does not bound the deployed residual.} The
certificate-object mismatch applies to FGIB unchanged
(Proposition~\ref{prop:object}(b)). What FGIB buys is that the perturbation is
side-path-only and its covariance is a known model parameter --- a cleaner separation of
regularizer from model, not a tighter or more valid bound (Remark~\ref{rem:senses}).

\emph{(d) The compressed endpoint is designed, not certified.} Caveat~2 of
\citet{kolchinsky2019caveats} identifies as uninteresting those variables that compress by
mixing a constant map with a copy of $Y$; FGIB's compressed corner $\mu_y = a v_y$ is a
deterministic function of $Y$ alone, i.e.\ a label code of exactly that kind. The
orthonormal frame is in fact \emph{opposed} to the semantic compression Caveat~2 holds up
as interesting: it places every pair of classes at the same distance and can never express
``merge these two classes, keep both far from that one''. What the anchors do is choose
\emph{one specific} label-code geometry --- a neural-collapse-like configuration
\citep{papyan2020prevalence} --- instead of leaving it to the optimizer, and refuse the
chance-level corner. Any claim that FGIB compresses \emph{input} semantics at that
endpoint is the claim Caveat~2 rules out (Remark~\ref{rem:endpoint}).

\emph{(e) The sweep is over a surrogate.} Result~\ref{res:sweep} concerns the
$(\KLd_{\mathrm{anchor}}, \CE)$ plane on the aligned family, not the information plane
$(I(X;T), I(Y;T))$. On that family the posterior is class-constant, so $I(X;Z|Y) = 0$ for
every $s$; what varies along the sweep is anchor mismatch and classifier margin, not a
proved compression of mutual information (Remark~\ref{rem:surrogate}).

\section{Confound ablations: full detail}
\label{app:ablation-detail}

\paragraph{What FGIB's robustness is made of.}
FGIB differs from the comparison in two coupled ways: its classifier consumes the
deterministic $h$ at training and evaluation (which also removes the trainable-prior
variance channel of Section~\ref{sec:prelim}), and its prior is frozen (the zero-penalty
set is designed). Table~\ref{tab:ablation} separates the contributions. VIB and CEB fall
to chance at $\beta=1$ ($15.3\%$ and $26.8\%$); \emph{DCEB} --- the deterministic path
of FGIB with CEB's trainable backward encoder as its prior --- holds at $\approx 98$
through $\beta=1$ and falls to $11.4\%$ only at $\beta=10$.
Corollary~\ref{cor:family} locates \emph{why} DCEB is the odd one out, in the corrected
direction: its trainable prior \emph{variance} is driven \emph{up}
($\partial_{\ell_p}\KLd < 0$ at the initialized point), and the variance-inflation
escape dilutes the anchor force $e^{-\ell_p}(\mu-\mu_p)$ --- the regularizer dies rather
than amplifies; the trajectory is measured in Figure~\ref{fig:longtrain}. By contrast, every
variant that \emph{starts} from the orthonormal anchor table stays at
$\approx 98.3$ across the entire grid: \emph{TAFGIB} (trainable anchor means),
\emph{CentFGIB} (KL replaced by the plain center loss $\tfrac12\|\mu - a v_y\|^2$),
FGIB with $A$ frozen after random initialization, and \emph{FGIB-H} with $A{=}I$. The
equivalence is not a numerical coincidence: by
Result~\ref{res:varfreeze} the zero-initialized variance head receives zero gradient at
every step, so FGIB's KL \emph{is} the center loss exactly, and CentFGIB, TAFGIB and
FGIB-H are the same objective up to the trainability of the anchor means. The trainable
anchors of TAFGIB do move (final column norms shrink from $16$ to
$5.7$--$10$, pairwise correlations reach $0.78$ at the smallest
$\beta$), yet the accuracy curve is untouched --- the
protective property is the anchor-table \emph{initialization} geometry at scale $a$,
not the orthonormality of the trained table. Two conclusions follow. First,
the load-bearing ingredient on MNIST is that initialization geometry ---
orthonormal per-class directions at scale $a$ --- not the frozenness of its means, and
not any Gaussian variance machinery, which is provably inert here
(Result~\ref{res:varfreeze}). Second, freezing the means and keeping the KL
is what the \emph{theory} buys --- the persistent force of Remark~\ref{rem:force}, the
convexified sweep of Proposition~\ref{prop:sweep}, the
residual-information reading of Result~\ref{res:equiv} --- so FGIB retains an exact
variational upper-bound interpretation for the auxiliary noisy code; its predictive
robustness and endpoint behavior outside the analyzed family remain empirical.
Constraining the side head changes nothing in the
$\beta$-profile ($98.2$--$98.4$ across the grid for frozen $A$ and $A{=}I$): the
$A$-channel degrades $\kappa(A^{\top}A)$ but does not carry the robustness.

\paragraph{The trainable-prior variance race does not materialize at the protocol horizon.}
We ran the falsification test that Proposition~\ref{prop:variance} invites, logging the
mean posterior log-variance, the clamp fraction ($e^{-10}$), and (for CEB/DCEB) the
prior log-variance at every epoch of every run, aggregated over the \emph{entire}
validation set, not a single batch. At the final epoch, averaged over the 5 seeds, CEB's
posterior log-variance sits at $+0.37, +0.27, +0.13, +0.08, -0.05, -0.01$ (nats) for
$\beta = 10^{-4}, \dots, 10$ --- a mild monotone drift with $\beta$, nowhere near the
clamp --- and the clamp fraction is $0.0$ at \emph{every} epoch of
every run; CEB's prior tracks its posterior, and VIB's posterior stays within $0.37$
of zero (Figure~\ref{fig:diag}(b)). To probe whether the race is an early-stopping
artifact, we retrained CEB, DCEB, VIB and FGIB at
$\beta \in \{1, 10\}$ for 400 epochs with early stopping disabled. The answer is
nuanced and we report it as measured. CEB's variance \emph{does} drift, monotonically
and in exactly the direction Proposition~\ref{prop:variance}(i)--(ii) predicts --- at
$\beta=1$ the posterior log-variance ends at $-2.27$ nats ($-1.20$ at $\beta=10$; the
prior tracks the posterior), passing $-0.92$ at epoch 100 --- but the clamp fraction is
$0.0$ throughout, and $-2.3$ nats ($\sigma^2 \approx 0.10$) is still far from the
$e^{-10}$ clamp (Figure~\ref{fig:longtrain}): the race is \emph{real but slow}, and its
convergence time-scale is beyond 400 epochs at these hyperparameters --- the
conditional status Remark~\ref{rem:p3-status} assigns to
Proposition~\ref{prop:variance}(iii). VIB shows no race at all: its fixed prior
restores the posterior variance, whose log-variance holds at initialization while the
accuracy collapses through the mean ($q \to \Ncal(0,I)$) --- predictive-information
loss, not a clamp race (Remark~\ref{rem:vib-prior}). DCEB confirms the corrected sign
of Corollary~\ref{cor:family}: its prior log-variance rises at $\beta=1$ ($+0.12$ nats,
posterior tracking, never down), while its $\beta=10$ collapse occurs with the variance
near initialization --- the collapse mechanism is not the variance channel and remains
an open, measured diagnostic. For FGIB the question is
settled analytically: by Result~\ref{res:varfreeze} the
posterior variance is pinned at $\tau^2$ at every step --- the flat zero line of
Figure~\ref{fig:longtrain} is the fixed point itself, held for 400 epochs.

\paragraph{The side-head conditioning channel.}
Section~\ref{sec:limits-main}(a) identifies the \emph{trainable} side head $A$ as the
channel the optional variant leaves open, and predicts that the approach to a rank-$K$
projector would show
up as diverging $\kappa(A^{\top}A)$. Figure~\ref{fig:diag}(a) plots
$\log_{10}\kappa(A^{\top}A)$ over training, at $a=16$, for every $\beta$: from its
initialization near $3.9$ the condition number rises to $10^{5.8}$--$10^{6.8}$ at
the final epoch, with transient peaks to $10^{8.3}$ --- $A^{\top}A$ becomes
\emph{severely ill-conditioned} ($\approx 1000\times$ degradation, no monotone
dependence on $\beta$). Predictive accuracy nevertheless remains stable, so the
conclusion is narrow: the ill-conditioning does not hurt MNIST predictions in this
experiment, but it means the pulled-back reference of Result~\ref{res:equiv} is not
close to a well-conditioned frame, and the KL's correspondence with $h$ is not
guaranteed by anything we measured for the trainable variant. The canonical
configuration needs no such caveat: $A=I$ has $\kappa = 1$ at every step and anchors
$h$ directly (Result~\ref{res:orth}). The $A{=}I$ and frozen-$A$ rows of
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} show what the degradation costs in accuracy (little on
MNIST), and Table~\ref{tab:hanchor} quantifies the fixed head's price across the seven
classification settings: $\approx 0.5$ points per setting at the inherited anchor
scale, largely recovered by re-tuning the scale --- a real but bounded margin.

\paragraph{Fixed classifier scale.}
Proposition~\ref{prop:sweep} requires a fixed classifier scale $c$, which the default
\texttt{nn.Linear} does not enforce. The cosine rows of Table~\ref{tab:ablation}
enforce it by construction: FGIB's sweep is unchanged ($97.9$--$98.3$ across all six
$\beta$ values), while VIB and CEB are not rescued ($51.9\%$ at $\beta=1$ and $41.6\%$
at $\beta=0.1$ respectively), exactly as Remark~\ref{rem:c-scale} predicts --- fixed
$c$ does not repair the trainable-prior corners; the anchor geometry, not the free
classifier scale, keeps FGIB's corner non-degenerate (Appendix~\ref{app:tables},
cosine rows).

\paragraph{Does the geometry alone generalize?}
Result~\ref{res:varfreeze} proves the KL machinery is \emph{never} load-bearing in this
architecture, on any dataset. Table~\ref{tab:centfgib} asks the cross-dataset version:
CentFGIB run on all 12 settings under the
equal-budget protocol ($a=16$, 6-point $\beta$ grid, 5 seeds). The
geometry alone generalizes: at the validation-selected configuration, CentFGIB matches
FGIB($a{=}16$) to within $0.01$ points on 10 of 12 settings and to within $0.43$ on
the remaining two, including all four regression settings with RFF anchors; in a
nine-entry ranking the FGIB family occupies the top three slots. The empirical engine
of the method is the fixed anchor geometry
itself; the KL contributes the bound reading and the sweep theory
(Appendix~\ref{app:tables}).

\paragraph{Does the side head need to exist at all?}
The canonical configuration FGIB-H ($A{=}I$) removes the side head from the theory's
concerns entirely: the anchor matching acts directly on the deployed representation
(Result~\ref{res:orth}). Table~\ref{tab:hanchor} measures the price across the complete
seven classification settings at equal budget, with rank and win counts computed over
the same seven complete rows. At $a=16$, the scale inherited from the $z$-space sweep,
the fixed head trails the trainable one by $\approx 0.5$ points per setting (mean rank
$3.86$ of $5$; FGIB $1.57$, CentFGIB $1.71$) --- the trainable head is doing real
adaptive work, and part of that work is rescaling the effective geometry. Re-tuning the
anchor scale for $h$-space recovers most of the gap: at $a=4$ FGIB-H takes mean rank
$2.86$ of $5$, ahead of the backbone ($3.29$), with worst-case deficit against the
backbone of $0.23$ points and best-or-tied finishes on $2$ of $7$ rows (MNIST/CNN
$99.29$, best of the table; Cora $87.68$, tied-best) --- so the price of the $\kappa=1$
closure is small and quantifiable, an accuracy-versus-certification choice whose two
sides both ship in the released code. We also test the other fixed geometry the class
invites: a backbone with a \emph{frozen ETF classifier} (equiangular tight frame head,
no regularizer) scores $98.32\%$ on MNIST/MLP against the backbone's $98.33\%$ --- a
fixed classifier alone buys neither margin nor a knob, so the anchor regularization on
the representation is the load-bearing ingredient (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}, ETF row).

\paragraph{Which frame? The geometry ablation.}
Result~\ref{res:orth} fixes the head but not the frame: any fixed, class-separated,
well-conditioned frame would close the same channel, and the fixed-prototype literature
offers several (orthonormal, simplex/ETF, learned centers). Table~\ref{tab:geometry}
varies the frame on the \emph{same} representation --- MNIST/MLP with $A{=}I$, $a=16$,
the same 6-point $\beta$ grid, the regularizer being either the KL or the plain MSE:
orthonormal, simplex/ETF (unit norms, mutual cosine $-1/(K-1)$) and random-normalized
anchors behave alike across the grid, and learned centers (TAFGIB) too --- fixedness and
class separation matter more than the particular frame. A frozen ETF \emph{classifier}
is not a member of this family (it regularizes nothing), which is why the ETF row of
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} buys no knob.

\paragraph{AgeDB without identity leakage.}
The image-level AgeDB split of the main sweep leaks identity across the boundary. To
check that the leak inflates magnitudes rather than creates the pattern, we reran
AgeDB/CNN under a subject-disjoint split: the ordering is preserved --- the backbone
scores $21.37$ against $35.10$ for FGIB and CentFGIB and $33.71$ for FGIB-H (all
$R^2\times100$, validation-selected $\beta$) --- so the leakage does not manufacture
the regression claim (Table~\ref{tab:agedb-disjoint},
Appendix~\ref{app:tables}).

\paragraph{Anchor-scale sensitivity of the equal-budget rank.}
The $a{=}16$ equal-budget control is a post-hoc choice; Table~\ref{tab:anchor-ranks}
reports the fixed-anchor rank for every $a$ in the grid: the mean rank over the 12
settings ranges from $2.00$ ($a{=}14$, best or tied-best on $7$) to $3.17$ ($a{=}1$),
with $a{=}16$ at $2.33$ --- the edge is not an artifact of the chosen anchor, and any
$a \ge 8$ beats every bottleneck's rank. The remaining tables and diagnostic figures
of this section are collected in Appendix~\ref{app:tables}.

\section{What we have not measured: full statement}
\label{app:notyet}

\emph{(i) Separating the
$\beta$-effect from the early-stopping effect.} Remark~\ref{rem:c-scale}(b) predicts that
the $\beta$-dependence of the equilibrium decays with training length; our runs use
patience 10, so training well past saturation and comparing converged KL across $\beta$
would test it. The cosine-classifier runs of Section~\ref{sec:ablation} enforce the
fixed-$c$ assumption of Proposition~\ref{prop:sweep} but do not remove early stopping,
and the 400-epoch control of that section covers the variance trajectory, not the
sweep itself.
\emph{(ii) Equal budget for the other methods.} The $a=16$ column of
Table~\ref{tab:main-std} gives FGIB the same 6-point budget as the comparison;
Table~\ref{tab:anchor-ranks} shows the rank is stable across fixed anchor scales, not
only at $a=16$. The
converse --- giving VIB/CEB/DVCCA an extra temperature or variance hyperparameter
dimension --- is not run, so the comparison's rank ordering is only shown to survive
budget restriction for FGIB, not budget expansion for the others. \emph{(iii) The
remaining protocol asks.} All results come from one fixed $60/20/20$ split per dataset
(seed 42), so split-sensitivity is unmeasured --- the subject-disjoint AgeDB control
measures leakage sensitivity for one dataset, not split noise in general. Downstream
robustness properties claimed for conditional bottlenecks elsewhere (adversarial
robustness, OoD detection, calibration) are not evaluated here and carry no weight in
this paper's argument; the label-free anchor-distance score
$\min_y \KLd(q(z|x)\|\Ncal(a v_y, \tau^2 I))$ that FGIB's geometry makes available at
inference (Remark~\ref{rem:training-role}) is a hypothesis, not a result.

\section{Datasets and preprocessing}
\label{app:data}

All splits use seed 42. Except where noted, each dataset is divided $60/20/20$ into
train/validation/test; classification splits are stratified, regression splits are not.
Targets for regression are min-max scaled to $[0,1]$ with a scaler fitted on the training
split only, and MAE is reported in original units via the inverse transform.

\paragraph{MNIST} \citep{lecun1998mnist}: 70{,}000 $28\times28$ grayscale digits; the
60{,}000-image training partition is split into 50{,}000 train / 10{,}000 validation, the
official 10{,}000-image test set is untouched. Inputs are normalized to $[0,1]$.

\paragraph{ImageNet-100} \citep{deng2009imagenet}: frozen ResNet-50 \citep{he2016resnet}
layer-3 features. Two variants are used: global average pooling (1024-d, the MLP
backbone) and $4\times4$ spatial pooling ($1024\times4\times4$, the CNN backbone).
Features are split $60/20/20$ (stratified) and standardized with a StandardScaler fitted
on the training split.

\paragraph{Cora} \citep{sen2008collective}: the standard transductive node-classification
graph (2708 nodes, 1433-d features, 7 classes) with a fixed random split (seed 42) into
train/validation/test node masks; the adjacency matrix is symmetrized, self-loops are
added, and the normalized Laplacian-style $\tilde D^{-1/2}(\tilde A)\tilde D^{-1/2}$ is
used. Training is full-graph (transductive), following standard GCN practice
\citep{kipf2017gcn}; this is not the fixed Planetoid split, so absolute numbers are not
directly comparable with published Planetoid numbers --- all comparisons in this paper
are internal to the same split.

\paragraph{IMDb} \citep{maas2011imdb}: the official 50{,}000 labeled reviews (25k train +
25k test) are merged and re-divided $60/20/20$ (stratified) into 30k/10k/10k. HTML tags
are stripped, tokens are alphabetic words from a training-split vocabulary (approx.\
72k words at length cap 250), and sequences are truncated or padded to $250$ tokens.
Official-split literature numbers are therefore not directly comparable; again, all
comparisons are internal.

\paragraph{AG News} \citep{zhang2015character}: frozen bert-base-uncased
\citep{devlin2019bert} token features. We use the second-to-last hidden layer (layer 10)
of each token, PCA-reduced to 50 dimensions (the PCA fit on training-split tokens only),
sequences capped at 64 tokens; $60/20/20$ stratified split over 127{,}600 documents.

\paragraph{California Housing} \citep{pace1997sparse}: 20{,}640 samples, 8 features,
$60/20/20$ split (regression, unstratified); features standardized on the training split,
target min-max scaled.

\paragraph{STS-B} \citep{cer2017semeval,wang2019glue}: the official GLUE train and dev
partitions (7{,}249 labeled sentence pairs, similarity $0$--$5$) are merged and
re-divided $60/20/20$ into 4{,}349/1{,}450/1{,}450 (the GLUE test partition carries no
labels and is unused). Each sentence is stripped of HTML, tokenized alphabetically, and
truncated to $124$ tokens; the pair is joined by a [SEP] token into a single sequence of
length $\le 250$. The vocabulary is built from the training split (approx.\ 10k words).
Embeddings are GloVe-6B-100d \citep{pennington2014glove}, initialized and frozen;
the LSTM has a single 256-d hidden layer and max-pooling readout.

\paragraph{ZINC-12k} \citep{dwivedi2023benchmarking}: the official
benchmarking-gnns split (10{,}000 train / 1{,}000 validation / 1{,}000 test). Node
features are 28-d one-hot atom types (bond types are unused, matching the benchmarking
GCN baseline); the adjacency is symmetrized, self-loops added, and degree-normalized as
for Cora; batching uses block-diagonal adjacency (batch size 128). The target is the
penalized logP, min-max scaled on the training split; MAE is reported in original units
(our GCN baseline reproduces the literature value to within the expected margin).

\paragraph{AgeDB} \citep{moschoglou2017agedb}: the official full release, 16{,}488
images of 567 subjects aged 0--96, resized to RGB $64\times64$ and normalized with
ImageNet statistics; target age min-max scaled on the training split. \textbf{Split
caveat}: the default split is image-level $60/20/20$, under which $562$ of $567$ subjects
have images on both sides of the train/test boundary --- identity-specific age cues can
leak, and the AgeDB numbers should be read with that in mind. A subject-disjoint split
(grouping all images of a subject into one partition, then splitting subjects
$60/20/20$ with seed 42) is implemented in the released code and \emph{is run} as a
control in this paper: under it the objective ordering is preserved
(Table~\ref{tab:agedb-disjoint}), so the leak inflates AgeDB's magnitudes but does not
create the pattern.
