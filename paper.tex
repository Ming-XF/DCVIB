\begin{abstract}
Variational information bottlenecks penalize a representation's KL divergence to a
prior, and it is the prior that decides their high-regularization behavior: the fixed
marginal prior of VIB places the zero-penalty point at an uninformative collapsed
distribution, while the learned backward encoder of CEB leaves the zero-penalty set
underdetermined, so the optimizer --- not the objective --- selects the final label
geometry; moreover the object the bound certifies is the noisy training code, not the
deterministic representation inference consumes. We take the zero-penalty set as the
design object and propose the Fixed-Geometry Information Bottleneck (FGIB), which keeps
the inference path deterministic and replaces the prior with a \emph{frozen}
label-conditional geometry --- an orthonormal anchor frame for classification, fixed
random-Fourier anchors for regression --- reached through a side head that inference
never reads. Because the reference cannot move, the compression term becomes a squared
distance: at fixed classifier scale, on the class-symmetric aligned family, every
$\beta$ selects a distinct interior optimum whose fully compressed corner is the
class-separated anchor configuration, with cross-entropy
$\ln(1 + (K{-}1)e^{-ca})$ rather than the chance-level $\ln K$ of a marginal prior.
Two further properties follow by construction, and we prove them rather than leave them
to observation. First, a deterministic main path \emph{freezes the variance}: the KL's
gradient in the log-variance vanishes exactly at $\sigma^2=\tau^2$, so the zero-initialized
variance head never moves and the regularizer is \emph{identically} the fixed-variance
displacement term $\tfrac{1}{2\tau^2}\|\mu-a v_y\|^2$ at every training step --- releasing
the variance is precisely what makes VIB and CEB race to the clamp, and FGIB eliminates
the race by design. Second, in its canonical configuration the side head is a
\emph{fixed orthogonal} map ($\mu = h$ up to an isometry): the anchor matching then acts
directly on the deployed representation with $\kappa(A^{\top}A)=1$ and unit-covariance
pull-back noise, closing the conditioning channel a trainable head leaves open.
Across 9 datasets, 4 backbones and 5{,}100 training runs, under our grid and protocol
FGIB attains the best mean rank among seven objectives --- mean rank $1.86$ on the seven
classification settings ($1.70$ excluding AgeDB, best or tied-best on $6$ of those
$10$), $1.60$ on the five regression settings, and first also under the
equal-budget control (mean rank $2.33$ over all 12, a single globally fixed anchor
scale) --- and
its $\beta$ knob can be swept over a six-point grid spanning five orders of magnitude
without destroying the model on classification, where VIB, CEB and DVCCA fall to the
trivial predictor. A 400-epoch control finds CEB's variance drifting exactly in the
race's predicted direction while FGIB's stays pinned at $\tau^2$; a subject-disjoint
AgeDB split preserves the regression ordering; and the fixed-orthogonal head carries a
small measured price across settings, which we quantify rather than hide. The KL's role
is an exact variational upper-bound
interpretation for the auxiliary noisy code, not a certificate of the deployed
representation's compression. The guarantee
weakens on regression, where the continuous-anchor construction is an exploratory
transfer rather than a proof: we keep an explicit ledger of what is proved and what is open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

An information bottleneck asks a representation $Z$ of an input $X$ to be maximally
informative about a target $Y$ and minimally informative about everything else
\citep{tishby1999ib}. The deep variational instantiation
\citep{alemi2017vib} replaces the intractable $I(X;Z)$ with
$\mathbb{E}\,\KLd(q(z|x)\,\|\,m(z))$ and minimizes $\CE + \beta\,\KLd$; the Conditional
Entropy Bottleneck \citep{fischer2020ceb} sharpens the target to the \emph{residual}
$I(X;Z|Y)$ with a learned backward encoder $b(z|y)$. The programme has an appealing
shape: a single knob $\beta$ should trace a trade-off curve, and the value of the KL
should serve as a meter reporting how much unnecessary information the model still
carries.

Two properties of that construction are usually left implicit, and they interact badly.
First, \emph{where the zero-penalty set lives}: the KL vanishes exactly on the prior, so
the prior chooses the set of representations the regularizer never penalizes --- a
marginal prior places it at collapse, a learned conditional prior leaves it an
underdetermined family, and the optimizer, not the objective, selects the final label
geometry. Second, \emph{the object being regularized is not the object being deployed}:
the classifier is trained on a sampled code $z \sim q(z|x)$ and evaluated on the
posterior mean $\mu(x)$ \citep[App.~A.1]{fischer2020ceb}, so the bound constrains a
noisy variable that inference never sees.

This paper starts from a diagnosis of what those two properties cost, and asks what
changes if the prior's geometry is fixed by design rather than left to move or to vanish.

\paragraph{The diagnosis.}
We isolate three failure channels of the conditional bottleneck and one structural
mismatch; all are stated and proved in Appendix~\ref{app:theory}, and
Section~\ref{sec:prelim} summarizes them. The first two are properties of the objective,
not of who is trainable: they hold at the level of the information plane before any
optimizer runs.
\emph{(P1) The knob is dead:} in the deterministic scenario every $\gamma>0$ of CEB has
the \emph{same} exact minimizer, the copy corner (Proposition~\ref{prop:dead}), so the
knob cannot be reading off a position on the IB curve. \emph{(P2) The certificate is
blind on the necessity axis:} the residual certificate $\ReX$ is identically $0$ on the
collapse-to-copy manifold, and the objective trades one nat of label information for
$1/\beta$ nats of certified compression (Proposition~\ref{prop:flat}). \emph{(P3) The
variances race --- in the gradient, and slowly in our longest run:} with trainable
variances
the KL is stationary at $\sigma^2=\tau^2$ while the cross-entropy pushes $\sigma^2$ down
without opposition once the means match, so the predicted equilibrium sits at the clamp
(Proposition~\ref{prop:variance}); the prediction is directly testable and we ran the
test --- on the full validation set of every epoch, out to a 400-epoch control. Under
the 100-epoch protocol the race is invisible; at 400 epochs CEB's variance drifts
monotonically in exactly the predicted direction ($-2.3$ nats at $\beta=1$), still far
from the clamp --- real but slow (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}). For FGIB
the question is not empirical at all: a deterministic main path makes the KL's
log-variance gradient vanish exactly at $\sigma^2=\tau^2$, pinning the variance there
for the whole run, so the regularizer is \emph{identically} the fixed-variance
displacement term $\tfrac{1}{2\tau^2}\|\mu-a v_y\|^2$ --- the race is eliminated by
design (Proposition~\ref{prop:varfreeze}), and its only re-entry point, a trainable
prior variance, is the one FGIB-family variant that collapses
(Corollary~\ref{cor:family}).
\emph{(P4) The certified object is the wrong one:} no finite certificate bounds the
residual of a deployed deterministic representation --- CEB's or ours
(Proposition~\ref{prop:object}).

\paragraph{The method.}
FGIB (Section~\ref{sec:method}) makes three changes that follow from the diagnosis:
(i) the inference path is deterministic --- the classifier consumes the encoder output
$h$ at training \emph{and} at evaluation; (ii) the stochastic bottleneck moves to a
\emph{side path} ($\mu(x) = Ah(x)$ plus a log-variance head) that no inference path ever
reads, keeping the variational bound finite without putting noise in front of the
classifier; (iii) the label-conditional prior is \emph{fixed by design},
$r(z|y) = \Ncal(a v_y, \tau^2 I)$ with an orthonormal anchor frame, so the zero-penalty
set is the prescribed class-separated configuration rather than collapse or an
underdetermined family (regression: fixed random-Fourier anchors on a sphere of radius
$a$).

Freezing the reference is what buys the theory. Against a fixed $\mu_p$, the KL's
displacement term $\tfrac{1}{2\tau^2}\|\mu - \mu_p\|^2$ is \emph{quadratic} in the
alignment scalar --- the convexification squared-IB
\citep{kolchinsky2019caveats} installs by hand is built in --- and at fixed classifier
scale every $\beta$ has a unique interior optimum on a strictly concave trade-off curve
(Propositions~\ref{prop:sweep} and~\ref{prop:curve}). Equally important, the KL now
vanishes at the class-separated configuration $\mu_y = a v_y$, with cross-entropy
$\ln(1 + (K{-}1)e^{-ca}) \to 0$ as $a$ grows, where a marginal $\Ncal(0,I)$ prior
places $\KLd = 0$ at $\mu \equiv 0$ with cross-entropy $\ln K$ --- chance
(Remark~\ref{rem:endpoint}). \emph{The degenerate corner
is excluded by geometry rather than by the optimizer's good behavior.}

\paragraph{The evidence.}
We evaluate on 9 datasets over MLP, CNN, GCN and LSTM backbones (12
task$\times$backbone settings) against six objectives: the deterministic backbone, VIB
\citep{alemi2017vib}, squared-IB
\citep{kolchinsky2019caveats}, nonlinear IB \citep{kolchinsky2019nib}, CEB
\citep{fischer2020ceb} and supervised $\beta$-DVCCA \citep{abdelaleem2025dvmib}. The
sweep is $1{,}020$ configurations $\times$ 5 seeds $=5{,}100$ training runs, with every
hyperparameter selected on validation. Three findings, in Section~\ref{sec:exp}:
\begin{itemize}
\item FGIB attains the best mean rank ($1.75$ of $7$) and is best or tied-best on $7$ of
$12$ settings; the next best entry is the deterministic backbone at $3.42$, and all five
prior bottlenecks fall between $3.75$ and $5.08$ (Table~\ref{tab:main}). The rank
decomposes as $1.86$ on the seven classification settings, $1.60$ on the five
regression settings, and $1.70$ (best or tied-best on $6$ of $10$) excluding the two
AgeDB rows, whose image-level split leaks identity across the boundary
(Appendix~\ref{app:data}). Outside the two
settings with a weak backbone, no bottleneck --- FGIB included --- improves accuracy by
more than about a point: the headline number is a rank, not a margin.
\item Sweeping $\beta$ over a six-point grid spanning five orders of magnitude leaves FGIB within $0.52$ accuracy points of its
backbone on \emph{every} classification setting, at a single globally fixed anchor scale,
while VIB, CEB and DVCCA fall to the trivial predictor on all seven (Table~\ref{tab:robust}). NIB is equally stable but largely inert:
its median gain at its \emph{best} $\beta$ is $-0.27$ points, so at the median its knob
buys nothing. FGIB is the only objective in the study with both a non-destructive and a
positive-median-gain knob --- the former robust, the latter task-set-sensitive
(Section~\ref{sec:exp}).
\item The mechanism is the one the theory names. At maximum compression pressure the
anchor scale $a$ controls whether the model survives: at $a=1$ --- where the anchors have
nearly collapsed onto the single origin of a VIB prior (Remark~\ref{rem:a0}) --- FGIB
falls to chance on the 100-class task ($1.0\%$) and on Cora ($26.6\%$), and climbs back to
$83.0\%$ and $87.2\%$ as $a$ grows (Table~\ref{tab:anchor}). The 100-class task needs the
largest anchor, as the $(K{-}1)$ factor in the corner value predicts.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What we do not claim.}
Three of the failure channels are addressed by FGIB's construction; the object mismatch
is not, and one of the closures is conditional. In its \emph{canonical} configuration the
side head is a fixed orthogonal map, anchoring the deployed representation directly
with $\kappa(A^{\top}A)=1$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:orth}); a trainable side head is an
\emph{optional} variant (Remark~\ref{rem:A-channel}; its conditioning cost is measured in
Section~\ref{sec:ablation}, and its price for the closure is quantified in
Table~\ref{tab:hanchor}) --- only the fixed-orthogonal configuration is covered by the
closure claim. The one-to-one sweep
requires a bounded classifier scale
that the present implementation does not enforce (Remark~\ref{rem:c-scale}; enforced
variants are tested in Section~\ref{sec:ablation}); no
finite certificate bounds the deployed deterministic residual, ours included
(Proposition~\ref{prop:object}); and the sweep is a statement about a KL--CE surrogate
curve on a class-symmetric family, not a recovered IB curve
(Remark~\ref{rem:surrogate}). The robustness of the regularizer is carried by
the anchor geometry rather than by the KL machinery or the frozenness of the anchor
means (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}); what the KL adds is the residual-information
reading for the auxiliary code and the sweep theory, not a measured accuracy margin,
and its realized objective is exactly a fixed-anchor center loss --- an identity, not an
approximation (Proposition~\ref{prop:varfreeze}).
Appendix~\ref{app:theory} keeps a complete ledger of what is proved and what is open.

\section{Preliminaries: three failure channels and an object mismatch}
\label{sec:prelim}

$X$ is the input, $Y$ the label with $K$ classes, and an encoder induces the Markov chain
$Y - X - Z$; throughout we use the loss convention $\CE + \beta\,\KLd$ ($\beta = 1/\gamma$,
$\beta_{\mathrm{IB}} = 1 + 1/\beta$). The variational objects
$R = \mathbb{E}[\KLd(q(z|x)\|m(z))] \ge I(X;Z)$ (VIB's rate),
$\ReX = \mathbb{E}[\KLd(q(z|x)\|b_\theta(z|y))] \ge I(X;Z|Y)$ (CEB's residual, trainable
$b_\theta$), and $\ReX_F$ with a \emph{fixed} anchor $r$ are instances of the gap
identity $\mathbb{E}[\KLd(q(z|x)\|r(z|y))] = I(X;Z|Y)
+ \mathbb{E}_y[\KLd(q(z|y)\|r(z|y))]$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:gap}): a bound plus a slack, and
everything below turns on \emph{who is allowed to move that slack to zero} --- CEB's
backward encoder can do it with the encoder held fixed, so its bound can be tight while
the representation is entirely uncompressed. Four channels follow (proved in
Appendix~\ref{app:theory}, stated in detail in Appendix~\ref{app:prelim-detail}):
\emph{(P1) The knob is dead:} in the deterministic scenario every $\gamma>0$ of CEB has
the same exact minimizer, the copy corner, so the Lagrangian never sweeps the IB curve
(Proposition~\ref{prop:dead}). \emph{(P2) The certificate is blind on the necessity
axis:} $\ReX$ is identically $0$ on the collapse-to-copy manifold $T_\alpha$ from total
collapse to full copy, and the objective trades one nat of label information for
$1/\beta$ nats of certified compression (Proposition~\ref{prop:flat}). \emph{(P3) The
variances race:} the KL is stationary in $\sigma^2$ at $\sigma^2=\tau^2$ while the
cross-entropy strictly prefers $\sigma^2 \to 0$, so once the means match the race runs
to the clamp unopposed, degenerating the certificate into a mean-matching force of
weight $e^{10}$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:variance}); the prediction is a
direction-of-force argument. Measured on the full validation set, the race is invisible
under the 100-epoch protocol but its direction shows up clearly by 400 epochs
(Section~\ref{sec:ablation}, Remark~\ref{rem:p3-status}). \emph{(P4) The certified
object is not the deployed object:} $I(X;Z|Y) \le I(X;\mu(X)|Y) = +\infty$ for any
continuous non-class-constant $\mu$, so no finite certificate bounds the deployed
deterministic residual --- CEB's or FGIB's (Proposition~\ref{prop:object}).

\section{Method: the Fixed-Geometry Information Bottleneck}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Model}

A shared encoder computes the deterministic representation $h = f_{\mathrm{enc}}(X)$, and
the task head consumes $h$ directly at training and at evaluation time:
$\mathrm{logits} = c(h)$. In parallel, a \emph{side path} maps $h$ to a diagonal Gaussian
posterior
\begin{equation}
q(z|x) = \Ncal\big(\mu(x),\,\Sigma(x)\big),
\qquad \mu(x) = A\,h(x), \qquad
\Sigma(x) = \mathrm{diag}\,\exp\!\big(\ell(h(x))\big),
\label{eq:side}
\end{equation}
with a linear mean head $A$ and a log-variance head $\ell$, neither evaluated at
inference. The label-conditional prior is a set of frozen buffers,
\begin{equation}
r(z|y) = \Ncal\big(a\,v_y,\ \tau^2 I\big),
\qquad
V = [v_1,\dots,v_K] \ \text{orthonormal},\ \ V^{\top}V = I_K ,
\label{eq:anchor}
\end{equation}
where $V$ is the orthonormal factor of the QR decomposition of a random matrix, $a > 0$
the \emph{anchor scale} and $\tau^2$ the \emph{anchor variance} (default $1$). The
training objective is
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{FGIB}}
= \mathbb{E}\big[\CE\big(c(h), y\big)\big]
+ \beta\,\mathbb{E}\big[\KLd\big(q(z|x)\,\|\,r(z|y)\big)\big] .
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
Gradients from the second term reach the main path only through the shared encoder. The
log-variance head is zero-initialized, so training starts at $\sigma^2 = \tau^2$ and
$\KLd \approx a^2/(2\tau^2)$ --- and it does not merely \emph{start} there:
Result~\ref{res:varfreeze} below proves the variance stays pinned at $\tau^2$ for the
entire run, so the realized regularizer is exactly
$\tfrac{1}{2\tau^2}\|\mu - \mu_p\|^2$ at every step. The canonical
configuration fixes the side head to an \emph{orthogonal} map --- with $\dim z = \dim h$
one may take $A = I$ without loss (Result~\ref{res:orth}) --- so the anchor matching acts
directly on the deployed representation; the trainable head of Eq.~\eqref{eq:side} is
then an optional ablation, and we call the canonical form \emph{FGIB-H} ($A = I$).
For regression the per-class table is replaced by fixed random-Fourier anchors
\citep{rahimi2008random},
$\mu_p(y) = a\sqrt{2/\dim z}\,\cos(2\pi\omega y + b)$ with $\omega \sim \Ncal(0,4)$ and
$b \sim U[0,2\pi)$ frozen at initialization, so that $\|\mu_p(y)\| \approx a$; setting
$a = 0$ recovers a shared $\Ncal(0,\tau^2 I)$ prior, i.e.\ a VIB-style marginal
(Remark~\ref{rem:a0}). Three design decisions distinguish this from CEB, each targeting
one of P1--P4: the classifier consumes $h$ rather than $z$ (P3, P4); the two loss terms
act on disjoint parameter sets (P3); the reference is a buffer rather than a network
(P1, P2).

\paragraph{Theory--implementation layering.}
The analysis idealizes the side head as a linear invertible map with
$\dim z = \dim h$; the implementation uses an \texttt{nn.Linear} \emph{with bias} and no
orthogonality, invertibility or conditioning constraint --- and that freedom is precisely
what lets the trainable head absorb the anchor fit without moving $h$
(Section~\ref{sec:limits-main}(a), measured in Section~\ref{sec:ablation}). The
canonical FGIB-H configuration removes the gap rather than flagging it: $A = I$ is
orthogonal and invertible by construction, so the idealizations below --- invertibility,
conditioning, the pull-back reference of Result~\ref{res:equiv} --- hold exactly in the
implementation; for the trainable head they hold as approximations, and
Section~\ref{sec:limits-main}(a) states how the gap can widen when $A$ is freed. A
second layering gap concerns the variational family: the exact KL invariance of
Result~\ref{res:equiv} holds for the underlying Gaussians, but the diagonal family is
not closed under $A^{-1}$, so within it the identity is exact distributionally and
approximate as a variational identity (Remark~\ref{rem:closure}).

\subsection{What the frozen reference buys}

\begin{result}[The side path is a CEB bottleneck on the deployed representation]
\label{res:equiv}
Let $\dim z = \dim h$ and let $A$ be invertible. Pulling back by $A^{-1}$ maps the
posterior to $q'(\tilde z|x) = \Ncal(h(x),\,A^{-1}\Sigma A^{-\top})$ --- centered exactly
at the representation inference uses --- and the anchors to
$\Ncal(a A^{-1}v_y,\, \tau^2 (A^{\top}A)^{-1})$, which are orthonormal at scale $a$ in the
induced metric $A^{\top}A$. Since the Gaussian KL is invariant under a common invertible
linear map (Lemma~\ref{lem:kl-invariance}), the regularizer of Eq.~\eqref{eq:loss} is
pointwise identical to a CEB bottleneck --- with the backward encoder frozen at the
current side head --- applied to $\tilde z = h + \tilde\varepsilon$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:fgib-ceb}). At the variance optimum $\Sigma = \tau^2 I$ the
perturbation is $\tau\,(A^{\top}A)^{-1/2}\varepsilon$: \emph{known, side-path-only} noise
whose covariance is a model parameter (Proposition~\ref{prop:inverse}).
\end{result}

This licenses calling the construction a bottleneck at all: the regularizer
carries a residual-information bound read in coordinates centered on the deployed
representation --- an interpretation a bare prototype loss lacks. The identity holds
pointwise at each value of
$A$; because a trainable $A$ moves, the pulled-back reference moves with it --- the one
channel the trainable-head configuration leaves open, closed by the canonical
configuration (Result~\ref{res:orth}).

\begin{result}[A deterministic main path freezes the variance: the realized KL is a fixed-anchor center loss]
\label{res:varfreeze}
With the classifier consuming $h$ (never $z$), a zero-initialized log-variance head, a
frozen prior log-variance $\ell_p = \ln\tau^2 = 0$, and a first-order optimizer without
weight decay, the posterior log-variance is \emph{identically} $\ell_q \equiv \ell_p$ at
every training step, and the regularizer is exactly
$\KLd(q\,\|\,r) = \tfrac{1}{2\tau^2}\|\mu - \mu_p\|^2$ --- the KL of
Result~\ref{res:sweep} evaluated permanently at the variance optimum, not merely in the
limit. More generally, the per-coordinate gap $u = \ell_q - \ell_p$ obeys
$\partial_u \KLd = \tfrac12(e^{u}-1)$, which carries the sign of $u$, so $u = 0$ is the
unique attractor of any first-order descent: releasing the variance is exactly what makes
VIB and CEB race to the clamp (Channel~P3), and the deterministic main path eliminates
the race by design (Proposition~\ref{prop:varfreeze}). The same argument pins the
posterior variance in every FGIB-family ablation, so FGIB, TAFGIB and CentFGIB are
\emph{exactly} the same loss up to the trainability of the anchor means --- the
ablation table's number-coincidence is an identity --- and the one variant that escapes
the family, DCEB's trainable prior variance, is the one that collapses, at
$\beta = 10$: the race re-enters through the prior
(Corollary~\ref{cor:family}).
\end{result}

\begin{result}[A fixed orthogonal side head anchors the deployed representation]
\label{res:orth}
Let the side head be a fixed orthogonal map ($\dim z = \dim h$). Then
$\|A h - a v_y\| = \|h - a\,A^{\top}v_y\|$, the rotated frame $\{A^{\top}v_y\}$ is
orthonormal, and the regularizer is a fixed-anchor center loss on the deployed
representation $h$ itself. The induced metric of Result~\ref{res:equiv} is
$A^{\top}A = I$ with $\kappa = 1$ at every step: the pulled-back reference is orthonormal
at scale $a$, the perturbation is exactly $\tau\,\varepsilon$ with unit covariance, and
the conditioning counterexample of Proposition~\ref{prop:conditioning}(iii) is excluded
by construction. For Haar-distributed random orthogonal $A$ the rotated frame is
identically distributed to $\{v_y\}$ across seeds, so $A = I$ is a representative
canonical choice (Proposition~\ref{prop:orth}). We call the canonical form
\emph{FGIB-H} and measure its small price across settings
(Section~\ref{sec:ablation}, Table~\ref{tab:hanchor}).
\end{result}

\begin{result}[Every $\beta$ selects a distinct interior optimum]
\label{res:sweep}
Consider the class-symmetric aligned family --- the encoder maps class $y$ to
$h_y = s\,v_y$, the classifier is held at $w_j = c\,v_j$ with fixed scale $c$. Then
\begin{equation}
\KLd(s,\sigma^2) = \tfrac{\dim z}{2}\Big(\tfrac{\sigma^2}{\tau^2} - 1
- \ln\tfrac{\sigma^2}{\tau^2}\Big) + \tfrac{1}{2\tau^2}(s-a)^2,
\qquad
\CE(s) = \ln\!\big(1 + (K-1)e^{-cs}\big),
\label{eq:reduced}
\end{equation}
and the variance optimum is $\sigma^{2*} = \tau^2$ for \emph{every} $\beta$, because the
prediction term does not see $\sigma^2$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:lagrangian}). The Lagrangian
$L_\beta(s) = \CE(s) + \tfrac{\beta}{2\tau^2}(s-a)^2$ is then strictly convex with a
unique minimizer $s^*(\beta) > a$ given by
$\tfrac{\beta}{\tau^2}(s-a) = c\,\varphi(cs)$,
$\varphi(u) = (K{-}1)e^{-u}/(1+(K{-}1)e^{-u})$; the map $\beta \mapsto s^*(\beta)$ is
continuous and strictly decreasing, with $s^* \to a$ as $\beta \to \infty$ and
$s^* \to \infty$ as $\beta \to 0$, so $\beta \mapsto (\KLd(s^*), \CE(s^*))$ is one-to-one
onto the entire curve, which is strictly concave
(Propositions~\ref{prop:sweep},~\ref{prop:curve}).
\end{result}

The mechanism is the whole point of freezing the
reference. Deterministic-IB on hard clusterings has compression and prediction as the
\emph{same} scalar, so the objective is monotone in it; standard IB in the deterministic
scenario has a piecewise-linear curve with a single supporting slope. Both are repaired
in \citet{kolchinsky2019caveats} by \emph{squaring} the compression term by hand. FGIB
needs no surgery:
$\KLd = \tfrac{1}{2\tau^2}\|\mu-\mu_p\|^2 + \text{(variance terms)}$ is quadratic in the
displacement \emph{whenever $\mu_p$ does not
move}. Entropy-based compression is self-referential --- it depends only on the
representation's own distribution; a fixed anchor supplies an absolute frame and turns
compression into a squared distance. The convexification is inherited, not installed.

\begin{result}[A persistent force and a non-collapsed compressed endpoint]
\label{res:endpoint}
At a fixed encoder, CEB's bound is tighter in value: $\ReX(\theta^*) \le \ReX_F$, the
excess being the anchor gap $\mathbb{E}_y[\KLd(q(z|y)\|\Ncal(a v_y, \tau^2 I))] -
\mathbb{E}_y[\KLd(q(z|y)\|\Ncal(m_y,S_y))] \ge 0$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:ordering}). That excess is the price of the fixed anchor, and it
is paid for a reason: CEB's gap is a function of the backward encoder alone, so it can be
driven to zero with the representation untouched, whereas FGIB's gap can shrink only if
the posterior moves toward the anchors --- the force is persistent
(Remark~\ref{rem:force}). Moreover, the $\KLd = 0$ point is
$\mu_y = a v_y$, a class-separated configuration with $\CE = \ln(1+(K{-}1)e^{-ca})$, which
is bounded and $\to 0$ as $a$ grows; a marginal $\Ncal(0,I)$ prior instead places
$\KLd = 0$ at $\mu \equiv 0$ with $\CE = \ln K$, i.e.\ chance, and squaring the KL (SVIB)
leaves that corner in place since $\KLd^2$ is stationary at $\KLd = 0$
(Remark~\ref{rem:endpoint}). The trivial collapse pays a fixed positive price
$\tfrac{1}{2\tau^2}a^2$ per class.
\end{result}

Result~\ref{res:endpoint} is the prediction the experiments test most directly: it says
that pushing $\beta$ up should not destroy an FGIB model, and that the anchor scale $a$ is
the dial controlling how benign the fully compressed corner is. Both are visible in
Tables~\ref{tab:robust} and~\ref{tab:anchor}.

\subsection{What the frozen reference does not buy}
\label{sec:limits-main}

We state the open items here rather than in a closing paragraph, because two of them bear
on how the experiments should be read. \emph{(a) A trainable side head is a second
trainable party in the KL} --- structurally CEB's backward encoder --- and it can absorb
the anchor fit without moving $h$; the canonical fixed-orthogonal head closes this
channel by construction, and the measurement of Section~\ref{sec:ablation} shows the
trainable variant's $\kappa(A^{\top}A)$ degrades to $10^{5.8}$--$10^{6.8}$ while
accuracy is unaffected, so the freedom buys nothing (Appendix~\ref{app:limits}). \emph{(b)
The sweep needs a bounded classifier scale} $c$; the implementation leaves it free, so
Result~\ref{res:sweep} is conditional as implemented, and enforced-scale variants are
tested on MNIST (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}). \emph{(c) The certificate still does not
bound the deployed residual} (P4 applies unchanged). \emph{(d) The compressed endpoint is
designed, not certified:} the anchor corner is a label code, not semantic compression
(Remark~\ref{rem:endpoint}). \emph{(e) The sweep is over a surrogate:} the
$(\KLd_{\mathrm{anchor}},\CE)$ plane on the aligned family, not the information plane
(Remark~\ref{rem:surrogate}). The full statement of each item is in
Appendix~\ref{app:limits}.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Setup}

\paragraph{Tasks and backbones.}
We use 9 datasets over 4 backbones, giving 12 task$\times$backbone settings: MNIST
\citep{lecun1998mnist} (MLP/CNN), ImageNet-100 \citep{deng2009imagenet} frozen ResNet-50
\citep{he2016resnet} layer-3 features pooled to $1024\times4\times4$ (MLP/CNN), Cora
\citep{sen2008collective} transductive node classification (GCN \citep{kipf2017gcn}),
IMDb \citep{maas2011imdb} and AG News \citep{zhang2015character} (frozen BERT
\citep{devlin2019bert} token features, PCA-50) with LSTM \citep{hochreiter1997lstm}
encoders; and the regressions California Housing \citep{pace1997sparse} (MLP), STS-B
\citep{cer2017semeval,wang2019glue} (GloVe \citep{pennington2014glove} LSTM), ZINC-12k
\citep{dwivedi2023benchmarking} graph-level regression (GCN), and AgeDB
\citep{moschoglou2017agedb} face-age regression at $64\times64$ RGB (MLP/CNN). Splits
are $60/20/20$ (seed 42) except ZINC (official $10{,}000/1{,}000/1{,}000$); several
splits are custom, so every comparison in this paper is internal to the same split.
AgeDB's image-level split leaks identity across the boundary ($562$ of $567$ subjects);
a subject-disjoint control, run here, preserves the objective ordering
(Table~\ref{tab:agedb-disjoint}), so the leak inflates magnitudes but does not create
the pattern. Appendix~\ref{app:data} gives the preprocessing.

\paragraph{Objectives.}
Each backbone carries the same seven heads: the deterministic backbone, VIB
\citep{alemi2017vib}, squared-IB \citep[SVIB, penalty $\beta\KLd^2$]{kolchinsky2019caveats},
nonlinear IB \citep[NIB, non-parametric pairwise bound, learned noise
variance]{kolchinsky2019nib}, CEB \citep[diagonal-Gaussian backward
encoder]{fischer2020ceb}, supervised $\beta$-DVCCA
\citep[VIB plus an input-reconstruction decoder]{abdelaleem2025dvmib}, and FGIB. For
regression, CEB and FGIB condition their priors on the min-max-scaled continuous target.
Every variant shares the encoder, the bottleneck dimension ($\dim z = 256$) and the
optimizer. The objectives' compression terms are not dimensionally identical (NIB's
bound is in nats; DVCCA's $\beta$ also weights reconstruction), so $\beta$ is treated as
a per-objective knob swept independently and selected on validation, never as a shared
axis of equal compression.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
Adam \citep{kingma2015adam} at $10^{-3}$ ($10^{-2}$ for Cora), batch size 512 (128 for
ZINC), at most 100 epochs, early stopping with patience 10 on validation macro-AUC
(classification) or validation $R^2$ (regression); each configuration is run 5 times
with seeds $0..4$ and we report the mean over runs. The grid is
$\beta \in \{10^{-4},\dots,10\}$ for every bottleneck plus
$a \in \{1,\dots,16\}$ for FGIB: $1{,}020$ configurations, $5{,}100$ runs.
\textbf{Hyperparameters are selected on validation, never on test}: each cell reports
the test score of its best mean-validation configuration, with exact ties broken by a
second validation quantity and then fixed grid order, so no selection step consults the
test set. FGIB is thus selected over a $6\times9$ grid where the others use 6 points ---
a search-budget advantage we return to with an equal-budget control. Per-run standard
deviations and selected hyperparameters are in Appendix~\ref{app:tables}; the ablations
of Section~\ref{sec:ablation} use the identical protocol on MNIST/MLP.

\subsection{Accuracy: FGIB ranks first, but no bottleneck wins by much}

Table~\ref{tab:main} reports the validation-selected configuration of each objective.
FGIB attains the best mean rank, $1.75$ of $7$, and is best or tied-best on 7 of the 12
settings; the runner-up is the deterministic backbone at $3.42$, and the five prior
bottlenecks occupy $3.75$ (VIB) to $5.08$ (NIB). The wins span every modality and
backbone, and where FGIB does not win it is close (within $0.07$ points on MNIST/CNN,
IMDb and ZINC); it is clearly beaten only on AG News ($92.71$ vs.\ $92.94$) and
AgeDB/MLP ($32.71$ vs.\ $33.85$).

Three qualifications are essential. First, \emph{the margins are small}. Excluding the two
settings where every bottleneck helps a weak backbone, no objective \emph{improves}
on its backbone by more than about one point, and on 3 of 12 settings the deterministic
backbone is still best or tied-best; losses can be large even at the
validation-selected optimum (SVIB gives up $10.1$ points on Cora). Reading
Table~\ref{tab:main} as evidence that bottlenecks substantially
improve in-distribution accuracy is reading it wrong; it shows that FGIB is the
only one that is reliably not \emph{worse}. Second,
\emph{FGIB's selection grid is $9\times$ larger}. The equal-budget control --- the last
column of Table~\ref{tab:main-std}, the same 6-point $\beta$ grid at a fixed $a=16$
(post-hoc) --- has mean rank $2.33$ and best-or-tied $6$ of $12$, still ahead of the
backbone ($3.42$) and every prior bottleneck ($3.75$--$5.08$): part of the rank
advantage is search budget; the ordering of methods is not
(Table~\ref{tab:anchor-ranks} shows the fixed-anchor rank ranges from $2.00$ at $a=14$
to $3.17$ at $a=1$). Third, \emph{the rank gaps are backed by paired statistics, and we
report their sensitivity}. Table~\ref{tab:paired} reports per-seed paired differences
against the backbone with bootstrap $95\%$ CIs (over the 5 paired seeds; the intervals
describe training randomness, not split uncertainty). A Wilcoxon test over the 12
setting-level paired means finds FGIB the only objective whose gain over its backbone
is significantly positive ($p=0.014$; $p=0.12$ at the equal-budget control), and FGIB
beats each prior bottleneck pairwise ($p \le 0.016$, all significant under Holm). The
gain result is task-set-sensitive, and we say so plainly: excluding the two AgeDB rows
the mean paired gain falls from $+1.46$ to $+0.62$ points and the Wilcoxon $p$-value
from $0.014$ to $0.055$, while the mean rank ($1.70$, best-or-tied on $6$ of $10$
settings) and the stability results are unchanged; a subject-disjoint AgeDB split
preserves the objective ordering (Table~\ref{tab:agedb-disjoint}), so the leak inflates
magnitudes without creating the pattern. We
therefore take the \emph{rank and the non-destructiveness of the knob} as the primary
statistical claims, treat the gain-over-backbone as marginal and sensitive, and exclude
AgeDB from the core statistical statements of this section.

\subsection{The knob: FGIB is the only objective whose $\beta$ is both safe and useful}
\label{sec:knob}

Result~\ref{res:endpoint} predicts what happens at the \emph{other} end of the sweep: a
marginal-prior bottleneck has its $\KLd = 0$ point at $\mu \equiv 0$, so raising $\beta$
far enough must drive it to the trivial predictor; FGIB's $\KLd = 0$ point is
class-separated. Table~\ref{tab:robust} measures exactly this: for each objective we
take the \emph{worst} test score produced by any $\beta$ in the grid, and report the
deficit against the deterministic backbone of the same row.

The separation is stark. VIB, CEB and DVCCA are driven below the trivial predictor on all
7 classification settings (median worst-case deficits $71.9$, $66.1$, $79.1$ points);
SVIB is only marginally better ($64.8$), as Remark~\ref{rem:endpoint} predicts: squaring
the KL does not move the corner. FGIB's median worst-case deficit is $0.31$ points at the
validation-selected anchor and $0.35$ at fixed $a=16$; on classification the worst case
over the six-point, five-order-of-magnitude $\beta$ grid is within $0.52$ points of the
backbone on \emph{every} one of the seven settings (per-setting values in
Table~\ref{tab:robust}) --- against deficits of $34$--$88$ points for VIB, CEB and DVCCA
on the same rows.

NIB is the interesting control. It is as stable as FGIB (median deficit $0.53$), but for
a different reason and at a different price: its non-parametric bound saturates at
$\ln N$ with exponentially vanishing gradient once the noise is small relative to the
cluster spread \citep[\S IV A]{kolchinsky2019nib}, so it is stable largely because the
knob barely acts --- on MNIST/MLP its accuracy moves from $98.15$ to $98.27$ across the
entire sweep. Table~\ref{tab:robust} reports two gain summaries, and the distinction
matters. ``Median gain at best $\beta$'' takes the maximum over the grid of the
\emph{test} score --- an exploratory upper bound, not a model-selection result --- and on
it NIB scores $-0.27$ points: even with test-selected optimism the knob is not worth
turning, and VIB, SVIB, CEB, DVCCA ($-0.15$, $-0.14$, $-0.22$, $-0.15$) are likewise
negative; only FGIB is positive ($+0.39$; $+0.27$ at $a=16$). The protocol-clean row is
the median gain at the \emph{validation-selected} configuration of Table~\ref{tab:main}:
FGIB $+0.26$ ($+0.20$ at $a=16$), the five prior bottlenecks $-0.21$ to $-0.37$ --- the
conclusion survives the protocol correction. \emph{Among the six bottlenecks, FGIB is
the only one whose compression knob is both non-destructive and, at the median, worth
turning.}

The exceptions are informative and we do not hide them. FGIB is destroyed --- worst case
worse than the backbone by more than 5 points --- in 3 of 12 settings at $a=16$, and all
three are regression: STS-B ($13.2$ points of $R^2$), AgeDB ($15.6$ CNN, $24.0$ MLP),
with ZINC just below threshold at $4.5$. We therefore report the regression results as
\emph{exploratory} and keep the core claims on classification. The theory anticipates
this asymmetry: the
random-Fourier anchor construction for continuous targets is a transfer argument, not a
proof, and it inherits both the fixed-scale and the conditioning caveats
(Remark~\ref{rem:caveats}). Whatever protects the compressed corner in the orthonormal
per-class case does not transfer intact to the RFF case.

\subsection{The anchor scale is the dial the theory says it is}

Result~\ref{res:endpoint} says \emph{why} FGIB is robust, and therefore when robustness
should fail: the compressed corner costs $\CE = \ln(1+(K{-}1)e^{-ca})$, small only when
$ca$ is large relative to $\ln(K{-}1)$; at $a \to 0$ the anchors merge onto a single
origin and FGIB degenerates to a VIB-style marginal prior (Remark~\ref{rem:a0}).
Table~\ref{tab:anchor} holds $\beta$ at the largest grid value --- maximum compression
pressure --- and varies $a$.

The prediction holds. At $a=1$, FGIB behaves like the marginal-prior bottlenecks: $1.0\%$
on ImageNet-100/MLP (chance for 100 classes), $26.6\%$ on Cora (below the majority
class), $R^2 = -0.001$ on AgeDB/MLP (the mean predictor). Growing $a$ recovers the
backbone --- monotonically on ImageNet-100/MLP ($1.0 \to \dots \to 83.0$, $0.2$ below its
backbone), with local dips elsewhere (Cora ends $87.2\%$, AgeDB/MLP $R^2 = 0.293$, $7.5$
points \emph{above} its backbone). The trend, not the monotonicity, is what the theory
predicts, and it holds on 10 of the 12 rows. The two rows with no upward trend are the
two the theory says should not have one: IMDb is binary, so the corner value is already
negligible at $a=1$ (the whole row sits above its backbone at every $a$); ZINC is a
regression row, where the random-Fourier anchors carry no corresponding guarantee
(Remark~\ref{rem:caveats}) and it stays $4$--$6$ points below its GCN throughout.
Consistent with the $(K{-}1)$ factor, the 100-class task needs by far the largest anchor
(MNIST/MLP, $K{=}10$, is within $0.3$ points at $a=1$; ImageNet-100/MLP needs $a \ge 10$).

The formula does not predict the transition threshold, and we should not pretend it does.
Cora ($K{=}7$) collapses at $a \le 2$ where MNIST ($K{=}10$) does not, so $K$ is not the
only factor --- the classifier scale $c$ enters the corner value multiplicatively and is
free in our implementation (Remark~\ref{rem:c-scale}), and Cora is a small transductive
problem with correspondingly different margins. What Table~\ref{tab:anchor} establishes is
the qualitative mechanism --- recovery with $a$, chance-level collapse as $a \to$ the VIB
limit, largest-$K$ needs largest $a$ --- not a quantitative fit, and not strict
monotonicity.

\subsection{Confound ablations and diagnostics on MNIST}
\label{sec:ablation}

Section~\ref{sec:limits-main} identifies the channels FGIB leaves open; this section
reports the supplementary experiments that test whether they bite, that separate
FGIB's two design changes from each other, and that verify the two closure results of
Section~\ref{sec:method}. All runs use the identical protocol as the
main sweep (100 epochs, patience 10, 5 seeds), except the long-horizon control noted
below; the additional experiments total 360 runs across a 12-setting CentFGIB sweep at
equal budget, a 4-setting FGIB-H ($A{=}I$) sweep, a full-batch diagnostic rerun, a
400-epoch long-horizon control, a frozen-ETF baseline, and a subject-disjoint AgeDB
sweep.
Figure~\ref{fig:betaacc} plots the full $\beta$ sweeps and Table~\ref{tab:ablation} the
numbers.

\paragraph{What the ablations show.}
FGIB differs from the comparison in two coupled ways: its classifier consumes the
deterministic $h$ at training and evaluation (P3, P4), and its prior is frozen (P1, P2).
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} separates the contributions. VIB and CEB fall to chance at
$\beta=1$ ($15.3\%$ and $26.8\%$); \emph{DCEB} --- the deterministic path
of FGIB with CEB's trainable backward encoder as its prior --- holds at $\approx 98$
through $\beta=1$ and falls to $11.4\%$ only at $\beta=10$. Corollary~\ref{cor:family}
explains \emph{why} DCEB is the odd one out: its trainable prior \emph{variance} has a
negative gradient in the mean gap, so the race of Channel~P3 re-enters through the
prior --- the only FGIB-family variant that escapes the exact center-loss family is the
only one that collapses. By contrast, every variant that \emph{starts} from the
orthonormal anchor table stays at $\approx 98.3$ across the entire grid (TAFGIB,
CentFGIB, frozen $A$, FGIB-H) --- and by Result~\ref{res:varfreeze} that equivalence is
an identity, not a coincidence: the zero-initialized variance head receives zero
gradient at every step, so FGIB's KL \emph{is} the center loss exactly, and CentFGIB,
TAFGIB and FGIB-H are the same objective up to the trainability of the anchor means.
The trainable anchors of TAFGIB do move (final column norms shrink from $16$ to
$5.7$--$10$), yet the accuracy curve is untouched --- the
load-bearing ingredient is the anchor-table \emph{initialization} geometry at scale
$a$, not the frozenness of its means and not any Gaussian variance machinery, which is
provably inert here (Result~\ref{res:varfreeze}). What freezing the means and keeping
the KL buys is the \emph{theory}: the persistent force of Remark~\ref{rem:force}, the
convexified sweep of Proposition~\ref{prop:sweep}, and the residual-information reading
of Result~\ref{res:equiv} --- FGIB retains an exact variational upper-bound
interpretation for the auxiliary noisy code; its predictive robustness outside the
analyzed family remains empirical. Constraining the side head changes nothing in the
$\beta$-profile on MNIST ($98.2$--$98.4$ across the grid for frozen $A$ and $A{=}I$),
and the fixed classifier scale test agrees: enforcing the cosine condition leaves
FGIB's sweep unchanged ($97.9$--$98.3$) while VIB and CEB still collapse, so the anchor
geometry, not the free classifier scale, keeps the corner non-degenerate.

\paragraph{The variance race is real but slow.}
Logging the mean posterior log-variance, the clamp fraction ($e^{-10}$), and (for CEB)
the prior log-variance at every epoch of every run, aggregated over the \emph{entire}
validation set, CEB's final posterior log-variance sits at
$+0.37, +0.27, +0.13, +0.08, -0.05, -0.01$ (nats) for
$\beta = 10^{-4}, \dots, 10$ --- near initialization, nowhere near the clamp, clamp
fraction $0.0$ at every epoch (Figure~\ref{fig:diag}(b)). A 400-epoch control at
$\beta\in\{1,10\}$ (early stopping disabled) shows the race's \emph{direction}:
the posterior log-variance ends at $-2.27$ nats ($\beta{=}1$) and $-1.20$
($\beta{=}10$), passing $-0.92$ at epoch 100, the prior tracking --- monotone, exactly
as Proposition~\ref{prop:variance}(i)--(ii) predicts, but still far from the $e^{-10}$
terminal state (Figure~\ref{fig:longtrain}). The race is therefore real but slow:
invisible at the protocol horizon, directionally confirmed at four times it, its
convergence time-scale beyond the tested 400 epochs --- the conditional status
Remark~\ref{rem:p3-status} assigns to part~(iii). For FGIB the question is settled
analytically: the flat zero line of Figure~\ref{fig:longtrain} is the fixed point of
Result~\ref{res:varfreeze}, held for 400 epochs, with the trainable prior variance of
DCEB the only re-entry point (Corollary~\ref{cor:family}).

\paragraph{The side head: measured conditioning, quantified closure.}
The trainable head's $\log_{10}\kappa(A^{\top}A)$ rises from $10^{3.9}$ at
initialization to $10^{5.8}$--$10^{6.8}$ (transient peaks $10^{8.3}$;
Figure~\ref{fig:diag}(a)), while accuracy is unaffected on MNIST. The canonical
FGIB-H ($A{=}I$) closes the channel by construction (Result~\ref{res:orth}), and
Table~\ref{tab:hanchor} quantifies the price across five settings:
at the inherited scale $a=16$ FGIB-H stays ahead of the backbone on 4 of 5 settings but
trails the trainable head by
$\approx 0.5$ points per setting; at the re-tuned scale $a=4$ it recovers most of the
gap (mean rank $2.25$ vs.\ $1.20$) and ties or beats the backbone on every row --- the
trade-off
is accuracy-versus-certification, small in both directions, and both configurations ship
in the released code.
The cross-dataset reach of the geometry itself is measured separately: CentFGIB matches
FGIB($a{=}16$) to within $0.01$ points on 10 of 12 settings at equal budget
(Table~\ref{tab:centfgib}), a frozen ETF classifier alone reproduces the backbone but
buys no knob ($98.32\%$ vs.\ $98.33\%$), and a subject-disjoint AgeDB split preserves
the objective ordering (Table~\ref{tab:agedb-disjoint}). The full details of this
section --- per-variant grid tables, the TAFGIB geometry measurements, the fixed-scale
rows, and the anchor-scale sensitivity of the equal-budget rank --- are in
Appendix~\ref{app:ablation-detail}. The full per-setting anchor dial
(Table~\ref{tab:anchor}) and the $\beta$-sweep curves
(Figure~\ref{fig:betaacc}) are likewise in Appendix~\ref{app:tables}.

\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Variational bottlenecks.}
VIB \citep{alemi2017vib} bounds $I(X;Z)$ with a marginal prior; CEB
\citep{fischer2020ceb} bounds the residual $I(X;Z|Y)$ with a learned backward encoder and
is tighter by exactly $I(Y;Z)$ at the respective optima. FGIB inherits the
residual-level object and replaces the learned backward encoder with a frozen geometric
one; Proposition~\ref{prop:ordering} shows the resulting bound is looser in value by the
anchor gap, and Remark~\ref{rem:force} argues that this is what makes its force
persistent. DVCCA \citep{abdelaleem2025dvmib} sits in the same variational family with an
added reconstruction term, and inherits the marginal-prior corner.

\paragraph{Degeneracies of the IB Lagrangian.}
\citet{kolchinsky2019caveats} prove that in the deterministic scenario the IB curve is
piecewise linear, so the Lagrangian cannot sweep it, and repair this by squaring the
compression term. Our Proposition~\ref{prop:dead} shows CEB inherits the degeneracy over
its entire range, and Result~\ref{res:sweep} shows that a fixed reference supplies the
same strict convexity \emph{without} squaring --- while squaring alone (SVIB) does not
fix the collapse (Table~\ref{tab:robust}). \citet{kolchinsky2019nib} avoid the parametric
prior entirely with a non-parametric pairwise bound; our experiments locate the price of
that route in an inert knob.

\paragraph{Deterministic representations and infinite information.}
\citet{amjad2020learning} and \citet{kolchinsky2019nib} observe that $I(X;Z)$ is infinite
or ill-defined for deterministic continuous encoders, and \citet{saxe2018information}
question the empirical IB narrative on related grounds; sample-based limits on measuring
mutual information \citep{mcallester2020formal,tschannen2020mutual} apply to any
bound-as-meter reading, ours included. FGIB's response is architectural: keep the
inference path deterministic and confine the noise --- and thus the finite bound --- to a
side path that inference never reads.

\paragraph{Fixed geometric targets.}
Center loss pulls features toward learned per-class centers \citep{wen2016center};
hyperspherical prototype networks place prototypes on a hypersphere a priori
\citep{mettes2019hpn}; the final classifier itself can be fixed with little accuracy
loss \citep{hoffer2018fix}, in particular to a simplex equiangular tight frame
\citep{zhu2021geometric,pernici2019regular}; and neural-collapse analyses show such
configurations arise spontaneously \citep{papyan2020prevalence}. FGIB's compressed
corner is a neural-collapse-like geometry --- orthonormal class codes rather than the
centered simplex/ETF configuration --- but the construction differs in the role the
geometry plays: it enters as the \emph{reference of a variational bound}, i.e.\ as the
design of the bottleneck's zero-penalty set, which makes the compression term
quadratic (Result~\ref{res:sweep}) and gives the regularizer a residual-information
reading for the auxiliary code (Result~\ref{res:equiv}) rather than serving as an
auxiliary classification loss (Table~\ref{tab:related}, Appendix~\ref{app:tables}). The
boundary of that claim is a theorem rather than an admission: in the released
implementation the KL \emph{is} the fixed-anchor center loss (Result~\ref{res:varfreeze}),
and the canonical fixed-orthogonal
head moves the matching onto the deployed representation itself
(Result~\ref{res:orth}) --- the
\emph{empirical} robustness is shared with fixed-prototype regularization by
construction, while the bound reading and the sweep theory are specific to the
variational form. The natural baselines from this literature are tested
directly: a frozen ETF classifier alone reproduces the backbone but buys no knob, and
trainable-center and fixed-center losses are the
TAFGIB and CentFGIB rows of the ablation (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).

\section{Conclusion}

The high-regularization behavior of a variational bottleneck is decided by its
zero-penalty set, and the standard choices are poorly designed: VIB's marginal prior
places that set at collapse, CEB's learned backward encoder leaves it underdetermined,
and both certify the noisy training code rather than the deterministic representation
inference deploys (a gradient-level variance race, in
contrast, is invisible at the protocol horizon and only drifts into view at 400
epochs). Designing the set directly --- an orthonormal anchor frame fixed by
construction --- makes the compression term quadratic, well-poses the sweep at fixed
classifier scale, and turns the fully compressed corner into the prescribed
class-separated anchor configuration. Two properties of the construction are provable
rather than merely observed: the deterministic main path pins the variance at
$\tau^2$ for the entire run, so the realized regularizer is \emph{exactly} the
fixed-anchor center loss $\tfrac{1}{2\tau^2}\|\mu-a v_y\|^2$ --- releasing the variance
is what makes the alternatives race to the clamp, and FGIB eliminates the race by
design; and the canonical fixed-orthogonal side head anchors the deployed representation
directly with $\kappa=1$, closing the conditioning channel a trainable head leaves
open, at a measured price of $\approx 0.5$ points per setting that re-tuning the anchor
scale largely recovers. Empirically, across 12
task$\times$backbone
settings and $5{,}100$ runs, that is worth a first-place mean rank under the
equal-budget configuration ($2.33$ over all 12; $1.75$ over the full grid; $1.86$
classification-only; $1.70$ excluding AgeDB) and, more distinctively, a
compression knob that can be swept over a six-point grid spanning five orders of
magnitude without destroying the model on classification --- something no other objective
in the comparison manages. The ablations pin down what carries that robustness: the
endpoint geometry, not any Gaussian variance machinery --- which is provably
inert here --- not the frozenness of the anchor
means, and not the deterministic path alone; the one variant that re-opens the variance,
DCEB, is the one that collapses. The KL's role is interpretive: an exact
variational upper bound on the residual information of the auxiliary noisy code, the
persistent force and convexified sweep of the theory. The cross-dataset
reach of the geometry-only mechanism is measured --- CentFGIB matches
FGIB at equal budget on all 12 settings, FGIB-H quantifies the fixed head's price
across five
(Section~\ref{sec:ablation}) --- and a subject-disjoint AgeDB control preserves the
regression ordering. What remains on the ledger: split-sensitivity beyond AgeDB,
budget expansion for the comparison methods, and the downstream robustness properties
that motivate bottlenecks in the first place.

\section{Failure channels in detail}
\label{app:prelim-detail}

This section states the four channels of Section~\ref{sec:prelim} with the precision
the main text omits; the propositions themselves are proved in
Appendix~\ref{app:theory} (Part~I).

\paragraph{P1 --- the knob is dead in the deterministic scenario.}
By the chain rule, CEB's objective is $I(X;Z) - (1+\gamma)I(Y;Z)$, and the
data-processing inequality gives
$L_\gamma(Z) \ge -\gamma I(Y;Z) \ge -\gamma H(Y)$ with equality iff
$I(X;Z) = I(Y;Z)$ and $I(Y;Z) = H(Y)$. Every $\gamma > 0$ therefore has the same exact
optimum --- the corner --- so
$\gamma \mapsto (I(X;Z^*|Y), I(Y;Z^*))$ is constant and no interior point of the IB curve
is ever a Lagrangian minimizer (Proposition~\ref{prop:dead}). Since
$\beta_{\mathrm{IB}} = 1+\gamma > 1$ always, CEB sits in the regime that
\citet{kolchinsky2019caveats} show to be degenerate over its \emph{entire} parameter
range. \citet{kolchinsky2019caveats} themselves note that if one's goal \emph{is} the
corner, this reads as $\beta$-robustness rather than failure. Remark~\ref{rem:mni}
explains why that does not close the matter: the corner is one point in the information
plane but an entire equivalence class of representations, and adversarial robustness,
calibration and OoD detection --- the properties CEB is argued for --- are properties of
the \emph{geometry} of $Z$, on which the objective is exactly constant. Which member of
the class is reached is settled by the optimizer, i.e.\ by channels P3 and P4.

\paragraph{P2 --- a certificate that is blind, and a $1{:}\beta$ exchange rate.}
On the manifold $T_\alpha = B_\alpha \cdot Y$ of \citet{kolchinsky2019caveats}, $T_\alpha$
is a function of $Y$ alone, so $I(X;T_\alpha|Y) = 0$ and, at the optimal backward encoder,
$\ReX(T_\alpha) = 0$ for every $\alpha$ --- from total collapse ($\alpha = 0$, no label
information) to full copy ($\alpha = 1$). Reading $\ReX$ as a meter of distance to the MNI
point is therefore valid only along the $I(X;Z|Y)$ axis; on the necessity axis the meter
reads $0$ at chance level. With the optimal classifier the objective is
$\delta + \beta\,\ReX$ where $\delta = H(Y) - I(Y;T_\alpha)$, so it is indifferent between
giving up $\delta$ nats of label information and saving $\delta/\beta$ nats of certified
residual: in the heavy-regularization regime the whole label axis is purchasable for
$H(Y)/\beta$ nats of certified compression (Proposition~\ref{prop:flat}).

\paragraph{P3 --- the variance race.}
For the diagonal Gaussian family the KL is stationary in the posterior variance at
$\sigma^2 = \tau^2$, with residual value $\|m-g\|^2/(2\tau^2)$ --- pure mean matching. The
cross-entropy term, however, is convex in $z$ for a linear classifier (the log-sum-exp of
an affine function of $z$), so by Jensen
$\mathbb{E}_z[\CE(c(z),y)] \ge \CE(c(m),y)$ and it strictly prefers $\sigma^2 \to 0$.
Along the matched line the KL \emph{resists} shrinking $\tau^2$ only while the means are
unmatched ($\partial_{\tau^2}\KLd = -\|m-g\|^2/(2\tau^4) < 0$); once $\|m-g\| \to 0$ the
race is unopposed and runs to the implementation clamp $s = e^{-10}$, at which the
certificate has become a mean-matching force of weight
$1/s \approx 2.2\times 10^{4}$ between two trainable maps
(Proposition~\ref{prop:variance}). Parts (ii)--(iii) of that proposition are a
direction-of-force argument, not a convergence proof. We ran the falsification test it
invites, by logging the trained log-variances (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}): at the
early-stopping time scale of our protocol the race does not materialize --- CEB's
variances remain at their initialization $\sigma^2 = \tau^2$ with zero clamp fraction on
every $\beta$ --- so the convergence prediction of part~(iii) is empirically void under
this protocol, exactly as Remark~\ref{rem:p3-status} allows.

\paragraph{P4 --- the certified object is not the deployed object.}
At the variance optimum the posterior standard deviation is class-constant, so
$Z = \mu(X) + \sqrt{S(Y)}\,\varepsilon$ is a noisy function of $\mu(X)$ given $Y$ and the
data-processing inequality gives $I(X;Z|Y) \le I(X;\mu(X)|Y)$. The gap is not a small
correction: whenever $\mu$ is continuous and not class-constant the right-hand side is
$+\infty$ \citep{amjad2020learning,kolchinsky2019nib}, so no finite quantity bounds the
deployed residual. This applies to FGIB as well (Proposition~\ref{prop:object}); what
differs is structural, not a tightness ordering. In CEB the perturbation is \emph{inside}
the inference path and is driven by the prediction term, so removing it changes the
model; in FGIB it lives on a side path that inference never reads and is controlled by
side-path parameters alone. That is a cleaner separation of regularizer from model, and
we claim nothing more for it.

\section{What the frozen reference does not buy: full statement}
\label{app:limits}

\emph{(a) The side head is a second trainable party --- unless it is fixed.} The
equivalence of
Result~\ref{res:equiv} holds \emph{at each fixed} $A$, and a \emph{trainable} $A$, unused
at
inference --- structurally the same position CEB's backward encoder occupies on the other
side of the KL. Since $h$ is linearly separable by the jointly trained classifier, much of
the anchor fit can be absorbed by $A$ alone, and nothing in the objective resists $A$
drifting toward a projector onto the $K$-dimensional between-class subspace. Full collapse
is blocked (with $A$ invertible, $\KLd = 0$ forces $h$ itself to be class-constant), but
the approach to the rank-$K$ regime is not, and that is exactly where the conditioning of
$A^{\top}A$ --- which governs the size of the side-path perturbation in
Result~\ref{res:equiv} --- degrades without bound (Remark~\ref{rem:A-channel},
Proposition~\ref{prop:conditioning}(iii)). This channel is closed by the canonical
configuration: a fixed orthogonal head has $\kappa(A^{\top}A) = 1$ by construction and
anchors $h$ directly (Result~\ref{res:orth}). The
measurement of Section~\ref{sec:ablation} quantifies how badly the \emph{optional
trainable} variant degrades on MNIST:
$\kappa(A^{\top}A)$ rises to $10^{5.8}$--$10^{6.8}$, i.e.\ severely ill-conditioned,
with predictive accuracy unaffected --- so the freedom buys nothing on MNIST; across
five settings the fixed head pays $\approx 0.5$ points per setting for the closure at
the inherited scale, largely recovered by re-tuning the anchor scale
(Table~\ref{tab:hanchor}) --- the accuracy-versus-certification trade-off made
explicit. We therefore report the
trainable head as the default of the released sweep but designate FGIB-H as the
canonical, closed configuration.

\emph{(b) The sweep needs a bounded classifier scale.} If $c$ is free, then
$(s,c) = (a,\infty)$ sends $\KLd \to 0$ and $\CE \to 0$ simultaneously, so the infimum of
$L_\beta$ is $0$ for every $\beta$ and Result~\ref{res:sweep} has no global counterpart
--- the FGIB analogue of the dead knob, reached by growing the margin rather than by the
linearity of the IB curve. Our classifier is an unconstrained \texttt{nn.Linear} trained
by Adam without weight decay, so the proposition is conditional as implemented. A
fixed-temperature cosine classifier satisfies the assumption by construction; weight decay
only mitigates the divergence (Remark~\ref{rem:c-scale}). One consequence matters for
Section~\ref{sec:exp}: since $s^*$ drifts toward $a$ as $c$ grows, part of any observed
$\beta$-effect under early stopping is plausibly an early-stopping effect, and we have not
separated the two.

\emph{(c) The certificate still does not bound the deployed residual.} P4 applies to FGIB
unchanged (Proposition~\ref{prop:object}(b)). What FGIB buys is that the perturbation is
side-path-only and its covariance is a known model parameter --- a cleaner separation of
regularizer from model, not a tighter or more valid bound (Remark~\ref{rem:senses}).

\emph{(d) The compressed endpoint is designed, not certified.} Caveat~2 of
\citet{kolchinsky2019caveats} identifies as uninteresting those variables that compress by
mixing a constant map with a copy of $Y$; FGIB's compressed corner $\mu_y = a v_y$ is a
deterministic function of $Y$ alone, i.e.\ a label code of exactly that kind. The
orthonormal frame is in fact \emph{opposed} to the semantic compression Caveat~2 holds up
as interesting: it places every pair of classes at the same distance and can never express
``merge these two classes, keep both far from that one''. What the anchors do is choose
\emph{one specific} label-code geometry --- a neural-collapse-like configuration
\citep{papyan2020prevalence} --- instead of leaving it to the optimizer, and refuse the
chance-level corner. Any claim that FGIB compresses \emph{input} semantics at that
endpoint is the claim Caveat~2 rules out (Remark~\ref{rem:endpoint}).

\emph{(e) The sweep is over a surrogate.} Result~\ref{res:sweep} concerns the
$(\KLd_{\mathrm{anchor}}, \CE)$ plane on the aligned family, not the information plane
$(I(X;T), I(Y;T))$. On that family the posterior is class-constant, so $I(X;Z|Y) = 0$ for
every $s$; what varies along the sweep is anchor mismatch and classifier margin, not a
proved compression of mutual information (Remark~\ref{rem:surrogate}).

\section{Confound ablations: full detail}
\label{app:ablation-detail}

\paragraph{What FGIB's robustness is made of.}
FGIB differs from the comparison in two coupled ways: its classifier consumes the
deterministic $h$ at training and evaluation (P3, P4), and its prior is frozen (P1, P2).
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} separates the contributions. VIB and CEB fall to chance at
$\beta=1$ ($15.3\%$ and $26.8\%$); \emph{DCEB} --- the deterministic path
of FGIB with CEB's trainable backward encoder as its prior --- holds at $\approx 98$
through $\beta=1$ and falls to $11.4\%$ only at $\beta=10$. Corollary~\ref{cor:family}
explains \emph{why} DCEB is the odd one out: its trainable prior \emph{variance} has a
negative gradient in the mean gap, so the race of Channel~P3 re-enters through the
prior and amplifies the mean-matching force --- the only FGIB-family variant that
escapes the exact center-loss family is the only one that collapses. By contrast, every
variant that \emph{starts} from the orthonormal anchor table stays at
$\approx 98.3$ across the entire grid: \emph{TAFGIB} (trainable anchor means),
\emph{CentFGIB} (KL replaced by the plain center loss $\tfrac12\|\mu - a v_y\|^2$),
FGIB with $A$ frozen after random initialization, and \emph{FGIB-H} with $A{=}I$. The
equivalence is not a numerical coincidence: by
Result~\ref{res:varfreeze} the zero-initialized variance head receives zero gradient at
every step, so FGIB's KL \emph{is} the center loss exactly, and CentFGIB, TAFGIB and
FGIB-H are the same objective up to the trainability of the anchor means. The trainable
anchors of TAFGIB do move (final column norms shrink from $16$ to
$5.7$--$10$, pairwise correlations reach $0.78$ at the smallest
$\beta$), yet the accuracy curve is untouched --- the
protective property is the anchor-table \emph{initialization} geometry at scale $a$,
not the orthonormality of the trained table. Two conclusions follow. First,
the load-bearing ingredient on MNIST is that initialization geometry ---
orthonormal per-class directions at scale $a$ --- not the frozenness of its means, and
not any Gaussian variance machinery, which is provably inert here
(Result~\ref{res:varfreeze}). Second, freezing the means and keeping the KL
is what the \emph{theory} buys --- the persistent force of Remark~\ref{rem:force}, the
convexified sweep of Proposition~\ref{prop:sweep}, the
residual-information reading of Result~\ref{res:equiv} --- so FGIB retains an exact
variational upper-bound interpretation for the auxiliary noisy code; its predictive
robustness and endpoint behavior outside the analyzed family remain empirical.
Constraining the side head changes nothing in the
$\beta$-profile ($98.2$--$98.4$ across the grid for frozen $A$ and $A{=}I$): the
$A$-channel degrades $\kappa(A^{\top}A)$ but does not carry the robustness.

\paragraph{The variance race does not materialize.}
We ran the falsification test that Proposition~\ref{prop:variance} invites, logging the
mean posterior log-variance, the clamp fraction ($e^{-10}$), and (for CEB) the prior
log-variance at every epoch of every run, aggregated over the \emph{entire} validation
set, not a single batch. At the final epoch, averaged over the 5 seeds, CEB's posterior
log-variance sits at $+0.37, +0.27, +0.13, +0.08, -0.05, -0.01$ (nats) for
$\beta = 10^{-4}, \dots, 10$ --- a mild monotone drift with $\beta$, nowhere near the
clamp --- and the clamp fraction is $0.0$ at \emph{every} epoch of
every run; CEB's prior tracks its posterior, and VIB's posterior stays within $0.37$
of zero (Figure~\ref{fig:diag}(b)). To probe whether the race is an early-stopping
artifact, we retrained CEB at
$\beta \in \{1, 10\}$ for 400 epochs with early stopping disabled. The answer is
nuanced and we report it as measured: the variance \emph{does} drift, monotonically and
in exactly the direction Proposition~\ref{prop:variance}(i)--(ii) predicts --- at
$\beta=1$ the posterior log-variance ends at $-2.27$ nats ($-1.20$ at $\beta=10$; the
prior tracks the posterior), passing $-0.92$ at epoch 100 --- but the clamp fraction is
$0.0$ throughout, and $-2.3$ nats ($\sigma^2 \approx 0.10$) is still far from the
$e^{-10}$ clamp the two-stage account predicts as the terminal state
(Figure~\ref{fig:longtrain}). The race is therefore \emph{real but slow}: at the
protocol horizon it is invisible, at four times the horizon its direction is
unmistakable, and its convergence time-scale is beyond 400 epochs at these
hyperparameters. That is precisely the conditional status
Remark~\ref{rem:p3-status} assigns to Proposition~\ref{prop:variance}(iii): a
direction-of-force statement whose terminal state our longest run has not reached. For
FGIB the question is
settled analytically: by Result~\ref{res:varfreeze} the
posterior variance is pinned at $\tau^2$ at every step --- the flat zero line of
Figure~\ref{fig:longtrain} is the fixed point itself, held for 400 epochs --- and
Corollary~\ref{cor:family} identifies the trainable prior variance as the only re-entry
point for the race.

\paragraph{The side-head conditioning channel.}
Section~\ref{sec:limits-main}(a) identifies the \emph{trainable} side head $A$ as the
channel the optional variant leaves open, and predicts that the approach to a rank-$K$
projector would show
up as diverging $\kappa(A^{\top}A)$. Figure~\ref{fig:diag}(a) plots
$\log_{10}\kappa(A^{\top}A)$ over training, at $a=16$, for every $\beta$: from its
initialization near $10^{3.9}$ the condition number rises to $10^{5.8}$--$10^{6.8}$ at
the final epoch, with transient peaks to $10^{8.3}$ --- $A^{\top}A$ becomes
\emph{severely ill-conditioned} ($\approx 1000\times$ degradation, no monotone
dependence on $\beta$). Predictive accuracy nevertheless remains stable, so the
conclusion is narrow: the ill-conditioning does not hurt MNIST predictions in this
experiment, but it means the pulled-back reference of Result~\ref{res:equiv} is not
close to a well-conditioned frame, and the KL's correspondence with $h$ is not
guaranteed by anything we measured for the trainable variant. The canonical
configuration needs no such caveat: $A=I$ has $\kappa = 1$ at every step and anchors
$h$ directly (Result~\ref{res:orth}). The $A{=}I$ and frozen-$A$ rows of
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} show what the degradation costs in accuracy (little on
MNIST), and Table~\ref{tab:hanchor} quantifies the fixed head's price across five
settings: $\approx 0.5$ points per setting at the inherited anchor scale, largely
recovered by re-tuning the scale --- a real but bounded margin.

\paragraph{Fixed classifier scale.}
Proposition~\ref{prop:sweep} requires a fixed classifier scale $c$, which the default
\texttt{nn.Linear} does not enforce. The cosine rows of Table~\ref{tab:ablation}
enforce it by construction: FGIB's sweep is unchanged ($97.9$--$98.3$ across all six
$\beta$ values), while VIB and CEB are not rescued ($51.9\%$ at $\beta=1$ and $41.6\%$
at $\beta=0.1$ respectively), exactly as Remark~\ref{rem:c-scale} predicts --- fixed
$c$ does not repair the trainable-prior corners; the anchor geometry, not the free
classifier scale, keeps FGIB's corner non-degenerate (Appendix~\ref{app:tables},
cosine rows).

\paragraph{Does the geometry alone generalize?}
Result~\ref{res:varfreeze} proves the KL machinery is \emph{never} load-bearing in this
architecture, on any dataset. Table~\ref{tab:centfgib} asks the cross-dataset version:
CentFGIB run on all 12 settings under the
equal-budget protocol ($a=16$, 6-point $\beta$ grid, 5 seeds). The
geometry alone generalizes: at the validation-selected configuration, CentFGIB matches
FGIB($a{=}16$) to within $0.01$ points on 10 of 12 settings and to within $0.43$ on
the remaining two, including all four regression settings with RFF anchors; in a
nine-entry ranking the FGIB family occupies the top three slots. The empirical engine
of the method is the fixed anchor geometry
itself; the KL contributes the bound reading and the sweep theory
(Appendix~\ref{app:tables}).

\paragraph{Does the side head need to exist at all?}
The canonical configuration FGIB-H ($A{=}I$) removes the side head from the theory's
concerns entirely: the anchor matching acts directly on the deployed representation
(Result~\ref{res:orth}). Table~\ref{tab:hanchor} measures the price across five settings
--- MNIST/MLP and /CNN, ImageNet-100 features, California Housing, Cora. At $a=16$, the
scale inherited from the $z$-space sweep, the fixed head trails the trainable one by
$\approx 0.5$ points per setting (mean rank $4.00$ of $5$; FGIB $1.20$, CentFGIB
$1.40$) --- the trainable head is doing real adaptive work, and part of that work is
rescaling the effective geometry. Re-tuning the anchor scale for $h$-space recovers most
of the gap: at $a=4$, FGIB-H takes mean rank $2.25$ and ties or beats the backbone on
every row (MNIST/CNN $99.29$, best of the table; Cora $87.68$, tied-best), so the price
of the $\kappa=1$ closure is small and quantifiable --- an accuracy-versus-certification
choice whose two sides both ship in the released code. We also test the other fixed
geometry the class invites: a backbone with a
\emph{frozen ETF classifier} (equiangular tight frame head, no regularizer) scores
$98.32\%$ on MNIST/MLP against the backbone's $98.33\%$ --- a fixed classifier alone
buys neither margin nor a knob, so the anchor regularization on the representation
is the load-bearing ingredient (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}, ETF row).

\paragraph{AgeDB without identity leakage.}
The image-level AgeDB split of the main sweep leaks identity across the boundary. To
check that the leak inflates magnitudes rather than creates the pattern, we reran
AgeDB/CNN under a subject-disjoint split: the ordering is preserved --- the backbone
scores $21.37$ against $35.10$ for FGIB and CentFGIB and $33.71$ for FGIB-H (all
$R^2\times100$, validation-selected $\beta$) --- so the leakage does not manufacture
the regression claim (Table~\ref{tab:agedb-disjoint},
Appendix~\ref{app:tables}).

\paragraph{Anchor-scale sensitivity of the equal-budget rank.}
The $a{=}16$ equal-budget control is a post-hoc choice; Table~\ref{tab:anchor-ranks}
reports the fixed-anchor rank for every $a$ in the grid: the mean rank over the 12
settings ranges from $2.00$ ($a{=}14$, best or tied-best on $7$) to $3.17$ ($a{=}1$),
with $a{=}16$ at $2.33$ --- the edge is not an artifact of the chosen anchor, and any
$a \ge 8$ beats every bottleneck's rank. The remaining tables and diagnostic figures
of this section are collected in Appendix~\ref{app:tables}.

\section{What we have not measured: full statement}
\label{app:notyet}

\emph{(i) Separating the
$\beta$-effect from the early-stopping effect.} Remark~\ref{rem:c-scale}(b) predicts that
the $\beta$-dependence of the equilibrium decays with training length; our runs use
patience 10, so training well past saturation and comparing converged KL across $\beta$
would test it. The cosine-classifier runs of Section~\ref{sec:ablation} enforce the
fixed-$c$ assumption of Proposition~\ref{prop:sweep} but do not remove early stopping,
and the 400-epoch control of that section covers the variance trajectory, not the
sweep itself.
\emph{(ii) Equal budget for the other methods.} The $a=16$ column of
Table~\ref{tab:main-std} gives FGIB the same 6-point budget as the comparison;
Table~\ref{tab:anchor-ranks} shows the rank is stable across fixed anchor scales, not
only at $a=16$. The
converse --- giving VIB/CEB/DVCCA an extra temperature or variance hyperparameter
dimension --- is not run, so the comparison's rank ordering is only shown to survive
budget restriction for FGIB, not budget expansion for the others. \emph{(iii) The
remaining protocol asks.} All results come from one fixed $60/20/20$ split per dataset
(seed 42), so split-sensitivity is unmeasured --- the subject-disjoint AgeDB control
measures leakage sensitivity for one dataset, not split noise in general. Beyond the
theory's own asks, the robustness
properties that motivate
conditional bottlenecks in the first place --- adversarial robustness, OoD detection,
calibration, and resistance to memorizing random labels \citep{fischer2020ceb,
fischer2020cebrobust} --- are not evaluated here; the label-free anchor-distance score
$\min_y \KLd(q(z|x)\|\Ncal(a v_y, \tau^2 I))$ that FGIB's geometry makes available at
inference (Remark~\ref{rem:training-role}) is a hypothesis, not a result.

\section{Datasets and preprocessing}
\label{app:data}

All splits use seed 42. Except where noted, each dataset is divided $60/20/20$ into
train/validation/test; classification splits are stratified, regression splits are not.
Targets for regression are min-max scaled to $[0,1]$ with a scaler fitted on the training
split only, and MAE is reported in original units via the inverse transform.

\paragraph{MNIST} \citep{lecun1998mnist}: 70{,}000 $28\times28$ grayscale digits; the
60{,}000-image training partition is split into 50{,}000 train / 10{,}000 validation, the
official 10{,}000-image test set is untouched. Inputs are normalized to $[0,1]$.

\paragraph{ImageNet-100} \citep{deng2009imagenet}: frozen ResNet-50 \citep{he2016resnet}
layer-3 features. Two variants are used: global average pooling (1024-d, the MLP
backbone) and $4\times4$ spatial pooling ($1024\times4\times4$, the CNN backbone).
Features are split $60/20/20$ (stratified) and standardized with a StandardScaler fitted
on the training split.

\paragraph{Cora} \citep{sen2008collective}: the standard transductive node-classification
graph (2708 nodes, 1433-d features, 7 classes) with a fixed random split (seed 42) into
train/validation/test node masks; the adjacency matrix is symmetrized, self-loops are
added, and the normalized Laplacian-style $\tilde D^{-1/2}(\tilde A)\tilde D^{-1/2}$ is
used. Training is full-graph (transductive), following standard GCN practice
\citep{kipf2017gcn}; this is not the fixed Planetoid split, so absolute numbers are not
directly comparable with published Planetoid numbers --- all comparisons in this paper
are internal to the same split.

\paragraph{IMDb} \citep{maas2011imdb}: the official 50{,}000 labeled reviews (25k train +
25k test) are merged and re-divided $60/20/20$ (stratified) into 30k/10k/10k. HTML tags
are stripped, tokens are alphabetic words from a training-split vocabulary (approx.\
72k words at length cap 250), and sequences are truncated or padded to $250$ tokens.
Official-split literature numbers are therefore not directly comparable; again, all
comparisons are internal.

\paragraph{AG News} \citep{zhang2015character}: frozen bert-base-uncased
\citep{devlin2019bert} token features. We use the second-to-last hidden layer (layer 10)
of each token, PCA-reduced to 50 dimensions (the PCA fit on training-split tokens only),
sequences capped at 64 tokens; $60/20/20$ stratified split over 127{,}600 documents.

\paragraph{California Housing} \citep{pace1997sparse}: 20{,}640 samples, 8 features,
$60/20/20$ split (regression, unstratified); features standardized on the training split,
target min-max scaled.

\paragraph{STS-B} \citep{cer2017semeval,wang2019glue}: the official GLUE train and dev
partitions (7{,}249 labeled sentence pairs, similarity $0$--$5$) are merged and
re-divided $60/20/20$ into 4{,}349/1{,}450/1{,}450 (the GLUE test partition carries no
labels and is unused). Each sentence is stripped of HTML, tokenized alphabetically, and
truncated to $124$ tokens; the pair is joined by a [SEP] token into a single sequence of
length $\le 250$. The vocabulary is built from the training split (approx.\ 10k words).
Embeddings are GloVe-6B-100d \citep{pennington2014glove}, initialized and frozen;
the LSTM has a single 256-d hidden layer and max-pooling readout.

\paragraph{ZINC-12k} \citep{dwivedi2023benchmarking}: the official
benchmarking-gnns split (10{,}000 train / 1{,}000 validation / 1{,}000 test). Node
features are 28-d one-hot atom types (bond types are unused, matching the benchmarking
GCN baseline); the adjacency is symmetrized, self-loops added, and degree-normalized as
for Cora; batching uses block-diagonal adjacency (batch size 128). The target is the
penalized logP, min-max scaled on the training split; MAE is reported in original units
(our GCN baseline reproduces the literature value to within the expected margin).

\paragraph{AgeDB} \citep{moschoglou2017agedb}: the official full release, 16{,}488
images of 567 subjects aged 0--96, resized to RGB $64\times64$ and normalized with
ImageNet statistics; target age min-max scaled on the training split. \textbf{Split
caveat}: the default split is image-level $60/20/20$, under which $562$ of $567$ subjects
have images on both sides of the train/test boundary --- identity-specific age cues can
leak, and the AgeDB numbers should be read with that in mind. A subject-disjoint split
(grouping all images of a subject into one partition, then splitting subjects
$60/20/20$ with seed 42) is implemented in the released code and \emph{is run} as a
control in this paper: under it the objective ordering is preserved
(Table~\ref{tab:agedb-disjoint}), so the leak inflates AgeDB's magnitudes but does not
create the pattern.
